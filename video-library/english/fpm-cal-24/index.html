Volumes of revolution about the y-axis

Idea. Picture a potter shaping clay on a spinning wheel. A flat shape, spun round a line, makes a solid. That solid is called a solid of revolution. This time the line is the y-axis. Slice the solid into thin discs, stacked upwards. Each disc has radius x and thickness d y. One disc has volume pi x squared times d y. Add them all up and the volume is pi times the integral of x squared, d y. The curve is usually given as y in terms of x. So make x squared the subject first. Then everything is in y, and the limits are y values. This question asks for an exact volume, in terms of pi.

Method. Step one: find the y limits, the lowest and highest y of the region. Step two: rearrange each graph to give x squared in terms of y. Step three: integrate pi times outer x squared, minus inner x squared, with respect to y. Step four: substitute the y limits and simplify. The trap is using x limits, or leaving y squared inside. Everything must be in y.

Worked example. June twenty twenty three. The curve S is y equals x squared over four plus two, and the line l is two y minus x minus four equals nought, for x at least nought. The region between them is rotated about the y-axis. Find the volume in terms of pi. Four marks. Here is the region. It runs from A, at nought, two, up to B, at two, three. Step one: the y limits. A has y equals two, B has y equals three. So the integral runs from two to three. Step two: x squared in terms of y. For the curve, x squared equals four times y minus two. For the line, x equals two y minus four, so x squared is that, squared. Which is outer? Test y equals two and a half. The curve gives x squared equals two. The line gives one. So the curve is outer, and the line is the hole. Do not integrate with x limits. Subtract the line's solid from the curve's. Step four: integrate. At three, the value is minus eighteen. At two, it is minus fifty six over three. Subtract: minus eighteen plus fifty six over three. So V is two pi over three. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is using x limits with d y. Here the x limits are nought and two. That gives a different, wrong volume.

Your turn. Now you try, from January twenty twenty three. A region is bounded by the curve y equals x squared plus two, the y-axis and the line y equals a. It is rotated about the y-axis to make a solid of volume eighteen pi. Find a. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The limits are y equals two, up to y equals a. The curve gives x squared equals y minus two. So V is pi times the integral from two to a of y minus two, d y. Integrate: y squared over two, minus two y. At a, that is a squared over two, minus two a. At two, it is minus two. So V is pi times a squared over two, minus two a, plus two. Set this equal to eighteen pi. So a minus two is six or minus six. The question says a is bigger than two, so reject minus four. A equals eight. The common wrong answer is to give both, or to forget to reject. Remember: find the y limits, write x squared using y, integrate pi times x squared with respect to y, then simplify.
