Idea. On a bicycle, the pedal turns the gear and the gear turns the wheel. Two gear turns per pedal turn, three wheel turns per gear turn: six wheel turns per pedal turn. Rates multiply. A derivative, d y by d x, is the rate y changes as x changes. A function of a function has one inside another, like e to the two x. Chain rule: differentiate the outside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside. A product is two functions multiplied. Picture a rectangle with sides u and v. Its area is u times v. Both sides can change, so both change the area. The product rule: first times the derivative of the second, plus second times the derivative of the first. Both terms must appear. These questions say differentiate, find d y by d x, or show that. Spot the rule, use it, then take out a common factor.

Method. Step one: decide if it is a product, a function of a function, or both. The wrong rule loses every mark. Step two: list u, v and both derivatives. This stops you dropping the inside factor, the classic trap. Step three: put the pieces into the product rule, with both terms. Step four: take out the common factor. The accuracy mark is for the simplified form.

Worked example. A real question, June twenty nineteen. Given y equals four x minus three, times e to the two x, find d y by d x. Three marks. A bracket times e to the two x is a product, and the exponential has an inside, so the chain rule is needed too. Let u be four x minus three and v be e to the two x. Differentiate each separately. The derivative of u is four. For v, the chain rule gives two e to the two x. The extra two is from the inside. Product rule. u times d v by d x is four x minus three, times two e to the two x. Plus v times d u by d x, which is four e to the two x. Take out e to the two x. In the bracket: two times four x minus three, plus four. That is eight x minus two. A tempting wrong route is to differentiate each factor and multiply, giving eight e to the two x. That ignores the product rule. At x equals nought the true value is minus two. Exam answer: d y by d x equals eight x minus two, times e to the two x. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is dropping the factor from the inside. For e to the three x squared, the inside has derivative six x, so you must multiply by six x.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty. Differentiate six e to the three x squared, times cosine two x. Both factors need the chain rule. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Let u be six e to the three x squared. Its derivative is thirty six x e to the three x squared, since the inside gives six x. Let v be cosine two x, with derivative minus two sine two x. Product rule, then take out twelve e to the three x squared. The answer is twelve e to the three x squared, times three x cosine two x minus sine two x. A common wrong answer has three e to the three x squared, losing the six x. It treats the inside as three x, so accuracy marks go. Remember: spot the rule, list the pieces, use u times d v by d x plus v times d u by d x, then take out the common factor.
