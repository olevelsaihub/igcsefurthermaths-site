Idea. Think of a ramp. Its steepness is how far it rises for each metre across. In trigonometry, tangent is a steepness like that. Tan of an angle equals sine of the angle divided by cosine of the angle. On the board, theta, a Greek letter, stands for the angle. The tangent graph repeats every one hundred and eighty degrees, not every three hundred and sixty. So tan equals a number has a new solution every half turn. Suppose an equation has sine and cosine of the same angle. Divide both sides by cosine, and sine over cosine becomes tangent. So sine u equals three cosine u turns into tan u equals three. This question type asks you to solve such an equation for angles in a stated interval. You turn it into tan, find every solution, and change back.

Method. Step one: if there are compound angles, expand them with the addition formulae. Step two: collect the sine terms on one side and the cosine terms on the other. Step three: divide to get tan u equals a number. Step four: find the principal value, add one hundred and eighty degrees for each new solution, then change back. The trap is turning the fraction upside down. The cosine number goes on top. Check with one value at the end.
