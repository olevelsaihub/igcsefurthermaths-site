Idea. Think of a ramp. Its steepness is how far it rises for each metre across. In trigonometry, tangent is a steepness like that. Tan of an angle equals sine of the angle divided by cosine of the angle. On the board, theta, a Greek letter, stands for the angle. The tangent graph repeats every one hundred and eighty degrees, not every three hundred and sixty. So tan equals a number has a new solution every half turn. Suppose an equation has sine and cosine of the same angle. Divide both sides by cosine, and sine over cosine becomes tangent. So sine u equals three cosine u turns into tan u equals three. This question type asks you to solve such an equation for angles in a stated interval. You turn it into tan, find every solution, and change back.

Method. Step one: if there are compound angles, expand them with the addition formulae. Step two: collect the sine terms on one side and the cosine terms on the other. Step three: divide to get tan u equals a number. Step four: find the principal value, add one hundred and eighty degrees for each new solution, then change back. The trap is turning the fraction upside down. The cosine number goes on top. Check with one value at the end.

Worked example. January twenty twenty two. Solve sine of, two theta minus twenty degrees, minus root three cosine of, two theta minus twenty degrees, equals zero, for theta from zero to one hundred and eighty. Five marks. Move the cosine term across: sine u equals root three cosine u. Divide by cosine u. Then tan u equals root three. Now the interval. Theta runs from zero to one hundred and eighty. So u runs from minus twenty to three hundred and forty. Tan inverse of root three is sixty degrees. Add one hundred and eighty for the next solution: two hundred and forty. Another one eighty gives four twenty, too big. Subtracting gives minus one hundred and twenty, too small. Change back to theta. Add twenty, then divide by two. Sixty gives theta equals forty. Two hundred and forty gives theta equals one hundred and thirty. A tempting wrong route is to write tan u equals one over root three. That is the ratio upside down. It gives theta equals twenty five, which fails: sine thirty is a half, but root three cosine thirty is one and a half. So theta equals forty, or one hundred and thirty. Common mistake. A common mistake is to stop after the principal value. Tangent repeats every one hundred and eighty degrees, so there is a second solution. Theta equals forty is only half the answer.

Answer. Move the cosine term across: three sine theta equals minus five cosine theta. Divide by three cosine theta. Then tan theta equals minus five over three. Tan inverse of minus five thirds is minus fifty nine point nought four degrees. Adding and subtracting one eighty gives every angle in range. To the nearest degree: minus two thirty nine, minus fifty nine, one twenty one, and three oh one. The most common wrong answer turns the fraction upside down, giving tan theta equals minus three over five. That gives minus thirty one, a different set of angles, so no marks. Remember: collect sine and cosine, divide to get tan, add one eighty each time, and keep the angles in the interval.
