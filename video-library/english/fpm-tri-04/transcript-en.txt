Idea. A detective needs two clues for two unknowns. A sector may have two unknowns, the radius r and the angle theta. Each fact about it gives one equation. Recall the three formulae, with theta in radians. Arc length is r theta. Area is a half r squared theta. Perimeter is two r plus r theta, two radii plus the arc. The trick is to remove theta. From the perimeter, r theta is P minus two r. Put that into the area, which is a half r times r theta. This leaves an equation in r only, usually a quadratic. It has two roots, and one may have to go. Perhaps r must be an integer, or theta must be less than two pi. So you get two facts about one sector, and find the radius and the angle.

Method. Step one: write each fact as an equation in r and theta. Step two: make r theta the subject of the perimeter, and put it into the area. Step three: solve for r, and reject any root that breaks a condition. Step four: find theta, and check both original facts. The trap: mixing up perimeter and arc. A perimeter includes the two radii.

Worked example. November twenty twenty one. Sector A O B has radius r, an integer, and angle theta radians. Its area is sixteen point eight square centimetres, and its perimeter is sixteen point four centimetres. Find r and theta. Eight marks. Here is Figure one redrawn. The sector has radius r, an angle of theta radians at O, and two facts: its area and its perimeter. Step one: area gives a half r squared theta equals sixteen point eight. Perimeter gives two r plus r theta equals sixteen point four. A tempting wrong route treats sixteen point four as the arc length. Then r is about two point oh five, not an integer, so the route fails. Step two: from equation two, r theta is sixteen point four minus two r. Put that into the area, and tidy. We get r squared minus eight point two r, plus sixteen point eight, equals nought. Step three: the formula gives r equals eight point two, plus or minus nought point two, over two. So r is four, or four point two. The integer is four. Step four: r theta is sixteen point four minus eight, which is eight point four. So theta is two point one. Check: a half times sixteen times two point one is sixteen point eight. So r is four centimetres, and theta is two point one radians. Common mistake. A common mistake is keeping both roots. Four point two also solves the quadratic, but r must be an integer. Say which root you reject, and why.
