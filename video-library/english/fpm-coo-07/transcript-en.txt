Idea. Two paths cross in a park. The crossing spot is on both paths. Two lines on a graph meet at a point whose coordinates fit both equations. That point is the intersection. Two unknowns need two equations. Substitution means writing y using x from one equation, and replacing it in the other. That leaves one equation with one unknown. Elimination is the other method. Add or subtract the equations so one letter cancels. Then put the answer back to find the other letter. These questions say the lines meet at a point, find it, or show that it has given coordinates. Sometimes you must find the equations first.

Method. Step one: write both equations, finding them first if needed. Step two: eliminate one letter, by substitution or by adding and subtracting. Step three: solve for that letter, then substitute back for the other. Step four: check in the second equation, and give a pair. The trap is a slip when multiplying a bracket.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. A is minus five, three. B is four, nought. C is minus one, five. The line l goes through C, perpendicular to A B, and meets A B at D. Show that D is minus two, two. Three marks. First the line A B. Its gradient is minus three over nine, which is minus one third. Through B, that gives x plus three y minus four equals zero. Next, the line l. It is perpendicular to A B, so its gradient is three. Through C, minus one, five, it is y equals three x plus eight. Step three: substitute y equals three x plus eight into the equation of A B. That gives x plus three, times, three x plus eight, minus four, equals zero. Multiply out: x plus nine x plus twenty four, minus four. That is ten x plus twenty equals zero. So x is minus two. Back in: y is three times minus two, plus eight, which is two. D is minus two, two. Check in A B: minus two, plus six, minus four, is zero. A tempting slip is three times eight written as eight, which gives the wrong x. So D is minus two, two, as required. Common mistake. A common mistake is stopping at x. A point needs both coordinates. Substitute back to find y, and write the pair.

Your turn. Now you try, from January twenty twenty. A is three, nought. L one is x minus two y plus two equals zero. It meets L two, x minus seven y minus three equals zero, at C. M is the midpoint of A C. Find M. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Both lines have x with coefficient one, so subtract. L one minus L two gives five y plus five equals zero, so y is minus one. Put y equals minus one into L one: x is minus four. C is minus four, minus one. M is the midpoint of A and C: minus one half, minus one half. The most common wrong answer is a sign slip when subtracting. Minus two y minus minus seven y is written as minus nine y. Watch the double negative. Remember: two equations, eliminate a letter, solve, substitute back, check, and give a pair.
