Idea. A curve and a straight line meet where they cross. There they have the same x and the same y. So that x solves the equation curve equals line. You have drawn y equals x squared. To solve x squared minus x minus two equals nought, move terms until one side is exactly x squared. Then x squared equals x plus two. The other side is the line. A straight line needs two points, and a third is a safe check. Draw it with a ruler across the whole interval. Read the x values where it meets the curve. These are the roots, the solutions of the equation. This question says: by drawing a suitable straight line, estimate the roots. You rearrange, draw the line, and read the x values.

Method. Step one: write down the curve you plotted, y equals f of x. Step two: rearrange the new equation until f of x stands alone on one side. Step three: replace f of x by y. The other side is the line. Find two or three points and draw it across the whole interval. Steps four and five: read the x value of each crossing, to one decimal place. The trap is reading y values. The roots are x values.

Worked example. November twenty twenty one. You have drawn y equals two x, plus three over x squared, minus three. Use a straight line to estimate the roots of four x cubed, minus ten x squared, plus three, equals nought. Five marks. Step two: divide by x squared. Four x cubed gives four x. Ten x squared gives ten. The three becomes three over x squared. Still step two: add two x minus three to both sides. The left becomes the plotted curve. The right becomes seven minus two x. That is the line. Step three: draw the line. At x equals nought point five, y is six. At two, y is three. At three point five, y is nought. Step four: the line meets the curve twice, near x equals nought point six and two point four. Those are the roots. A tempting wrong route is reading the y values, about five point seven and two point three. The roots are x values. So the roots are about nought point six and two point four. Common mistake. A common mistake is dividing by x squared but leaving the three. The three becomes three over x squared, not plus three.
