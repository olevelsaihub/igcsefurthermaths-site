Idea. A series starts at twenty four and goes up by six. When is the total of the first two n terms exactly three times the total of the first n terms? S n means the sum of the first n terms. Test some values of n. Only n equal to seven works. Algebra finds it, not trial. For S two n, replace every n in the sum formula by two n. Use brackets. Put every sum into the relation. That gives an equation in n, often a quadratic. Keep only the positive whole number, because n counts terms. These questions say find n. Use algebra means form and solve the equation. Trying numbers is not enough.

Method. Step one: find a and d, and write S n in terms of n. Step two: write other sums, like S two n, by replacing n carefully. Step three: substitute into the relation and expand. Step four: collect terms. If n is a common factor, divide by it. Step five: solve, keep the positive whole number, and check. The trap is writing S two n as two times S n.

Worked example. January twenty twenty, paper one. A different arithmetic series has first term twenty four and common difference six. The sum of the first two n terms is three times the sum of the first n terms. Find n. Five marks. Step one: S n. N over two, times forty eight plus six n minus six. That simplifies to n times, twenty one plus three n. Step two: S two n. Replace every n by two n. Two n times, twenty one plus six n. Step three: S two n equals three times S n. Divide both sides by n, which is not zero. We get forty two plus twelve n equals sixty three plus nine n. Step four: collect. Three n equals twenty one. So n is seven. Reject a tempting route. Writing S two n as two times S n gives S n equals zero. No positive n works. Doubling the terms does not double the sum. Check. S seven is two hundred and ninety four. S fourteen is eight hundred and eighty two, three times as much. So n is seven. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is replacing only some n's. Keep the n over two, and the answer comes out as minus fourteen. Replace every n.
