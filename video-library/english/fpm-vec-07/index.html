Where lines cross: two routes and comparing coefficients

Idea. Two friends walk to the same cafe by different roads. In vectors, a crossing point can be reached by two routes. We write its position both ways, then make them equal. A scalar is an ordinary number, like two, or lambda, a Greek letter for an unknown number. A X equals lambda times A N says X is a fraction lambda of the way from A to N. A coefficient is the number in front of a or b. If a and b are not parallel, two expressions are equal only when the a parts match and the b parts match. This type gives a crossing point and asks for a ratio. Find it by two routes, compare coefficients, solve, then give the ratio.

Method. Step one: give each line its own scalar, lambda and mu, two Greek letters. Step two: write the position vector of the point by two routes. Step three: expand each route into a number of a plus a number of b. Step four: set them equal, and compare the a parts and the b parts. Step five: solve the two equations. Step six: turn the scalar into a ratio. The trap is using the same letter for both scalars.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. Lines A N and B M cross at X in triangle O A B. A N is three quarters b minus a. B M is half a minus b. Find A X to X N. Seven marks. Let A X be lambda A N, and B X be mu B M. Route one: O X is O A plus lambda A N. Route two: O X is O B plus mu B M. Collect terms. Route one: one minus lambda a, plus three lambda over four b. Route two: mu over two a, plus one minus mu b. Both are vector O X, so compare parts. The a parts: one minus lambda equals mu over two. The b parts: three lambda over four equals one minus mu. Put mu equals two minus two lambda into the b equation. Three lambda over four equals two lambda minus one. So lambda is four fifths. So A X is four fifths of A N, leaving one fifth for X N. A X to X N is four to one. A tempting wrong route uses one letter for both scalars. The a parts give lambda two thirds, the b parts give four sevenths. They clash. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is the wrong ratio. A X is four fifths of A N, so the ratio is four fifths to one fifth, which is four to one. Not four to five.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty three. Use the figure. M, Y and X are collinear, with X on O B extended. M Y is three b minus half a. Find O B to O X. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. X is on O B extended, so O X is k b. O M is half of two a, which is a. So M X is k b minus a. M, Y, X are in a line, so M X is lambda times M Y. Compare the a parts: minus one equals minus lambda over two, so lambda is two. The b parts: k is three lambda, so six. O X is six b. O B is four b, O X is six b. So O B to O X is four to six, two to three. The common wrong answer, three to two, is the wrong way round. Remember: a scalar for each line, two routes, collect, compare coefficients, solve, then give the ratio.
