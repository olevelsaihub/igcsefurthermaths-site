Idea. Railway tracks never meet because they have the same steepness. Lines with the same gradient are parallel. A door meets its frame at a right angle. Lines at a right angle are perpendicular. For perpendicular lines, flip the gradient upside down and change its sign. That is the negative reciprocal. Two becomes minus one half. Multiply them to get minus one. Gradients come from two points, change in y over change in x, or from rearranging the equation to y equals m x plus c. These questions say show that two lines are perpendicular or parallel, or find an unknown so that they are. For an unknown, write the condition as an equation.

Method. Step one: find each gradient. Step two: for a proof, compare them if parallel, or multiply them if perpendicular. For an unknown, write the condition as an equation and solve. Step three: solve any unknown. Step four: reject any root that breaks a condition, and write a conclusion. The trap is the sign: the perpendicular to two is minus one half, not one half.

Worked example. November twenty twenty. P is four, seven. Q is three, nought. R is ten, one. S is eleven, eight. Show, by calculation, that the lines P R and Q S are perpendicular. Three marks. Step one: the gradient of P R. Y goes from seven to one, a change of minus six. X goes from four to ten, a change of six. So the gradient is minus six over six, which is minus one. Step two: the gradient of Q S. Y goes from nought to eight, a change of eight. X goes from three to eleven, a change of eight. So the gradient is eight over eight, which is one. Step three: multiply the gradients. Minus one times one is minus one. That is the test for perpendicular lines. Step four: the conclusion. Since the product of the gradients is minus one, P R and Q S are perpendicular. Without this sentence, the last mark is lost. A tempting wrong route is to say the gradients are equal in size, so the lines are parallel. That mixes up the tests. Parallel needs equal gradients. Perpendicular needs a product of minus one. So the lines P R and Q S are perpendicular. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is the sign. The perpendicular to a gradient of two is not one half. It is minus one half. Then the product is minus one.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty one. A is minus three, minus five. B is seven, five. C is p, one, where p is negative. A C and B C are perpendicular. Prove that p is minus five. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The gradient of A C is six over p plus three. The gradient of B C is minus four over p minus seven. Multiply them and set the product equal to minus one. So the brackets multiply to twenty four. Expand and tidy: p squared, minus four p, minus forty five, equals zero. Factorise: p minus nine, times, p plus five. So p is nine or minus five. The question says p is negative, so reject nine. The most common wrong answer keeps both roots, and loses the final mark.
