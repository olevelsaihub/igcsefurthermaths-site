Idea. As r changes, the metal in the can changes. Too thin and tall, it is big. Too wide and flat, it is big again. In between is a lowest point, where the gradient is zero. To differentiate one hundred and eighty pi over r, first rewrite it as one hundred and eighty pi times r to the minus one. The derivative of r to the minus one is minus r to the minus two. Justify means give the evidence. A zero gradient is not enough. The second derivative must be positive for a minimum, or negative for a maximum, and you must say so. So these questions give a formula in one letter. Find the value that makes it a maximum or minimum, justify it, and sometimes find the optimum value.

Method. Step one: rewrite with negative powers and differentiate. Step two: set it to zero and solve. Keep only sensible answers: a length is not negative. Step three: find the second derivative, put in the value, and state its sign. The trap is no justification: saying minimum alone loses marks. Step four: for the optimum value, put the unrounded r into the original formula. Rounding early can change the answer.

Worked example. January twenty twenty two. The cylinder's surface area is two pi r squared, plus one hundred and eighty pi over r. Given that r can vary, find r for which S is a minimum, and justify it. Five marks. Step one: differentiate. The derivative is four pi r, minus one hundred and eighty pi r to the minus two. A tempting wrong route is to differentiate one hundred and eighty pi over r as one hundred and eighty pi. That ignores the r. The correct derivative is minus one hundred and eighty pi over r squared. Step two: set to zero. Four pi r equals one hundred and eighty pi over r squared. Multiply by r squared, divide by four pi: r cubed is forty five. The cube root is three point five five six nine, which is three point five six to three significant figures. Keep the full value. Justify. The second derivative is four pi, plus three hundred and sixty pi over r cubed. With r cubed forty five, that is twelve pi. Positive, so a minimum. Exam answer: r is three point five six, and the second derivative twelve pi is positive, so S is a minimum. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is rounding r early. Using three point five six in later working can change the third significant figure. Keep the full value.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty five. V is pi r times three hundred and fifty minus r squared. Given that r can vary, find r for which V is a maximum, and justify. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Expand: V is three hundred and fifty pi r, minus pi r cubed. The derivative is three hundred and fifty pi, minus three pi r squared. Set to zero: r squared is three hundred and fifty over three. So r is ten point eight. The second derivative is minus six pi r, about minus two hundred and four. Negative, so V is a maximum. The most common wrong answer states maximum with no second derivative. A bare statement loses the justification mark. Remember: differentiate, set to zero, solve, then the second derivative with its sign, and keep r unrounded.
