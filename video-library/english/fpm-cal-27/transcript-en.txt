Idea. A bus on a ring road never stops. Its speed is always above zero. A particle never comes to rest when its velocity is never zero, not at any time. To check, set v to zero and try to solve. A quadratic has real solutions only if b squared, minus four a c, called the discriminant, is zero or more. If it is negative, there are no real solutions. The least value of a velocity is the lowest point of the graph. Completing the square shows it. A square is never negative, so the smallest it can be is zero. Magnitude means size, ignoring direction. So least magnitude of velocity means the smallest speed. These questions ask you to show there is no rest, and to find that least speed.

Method. Step one: write v as a quadratic in t. For never at rest, work out b squared minus four a c. Step two: if it is negative, say there are no real roots, so v is never zero. Step three: for the least speed, complete the square. The number at the end is the least value. Step four: state the conclusion in words. The trap is the wrong inequality. A negative discriminant means no roots. A positive one means two roots, so rest does happen.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. The velocity of P is t squared, minus ten t, plus twenty eight. Part one: show that P is never at rest. Part two: find the least magnitude of the velocity. Three marks. Part one. At rest, v is zero: t squared, minus ten t, plus twenty eight, equals zero. Here a is one, b is minus ten and c is twenty eight. The discriminant is one hundred, minus one hundred and twelve. The discriminant is negative, so there are no real roots. So v is never zero, and P is never instantaneously at rest. That sentence is the conclusion the mark needs. Part two. Complete the square. Half of minus ten is minus five. So t minus five, all squared, then take off twenty five and add twenty eight. That is t minus five squared, plus three. A square is never negative, so v is at least three. It equals three when t is five. So the least magnitude of the velocity is three metres per second. A tempting wrong answer is t equals five. That is the time when the least speed happens. The question asks for the speed itself, which is three. So P is never at rest, and the least magnitude of velocity is three metres per second. Common mistake. A common mistake is to stop at the number. Some students write minus twelve and nothing else. The examiner needs the reasoning: negative, so no real roots, so never at rest.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty five. The velocity is four t squared, minus six t, plus five. Find the minimum speed of P. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Take out the four from the t terms: four, times, t squared minus three over two t, plus five. Complete the square inside: t minus three quarters, all squared, minus nine sixteenths. Multiply the four through: four times the bracket, minus nine quarters, plus five. That is four times t minus three quarters squared, plus eleven quarters. So the least value is eleven quarters. The most common wrong answer forgets that the four multiplies the nine sixteenths. That gives seventy one sixteenths. Multiply every term inside the bracket by four. Remember: write v as a quadratic, discriminant below zero for no rest, complete the square for the least value, and write the conclusion.
