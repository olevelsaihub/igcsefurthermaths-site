When is a binomial expansion valid?

Idea. A lift has a weight limit. Inside it, safe. Outside, not. An expansion with a negative or fractional power also has a limit, on x. It is valid when it gives the true value of the bracket. Take one plus x plus x squared and so on. At x equal to a half, the sums are one point five, one point seven five, one point eight seven five. They settle near two. At x equal to two, the sums are three, seven, fifteen. They never settle. The rule: one plus X, to a power that is not a positive whole number, is valid when modulus X is less than one. The modulus is the size, ignoring the sign. It means X is between minus one and one. In one plus b x, X is b x. So modulus b x is less than one. Divide by modulus b: modulus x is less than one over modulus b. So the question says: write down, state, or find the range of x for which your expansion is valid.

Method. Step one: look at the bracket, one plus b x. Step two: write modulus b x less than one. Step three: divide by modulus b. State modulus x less than c, or minus c less than x less than c. The trap: a bracket that does not start with one. Three plus x must first become three, times one plus x over three.

Worked example. June twenty twenty three. From part a, the expansion is of one plus x over three, to the power minus three. Write down the range of values of x for which the expansion is valid. One mark. Step one: the bracket. It is one plus x over three, so X is x over three. The bracket starts with one, so no constant needs taking out. Step two: modulus x over three is less than one. Multiply both sides by three. Modulus x is less than three. That means x is between minus three and three. A tempting wrong route gives modulus x less than one third. Wrong. One over one third is three. Check: at x equal to two, X is two thirds, less than one, so two is allowed. Test a value outside. At x equal to four, the terms are one, minus four, ten point six seven, minus twenty three point seven. They grow, so four is outside. So the expansion is valid for modulus x less than three. Common mistake. A common mistake is writing x less than three. That allows x equal to minus ten. Valid x go from minus three to three.
