Idea. A straight path meets a curved river. It may cross the river again further on. Here a line meets a curve at a point you know. You must find the second place they meet. The curve is a fraction with x in the denominator. Where line and curve meet, the y values are equal. So write the line equal to the curve. To clear the fraction, multiply every term by the denominator, here x minus one. Every term means both sides. You get a quadratic. The known point is one root. The other root is D. The question says find the x coordinate of D.

Method. Step one: write the line equal to the curve. Step two: multiply every term by the denominator. Step three: expand and rearrange to zero. Step four: factorise. The known x value is a root. Step five: reject the known point, and give the other root. The trap is giving the known point.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. The curve C is y equals q x minus two, over x minus p. It crosses the y axis at A. The line l, y equals x plus two, is the normal at A. A normal is a line at right angles to the curve. From part a, p is one and q is three. The line meets C again at D. Find the x coordinate of D. Four marks. Here is the picture. An asymptote is a line the curve approaches but never reaches. The line passes through A and meets the curve again at D. Step one: line equal to curve, then multiply every term by x minus one. x plus two, times x minus one, equals three x minus two. Step two: expand and rearrange. x squared minus two x equals zero. Factorise: x times x minus two equals zero. So x is nought or two. Nought is the known point A. So D has x equal to two. A tempting wrong route is x equals nought. But that is A, where we started. So the x coordinate of D is two. Check: at x equals two, the curve gives four over one, which is four, and the line gives two plus two, also four. Common mistake. A common mistake is to leave the left side alone. That gives x plus two equals three x minus two. It loses the quadratic, and the marks.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty two. The curve is five x plus one, over three x minus six. The normal at A meets the curve again at D. Give the x coordinate of D as an improper fraction, a fraction with the top bigger than the bottom. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Make y the subject of the line: seventy two x minus eleven, over sixty six. Equate it to the curve. Cross multiply. Sixty six times five x plus one, equals seventy two x minus eleven, times three x minus six. Expand both sides. Rearrange: two hundred and sixteen x squared minus seven hundred and ninety five x equals zero. Factorise. x equals nought is A, so reject it. x is seven hundred and ninety five over two hundred and sixteen, which is two hundred and sixty five over seventy two. The common wrong answer is x equals nought, the known point A. Another gives a decimal, three point six eight. That is not an improper fraction, so the final mark is lost. Remember: line equals curve, multiply every term, rearrange to zero, factorise, reject the known point.
