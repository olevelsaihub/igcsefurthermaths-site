Idea. Picture a triangular playing field. You know two sides and the angle between them. How long is the third side? Pythagoras needs a right angle, so we use the cosine rule. The cosine rule says: a squared equals b squared plus c squared minus two b c cosine A. Side a is opposite angle A, the side the angle faces. The angle sits between b and c. Cosine of sixty degrees is one half. Cosine of one hundred and twenty degrees is minus one half. A negative cosine means an obtuse angle, over ninety degrees. Often the sides use x. Then the rule gives a quadratic, an equation with x squared in it. It can have two answers, but a length is never negative, so we may reject one. So you are asked to use the cosine rule, often to build and solve a quadratic.

Method. Step one: mark the known angle and the side opposite it. That side goes on the left. Step two: write the rule, with each value in brackets. Step three: expand every bracket and collect to zero. Step four: solve, and reject any value that makes a side negative. The trap is multiplying the whole bracket by cosine. The cosine belongs only to the two b c term.

Worked example. June twenty nineteen. In triangle A B C, A B is five x, B C is three x minus one, and A C is two x plus five, all in centimetres. Angle A B C is sixty degrees. Find x, to three significant figures. Five marks. Angle B is between A B and B C. So the side opposite is A C. It goes on the left of the rule. Step two: write the rule. Cosine of sixty degrees is a half, so the two and the half cancel. Step three: expand every bracket. The left is four x squared plus twenty x plus twenty five. The right collects to nineteen x squared minus x plus one. Collect to one side, then divide by three. This gives five x squared minus seven x minus eight, equals zero. Use the formula: seven plus or minus root two hundred and nine, over ten. So x is two point one four five seven, or minus nought point seven four five seven. A negative x makes B C negative, so reject it. A tempting route multiplies the whole bracket by a half. That gives only negative answers, so it cannot be right. So x is two point one five, to three significant figures. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is squaring each term of a bracket. Three x minus one, all squared, is nine x squared minus six x plus one. Missing the middle term makes the quadratic wrong.
