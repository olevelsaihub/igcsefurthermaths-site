Idea. A logo is made of curved and straight parts. To find its area, cut it into pieces you know: sectors, which are slices of a circle, and triangles. Then add or subtract. Theta is in radians. Sector area is a half r squared theta. Arc length is r theta. A triangle with sides a and b, and angle C between them, has area a half a b sine C. A segment is the region between a chord and its arc. A chord joins two points on a circle. The segment is the sector minus the triangle: a half r squared, times theta minus sine theta. Diagrams often hide equilateral triangles. Their three angles are each pi over three. Sine of pi over three, and of two pi over three, are both root three over two. So you find the area or perimeter of a region with arcs and straight lines, often exactly.

Method. Step one: split the region into sectors and triangles. Step two: find each angle in radians, using geometry such as equilateral triangles. Step three: work out each piece. Step four: add or subtract, counting repeated pieces the right number of times. Keep exact values if asked. The trap: counting a piece once when it appears twice.

Worked example. June twenty twenty two. Two circles each have radius six centimetres. Each centre lies on the other circle. They meet at A and B and enclose the region R. Find its exact area P, in the form a pi minus b root c. Seven marks. Here is the figure. O one O two is six, and so are O one A and O two A. So the triangle is equilateral, and angle A O one O two is pi over three. By symmetry, angle A O one B is two pi over three. The region is two equal segments, cut off by the chord A B. Sector O one A B is a half, times thirty six, times two pi over three. That is twelve pi. Triangle O one A B is a half, times six, times six, times sine two pi over three. That is nine root three. A tempting wrong route stops at one segment, twelve pi minus nine root three. That is only half of the region. The region has two segments. So P is twice the segment: twenty four pi minus eighteen root three. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.
