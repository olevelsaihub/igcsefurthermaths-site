Idea. Think of a lake. The water line is the top edge and the curved bed is the bottom edge. The depth at any point is top minus bottom. Adding the depths gives the area. The same works for two graphs. The area between them is the integral of top minus bottom. Here the line y equals x is above the curve y equals x squared, from nought to one. The limits are where the graphs meet. There the two y values are equal, so set the two equations equal and solve. Which graph is on top? Test one x value between the limits. At x equals a half, the line gives a half and the curve a quarter. So the line is on top. These questions ask for the area of the finite region bounded by a curve and a line, or two curves. Finite means enclosed all the way round.

Method. Step one: solve the two equations together, to get the limits. Step two: test a value between the limits to see which graph is on top. Step three: simplify top minus bottom, then integrate. Step four: substitute the limits, keeping the answer exact if asked. The trap is subtracting the wrong way round. Bottom minus top gives a negative area.

Worked example. November twenty twenty three. The curve S is y equals x squared over two, minus two x, minus six. The line l is two y plus x plus eight equals nought. Find the exact area bounded by S and l. Seven marks. Here is the picture. The line l cuts the parabola S at P, and at one more point. The shaded region lies between them. Step one: the limits. Set l equal to S. That gives x squared minus three x minus four equals nought, so x is minus one or four. Step two: test x equals nought. The line gives minus four, S gives minus six, so l is on top. Integrate l minus S. The minus applies to every term of S. Step three: integrate and put in the limits. At four we get twenty eight thirds. At minus one we get minus thirteen twelfths. The difference is one hundred and twenty five twelfths. So the exact area is one hundred and twenty five over twelve. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is dropping a minus sign. When you subtract S, the minus applies to every term inside the bracket. So minus minus two x becomes plus two x.
