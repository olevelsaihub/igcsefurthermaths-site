Idea. Imagine saving the same pocket money each week. A list that goes up by a fixed step is an arithmetic series. The fixed step is the common difference. Here the terms are logarithms. L N four is the power of e that gives four. Two laws help. L N of a b is L N a plus L N b. L N of a to the k is k L N a. The big idea: treat L N two like an ordinary number. Then L N a plus n minus one times L N b has first term L N a and common difference L N b. Be careful. L N of a plus b is not L N a plus L N b. The law needs a product. So you get a series made of logs. Find its first term and difference, use a series formula, then use log laws to reach the form asked.

Method. Step one: find the first term, u one, and the common difference, d. Step two: use the formula for S n, the sum of the first n terms. It works for any numbers, logs included. Step three: simplify with log laws into the form asked. Step four: for unknowns, write equations, solve, and turn logs back into numbers. The trap is the answer form. If the question wants L N two, change L N four into two L N two.

Worked example. June twenty twenty one. The nth term is u n, equal to n plus one, times L N four. The sum of the first n terms is S n. Show that S n is L N of two to the power n squared plus a n, and find the integer a. Five marks. Step one: n equal to one gives two L N four. n equal to two gives three L N four. The common difference is L N four. A tempting wrong route puts the bracket inside the log. But the bracket multiplies L N four. Check n equal to one: two L N four is L N sixteen, not L N eight. Step two: S n is n over two, times two a plus n minus one d. Put in the first term and difference. The bracket becomes n plus three, times L N four. Step three: L N four is two L N two. The two cancels the half, leaving n squared plus three n, times L N two. Step four: a multiplier goes back inside as a power. So S n is L N of two to the n squared plus three n. That matches, and a is three. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is the first term. Use n equal to one, not nought. Nought gives first term L N four, and then a equals one, which is wrong.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty. Given u two is L N twelve and u five is L N seven hundred and sixty eight, find a and b. Seven marks. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Two gives L N a b equals L N twelve, so a b is twelve. Five gives L N a plus four L N b, which is L N of a b to the fourth. So a b to the fourth is seven hundred and sixty eight. Divide the second by the first. b cubed is sixty four, so b is four. Then a is three. Check: three times four to the fourth is seven hundred and sixty eight. The common wrong answer subtracts inside the log, giving L N seven hundred and fifty six. Subtracting logs means dividing, which gives sixty four. Remember: terms, formula, log laws, then numbers.
