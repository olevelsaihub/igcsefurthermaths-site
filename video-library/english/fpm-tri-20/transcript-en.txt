Idea. Picture a door. You open it by an angle, then by the same angle again. Call the angle A. The total turn is two A, and we call it a double angle. Page two of your exam paper gives the addition formulae. They give the sine and cosine of A plus B. You do not need to memorise them, but you must know how to use them. Now make B the same as A. Then A plus B is A plus A, which is two A. So every addition formula turns into a double angle formula. The question says show that. So the result is given. Start from the formula, write every line, and end on the printed result. All the marks are for your working.

Method. Step one: write the addition formula from page two. Step two: replace B by A, and simplify. This gives cosine two A equals cosine squared A minus sine squared A. Step three: swap one square using sine squared plus cosine squared equals one. Replacing cosine squared gives one minus two sine squared. Replacing sine squared gives two cosine squared minus one. The trap is skipping a line. Each step carries a mark.

Worked example. June twenty twenty one. Using a formula from page two, show that cosine two x equals one minus two sine squared x. Three marks. It is a show that question, so we must end exactly on that result. Step one: the question has cosine of two x, so use the cosine formula. Write cosine of A plus B equals cosine A cosine B minus sine A sine B. Step two: put A and B both equal to x. Cosine of x plus x is cosine two x. The right side is cosine x times cosine x, minus sine x times sine x. That is cosine squared x minus sine squared x. Step three: the target has only sine. So replace cosine squared x by one minus sine squared x. Write this line out. It is a method mark. Step four: collect the sine terms. This gives one minus two sine squared x. That is the printed result. A tempting route is to say cosine two x is two cosine x. Try x as sixty degrees. Cosine of one hundred and twenty degrees is minus one half, but two cosine sixty is one. So the route is wrong. So every line is on the page. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common slip is a sign error in the swap. Cosine squared is one minus sine squared, not sine squared minus one. Writing it the wrong way round would give minus one.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty three. Use a formula on page two to show that sine squared A equals a half of one minus cosine two A. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Put B equal to A in cosine of A plus B. The target has only sine, so swap cosine squared A. This gives cosine two A equals one minus two sine squared A. Step four: rearrange. Add two sine squared A to both sides, and take cosine two A from both sides. Two sine squared A equals one minus cosine two A. Now divide by two. A common wrong answer halves only one term. Take A as thirty degrees. Sine squared of thirty degrees is one quarter, but this wrong form gives three quarters. Remember: write the addition formula, put B equal to A, swap with the identity, and end on the printed result.
