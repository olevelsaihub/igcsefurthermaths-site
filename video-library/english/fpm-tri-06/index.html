Regions made by tangents and a sector

Idea. Put a coin on a ruler. The ruler touches the coin at one point. That ruler is a tangent: a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point. Key fact: the radius to the touching point meets the tangent at a right angle, ninety degrees or pi over two radians. So radius and tangent make a right-angled triangle. If the angle at the centre is theta, the tangent is r times tan theta long. A sector is a slice of a circle. Its area is a half r squared theta, with theta in radians. The shaded region is the triangle minus the sector. You are given the area and asked for r. So build an equation in r, then solve it.

Method. Step one: mark the right angle where a radius meets a tangent. Step two: find the tangent length with tan, because the opposite side is r times tan of the angle. Step three: find the triangle area. Two tangents make a kite, which is two equal triangles. Step four: subtract the sector, then solve for r squared. The trap: thinking the tangent is as long as the radius.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. Sector A O B has radius r and angle nought point eight radians. C B is a tangent at B. The shaded area is one hundred and one. Find r to three significant figures. Six marks. Step one: C B is a tangent, so angle O B C is a right angle. Step two: B C is opposite and r is adjacent. So B C is r times tan nought point eight. Step three: triangle area is a half, r, times r tan nought point eight. The sector is a half r squared times nought point eight, which is nought point four r squared. A tempting wrong route takes the tangent as r. That gives nought point one r squared, and r about thirty one point eight. It is wrong. Step four: the shaded area is triangle minus sector, and it equals one hundred and one. Tan nought point eight is one point zero three, so r squared times nought point one one five is one hundred and one. The square root gives twenty nine point six six, so r is twenty nine point seven. Common mistake. A common mistake is leaving the calculator in degree mode. Then tan of nought point eight is tiny, and r comes out far too big.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty. A B and A C are tangents. Angle B O C is two pi over three radians. The shaded area is ten. Find r to three significant figures. Eight marks. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Join O to A. It cuts the angle in half, so angle B O A is pi over three. Triangle O B A has a right angle at B, so A B is r tan pi over three, root three r. The kite is two triangles: root three r squared. The sector is a half r squared times two pi over three, which is pi r squared over three. Kite minus sector is ten, so r squared is fourteen point six, and r is three point eight two centimetres. The common wrong answer uses one triangle only. Then r squared comes out negative, which is impossible. A negative square cannot be right, so that clue tells you to double the triangle. Remember the method: mark the right angle, use tan for the tangent, subtract the sector, then solve for r.
