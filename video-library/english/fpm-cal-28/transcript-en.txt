Idea. A taxi has a speedometer. It tells you the speed, but not where the taxi is. To find the position you add up the speed over time. That is integrating. To integrate is to do the reverse of differentiating. Two taxis can have the same speed all the time but start in different places. So integrating needs a constant, plus c. A starting condition, such as the position at time zero, tells us what c is. The rule: add one to the power, then divide by the new power. The integral of t squared is t cubed over three. A constant such as thirty becomes thirty t. Always add c at the end. These questions give a velocity or acceleration, and a fact about the particle at certain times. They ask for the velocity or position at another time, or for the starting position.

Method. Step one: integrate the given expression with respect to t. Step two: add plus c straight away. Do not wait until the end. Step three: put in the fact you were given, to find c. Step four: substitute the time you are asked about. The trap is the time of the fact: use the right t with the right value.

Worked example. January twenty twenty two. The velocity of P is three t squared, minus twenty three t, plus thirty. When t is zero, P is at d, nought. When t is eight, P is at twenty six, nought. Find d. Four marks. Step one: integrate the velocity. Three t squared becomes t cubed. Minus twenty three t becomes minus twenty three over two, t squared. Thirty becomes thirty t. Add c. Step two: when t is zero, x is d. Every term except c is zero, so c equals d. The constant is the starting position. Step three: when t is eight, x is twenty six. Eight cubed is five hundred and twelve. Twenty three over two times sixty four is seven hundred and thirty six. Thirty times eight is two hundred and forty. Five hundred and twelve, minus seven hundred and thirty six, plus two hundred and forty, is sixteen. So sixteen plus d equals twenty six, and d equals ten. A tempting wrong route is to say c is zero. Then x at eight is sixteen, not twenty six, so the fact in the question is broken. The constant must be found, never assumed. So d equals ten. Common mistake. A common mistake is a fraction slip when integrating. The integral of minus twenty three t is not minus twenty three t squared. The power goes up to two, so divide by two as well.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty four. The acceleration of P is three t, minus four. P starts at rest, at minus ten, nought. Find the displacement of P from the origin when t is three. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. First, integrate the acceleration to get velocity. Three halves t squared, minus four t, plus c. P is at rest when t is zero, so v is zero, and c is zero. Now integrate the velocity: t cubed over two, minus two t squared, plus k. When t is zero, x is minus ten, so k is minus ten. At t equals three: thirteen and a half, minus eighteen, minus ten. The most common wrong answer forgets the second constant. Then x at three is minus four and a half. The starting position minus ten must be added. Remember: integrate, add c, use the given fact to find c, then substitute the time asked.
