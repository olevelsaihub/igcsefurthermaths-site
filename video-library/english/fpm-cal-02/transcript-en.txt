Idea. Speed is distance divided by time. If both change, speed changes because of both. A fraction in maths is the same. Call the top u and the bottom v, and both can contain x. A quotient is a fraction. The quotient rule: bottom times the derivative of the top, minus top times the derivative of the bottom, all over the bottom squared. A square is never negative. So a constant over a square has the same sign as the constant. That is how we show a gradient is always positive or always negative. These questions ask you to differentiate a fraction, find its gradient at a point, or show the gradient is always positive or negative. All use the quotient rule.

Method. Step one: name the top u and the bottom v, and write both derivatives. Step two: write the formula with the bottom first. Getting the structure right earns the method mark. Step three: expand the top carefully, but leave the bottom squared. Step four: put in the x value for a gradient, or say why a constant over a square has one sign. The trap is the minus sign. You subtract the whole second term, so a bracket must be multiplied out with care.

Worked example. A real question from June twenty twenty five. A curve has equation y equals seven minus x, over two x plus three. It passes through the point A, one half, thirteen eighths. Show that the gradient at A is minus seventeen sixteenths. Three marks. It is a fraction with x on the bottom, so use the quotient rule. The top u is seven minus x, with derivative minus one. The bottom v is two x plus three, with derivative two. Put them in: bottom times the derivative of the top, minus the top times the derivative of the bottom, over the bottom squared. Expand the top. Minus two x minus three, minus fourteen, plus two x. The x terms cancel and leave minus seventeen. A tempting wrong route is to swap the order on top. That gives plus seventeen, and the sign is wrong, so the gradient would come out positive. The graph shows the curve is falling at A, so the gradient must be negative. Now put in x equals one half. Two x plus three is one plus three, which is four. Four squared is sixteen. So the gradient is minus seventeen over sixteen, as required. That is the exam-ready answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is writing the top the wrong way round, or forgetting to square the bottom. Both lose the method mark.

Your turn. Now you try. Differentiate x cubed, over cosine three x. It is from January twenty twenty two, three marks. Name u and v, use the quotient rule, then tidy up the top. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Let u be x cubed, with derivative three x squared. Let v be cosine three x, with derivative minus three sine three x. The minus sign matters. Bottom times the derivative of the top, minus top times the derivative of the bottom, over the bottom squared. The two minus signs make a plus. Take out three x squared. The most common wrong answer uses plus three sine three x for the derivative of the bottom. That turns the plus in the top into a minus, so the accuracy marks are lost. Remember: name u and v, write bottom times du, minus top times dv, over bottom squared, then expand the top only.
