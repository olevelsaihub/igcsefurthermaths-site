Idea. Doubling and halving undo each other. So do L N and e to the power. The natural log, L N, asks: what power of e gives this number? So L N y equals k means y equals e to the k. The log of a product is a sum, and the log of a power brings the power down. But there is no law for the log of a sum. You cannot split L N of, three minus x. The equation may look different from the plotted curve. Use the inverse, or a log law, until the curve appears on one side. The other side is the line. Read the x value where they meet. This question says: draw a straight line to estimate the root of a log or exponential equation. Rearrange, draw the line, and read x.

Method. Step one: compare the equation with the plotted curve. If the curve is e to the three x minus two, look for three x minus two beside a L N. Steps two and three: isolate the log term, then apply the inverse to the whole side, until the plotted curve stands alone. The other side is the line. Steps four and five: draw the line and read x to one decimal place. The trap is splitting a log of a sum. Leave L N of, three minus x, whole.

Common mistake. A common mistake is undoing only part of one side. Take e to the power of the whole side, three x minus two, not just three x.
