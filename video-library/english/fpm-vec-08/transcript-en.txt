Idea. Slice a pizza from the centre, a small slice and a big slice. Both have the same height, so the wider crust means the bigger slice. For triangles with the same height, area follows the base. Why? Area is half base times height. With the same height, only the base changes. Triangles may share an angle instead. Area is half a b sine C. If a small triangle takes fractions p and q of the two sides, its area is p times q times the big area. This type uses length ratios from vector work to compare areas. Build the shape from triangles, then write a ratio.

Method. Step one: list the length ratios from earlier parts. Step two: find a triangle with a known fraction of the whole shape, such as half a parallelogram. Step three: compare a smaller triangle with it. Shared angle: multiply the side fractions. Shared height: use the ratio of bases. Step four: add or subtract areas. Step five: write the ratio in simplest whole numbers. The trap: a length ratio is not an area ratio when both sides change.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. O A B C is a parallelogram. M and N are the midpoints of A B and B C. O B meets M N at X, and O X is three quarters of O B. Find the ratio of area O X N C to area O A B C. Three marks. Let the parallelogram have area S. The diagonal O B cuts it in half, so triangle O B C has area half S. X is three quarters along O B, so X B is one quarter of O B. N is the midpoint of B C, so B N is half of B C. Triangles X B N and O B C share the angle at B. Multiply the side fractions: a quarter times a half is an eighth. So X B N is one sixteenth of S. O X N C is triangle O B C minus triangle X B N. Half S minus S over sixteen is seven S over sixteen. A tempting wrong route uses three quarters for X B. Then X B N is three sixteenths of S, and the ratio is five to sixteen. So the ratio is seven S over sixteen to S: seven to sixteen. Common mistake. A common mistake is changing one side only. X B is a quarter of O B, but B N is also half of B C. Both sides change, so the area is an eighth, not a quarter.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty four. Use the trapezium. B X is three eighths of B D. Find the ratio of the area of triangle C X D to the area of the trapezium. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. B X is three eighths of B D, so X D is five eighths. Triangles C X D and C B D share the vertex C, with bases on B D. So C X D is five eighths of C B D. Triangle C B D and the trapezium have the same height. So compare the parallel sides: triangle C B D is three eighths of the trapezium. Then C X D is five eighths of three eighths, which is fifteen sixty fourths. The ratio is fifteen to sixty four. The most common wrong answer is five to eight. That is only the fraction of triangle C B D, not of the whole trapezium. Remember: list the ratios, find a known area, compare triangles, then add or subtract, and simplify.
