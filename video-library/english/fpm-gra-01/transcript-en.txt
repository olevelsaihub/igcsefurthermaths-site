Idea. A table of values pairs each input x with its output y. To substitute is to replace the letter with a number. Put x equals two into y equals two x plus one, and y is five. Log base three of nine asks: what power of three gives nine? The answer is two. Calculators have no base three key, so use change of base: L N of the number, over L N of three. L N is the natural log key. Trig curves in this paper use radians, not degrees. Set the calculator to R A D. Sine of one is point eight four in radians, but nearly nought in degrees. This question gives a table with some y values missing. You fill each empty box, to the decimal places asked.

Method. Step one: copy the equation and check the calculator mode. Step two: recompute one printed value. If it matches, you are set. Step three: substitute each missing x, with brackets for negatives and change of base for logs. Step four: round to the places asked. Step five: look along the row. Values should change smoothly. Here six sticks out where nine belongs. That is a key slip.
