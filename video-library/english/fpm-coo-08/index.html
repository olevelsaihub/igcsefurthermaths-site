Finding a point from a line and a distance

Idea. A map says the chest is ten steps from the tree, along a straight path. There are two such places, one each side of the tree. A point on a line at a fixed distance from a given point works the same way. Distance comes from the distance formula, which uses squares. So square the given length. Six root five, squared, is thirty six times five, which is one hundred and eighty. The line gives one coordinate using the other, so the equation has one unknown. It is a quadratic, so it gives two points. A sign condition may pick just one. These questions give an unknown point, a line it lies on, and a length. They ask for the coordinates, often both pairs.

Method. Step one: use the line or gradient to write one coordinate using the other. Step two: if the length is not given, find it first. Step three: use the distance formula, set it equal to the length squared, and solve. Step four: find both points, then use any sign condition. The traps are not squaring the length, and using the wrong line.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. The line l one is y plus two x minus four equals zero, through P, a, six. The line l two is through P, perpendicular to l one. R, e, f, is on l two with P R equal to six root five. Find the two possible pairs. Eight marks. From part a, P is minus one, six. Line l one has gradient minus two, so l two has gradient one half. Through P, f is e plus thirteen over two. Step two: P R squared is one hundred and eighty. The change in x is e plus one. The change in y is e plus one over two. So e plus one squared, plus a quarter of it, equals one hundred and eighty. Step three: five quarters of e plus one squared is one hundred and eighty. So e plus one squared is one hundred and forty four, and e plus one is twelve or minus twelve. So e is eleven or minus thirteen. Step four: find f. When e is eleven, f is twelve. When e is minus thirteen, f is nought. A tempting wrong route is to use l one, because its equation is printed. But R is on l two, the perpendicular line. So the pairs are eleven, twelve, and minus thirteen, nought. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is not squaring a surd properly. Six root five, squared, is not thirty. It is thirty six times five, which is one hundred and eighty.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty nineteen. A is minus one, three. B is five, six. P is three, five. C is m, n, with m positive, and C P is perpendicular to A B. The circle through A, P and C has radius five. Find m and n. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. A B has gradient one half, so C P has gradient minus two, through P. That gives n equals minus two m plus eleven. The angle at P is ninety degrees, so A C is a diameter. The radius is five, so A C is ten. A C squared is one hundred, so m plus one squared, plus n minus three squared, equals one hundred. That gives m minus seven, times, m plus one, equals zero. M is positive, so m is seven, and n is minus three. The most common wrong answer takes five as the distance A C. But five is the radius, so A C is ten. Remember: use the line, square the length, solve, find both points, and use the sign condition.
