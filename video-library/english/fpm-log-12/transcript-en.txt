Idea. Two clues, two mystery numbers: x plus y is ten, and x minus y is two. Simultaneous equations are two equations true at the same time. We find the x and y that fit both. Here the clues hide in powers or logs. The number e is about two point seven one eight. L N is the log to base e. L N of e to the k is k, so they undo each other. The plan: turn each equation into ordinary algebra. Match powers, or undo the log. Then you have a normal pair of equations. Then substitute. Make one letter the subject of one equation. Put it into the other. One unknown is left to solve. So you solve two equations with powers or logs for x and y. Then check every log has a positive number inside.

Method. Step one: turn each equation into ordinary algebra. Step two: make one unknown, or one exponential like e to the two y, the subject. Step three: substitute and solve. Step four: find the other unknown. Step five: check the logs, and round only at the end. The trap: e to the two y plus one is e to the two y times e, not plus e.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. Solve these simultaneous equations to two decimal places: e to the two y minus x plus two equals zero, and L N of x plus three, minus two y, minus one, equals zero. Eight marks. Step one: from the first equation, e to the two y equals x minus two. Treat e to the two y as one unknown. Step two: in the second, L N of x plus three equals two y plus one. Undo L N with e: x plus three equals e to the two y plus one, which is e times e to the two y. Step three: substitute x minus two for e to the two y. We get x plus three equals e times x minus two. A tempting wrong route is adding the two equations. Nothing cancels, as x and y sit in different kinds of terms. Step four: collect x terms. Two e plus three equals x times e minus one. So x is two e plus three over e minus one, which is four point nine one. Step five: e to the two y is x minus two, about two point nine one. So y is half of its L N: nought point five three. Common mistake. A common mistake is writing x plus three as e to the two y plus e. Adding powers means multiplying: e to the two y times e.

Answer. Write everything as powers of two. Sixteen is two to the four, eight is two cubed, a quarter is two to the minus two. Dividing subtracts powers: four x minus three y equals minus two. Second equation: four is two squared and sixteen is two to the four. Multiplying adds powers: two x plus y equals four. Substitute y equals four minus two x. Four x minus twelve plus six x equals minus two. Ten x equals ten, so x is one and y is two. The most common wrong answer divides the powers instead of subtracting them. Two to the four x, over two to the three y, is two to the power four x minus three y. Remember: same base or undo the log, make ordinary equations, substitute, then find the other letter.
