In a geometric series, n sits in a power, like r to the n. To bring n down, take logarithms, or logs. The rule: log of r to the n is n times log r. When r is a fraction, its log is negative. Dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign. This step loses many marks. These questions ask for the least n so that a sum or a term passes a target. You use logs, then round to a whole number.

Method. Step one: write the sum or term using a and r, and make the inequality. Step two: get the power of r on its own, before taking logs. Step three: take logs, bring n down, and divide by log r. If r is a fraction, log r is negative, so reverse the sign. Step four: round to a whole number the right way, and check. The trap is not reversing the sign.

Worked example. June twenty twenty four, paper one. The first term is three hundred and twenty and the common ratio is one quarter. Using logarithms, find the least n such that S n is greater than four hundred and twenty six point six. Four marks. Step one: S n is a times one minus r to the n, over one minus r. That is twelve hundred and eighty over three, times one minus one quarter to the n. Step two: divide by twelve hundred and eighty over three. Then one quarter to the n is less than one over six thousand four hundred. Step three: take logs. N times log of one quarter is less than log of one over six thousand four hundred. Step four: log of one quarter is negative, so dividing reverses the sign. N is greater than log six thousand four hundred over log four. That is six point three two. Reject a tempting route. Not reversing the sign gives n less than six point three two, so n is six. But S six is four hundred and twenty six point five six, not above the target. So the least whole number is seven. Common mistake. Another mistake is taking logs too early. Log of one minus a power is not log one minus log of the power. Isolate the power first.
