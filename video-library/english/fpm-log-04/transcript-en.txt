Idea. A square tile has an area of twelve square units. Its side is the square root of twelve. That is a surd: a root that is not a whole number. Exact means we keep the root, not a calculator decimal. To simplify a surd, take out a square number. Twelve is four times three, and the root of four is two. So root twelve is two root three. The denominator is the bottom of a fraction. To rationalise means to remove the surd from the bottom. Multiply top and bottom by the same number. One over root five becomes root five over five. If the bottom has two terms, use its conjugate: the same terms with the sign between them changed. Multiply them and the surd vanishes: twenty minus one is nineteen. These questions give a fraction and ask for a and b in the form a plus b root c. You rationalise, simplify, then compare.

Method. Step one: put the surd fraction on its own. If a side comes from an area, divide the area by the known length. Step two: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the bottom. Step three: expand the top term by term. Step four: collect whole numbers and surds, and divide. Step five: compare with the form. The trap: two root five squared is twenty, not ten.

Worked example. November twenty twenty three. A right-angled triangle has sides two plus four root five, and a plus b root five. Its area is thirty four plus eleven root five. Find a and b without a calculator. Four marks. Step one: the area is a half times the two short sides. Half of two plus four root five is one plus two root five. So that times a plus b root five equals the area. Step two: divide, then multiply top and bottom by the conjugate. Write the bottom as two root five plus one. Its conjugate is two root five minus one. A tempting wrong route is multiplying by the bottom itself. The bottom becomes twenty one plus four root five. The surd is still there. Step three: the top. Thirty four times two root five is sixty eight root five. Then minus thirty four. Then one hundred and ten. Then minus eleven root five. Step four: the bottom is twenty minus one, so nineteen. Divide: four plus three root five. So a is four and b is three. That is exact, with no calculator. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is squaring three root three as nine root three. Square the three too: nine times three is twenty seven.
