Idea. A sat nav says your journey takes about twenty minutes. That is an estimate: close, but not exact. A binomial expansion can estimate a number such as the root of nought point seven six. The trick is to choose x so that the bracket becomes your number. Set one minus four x equal to nought point seven six. Then x is nought point nought six. Small x gives the best estimate. The powers of nought point nought six shrink fast: x squared is nought point nought nought three six. So the terms we leave out are tiny. The x must also be in the valid range. Here, modulus x is less than one quarter. Nought point nought six is inside. Nought point seven six is not. So you use your expansion, with a suitable x, to estimate a number. You choose x, substitute, add, and round last.

Method. Step one: set the bracket equal to the number, and solve for x. Step two: check x is in the valid range. Step three: substitute into every term, and add. Step four: round only at the end. A later part may scale it: the root of nineteen is five times the root of nought point seven six. The trap is putting x equal to the number itself. You solve for x first.

Common mistake. A common mistake is rounding every term early. Keep all the digits. Round the final sum once, or the last digit can change.
