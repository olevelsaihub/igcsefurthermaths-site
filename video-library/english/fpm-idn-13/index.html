Idea. Two runners start together. One runs at a steady speed, a straight line on a graph. The other speeds up, a curve. They are level where the graphs share a point. That is a point of intersection. At a meeting point both equations are true together. Both give y, so make the two expressions equal. This is solving simultaneous equations. You get a quadratic. Two solutions mean two points. Find each y from the straight line, which is simpler, and keep each x with its own y. The question says use algebra to find the coordinates. Show the full method. Reading from a graph earns nothing.

Method. Step one: make one letter the subject of the linear equation, or equate the two expressions for y. Step two: rearrange to a quadratic equal to zero. Step three: solve the quadratic. Step four: put each x into the linear equation to find y. Step five: write each answer as a coordinate pair. The trap is pairing x and y wrongly. Always find y from the same x using the linear equation.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. The curve C has equation y equals f of x, which is eight x squared plus ten x minus three. The straight line l has equation y equals two x plus thirteen. Use algebra to find the coordinates of the two points of intersection. Four marks. Step one: equate. Eight x squared, plus ten x, minus three, equals two x plus thirteen. Rearrange: eight x squared plus eight x minus sixteen equals zero. Divide by eight: x squared plus x minus two equals zero. Step two: factorise. x plus two, times x minus one, equals zero. So x is minus two, or x is one. Step three: find each y from the line. When x is minus two, y is two times minus two plus thirteen, which is nine. When x is one, y is two plus thirteen, which is fifteen. Here are the two points on the graph. Check on the curve: eight times four, minus twenty, minus three, is nine. Eight plus ten minus three is fifteen. Both fit. A tempting wrong route is to swap the pairing, giving minus two, fifteen. Check it on the line: two times minus two plus thirteen is nine, not fifteen. So that point is not on the line. So the points are minus two, nine and one, fifteen. Common mistake. A common mistake is to give only the x values. The question asks for coordinates. Give minus two, nine, and one, fifteen.

Answer. Subtract the first equation from the second. That removes x y: x plus two y equals seventeen. Substitute into x y equals thirty six. Seventeen minus two y, times y, equals thirty six. That is two y squared minus seventeen y plus thirty six equals zero. Factorise: two y minus nine, times y minus four. So y is nine over two, or four. Now find each x. When y is nine over two, x is eight. When y is four, x is nine. A common wrong answer swaps the pairs, which fails: nine times nine over two is not thirty six. Remember: equate or substitute, rearrange to a quadratic, solve, then find each partner and write pairs.
