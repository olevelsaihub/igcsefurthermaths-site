Idea. Multiply out one plus x, cubed, and it stops. But the square root of one plus x never stops. A binomial expansion rewrites a bracket to a power as a sum of powers of x. Ascending means smallest power first. Here is the formula. One plus X, to the n, is one, plus n X, plus n times n minus one over two factorial, times X squared, and so on. Three factorial is three times two times one, which is six. Rewrite roots and fractions as powers. The root of one minus four x is one minus four x, to the power a half. One over one plus three x, squared, is one plus three x, to the minus two. X is the whole second term, with its sign and number. For one minus four x, X is minus four x. So X squared is sixteen x squared. So you get a root, fraction or negative power, and expand up to a stated power of x, term by term.

Method. Step one: write one plus something, to a power. Step two: find n and X. The formula only works when the bracket starts with one. Step three: write each term with X in brackets. Step four: simplify each coefficient, watching the signs. The trap is squaring only the x. Minus four x, squared, is sixteen x squared.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. Expand the square root of one minus four x in ascending powers of x, up to and including the x cubed term. Each coefficient is an integer. Three marks. Step one: rewrite as a power. The root is one minus four x, to the power one half. So n is a half, and X is minus four x. Step two: write the terms with brackets. The coefficients use a half, minus a half, and minus three halves. The last is divided by six. Step three: simplify. A half times minus four x is minus two x. Minus one eighth times sixteen is minus two. One sixteenth times minus sixty four is minus four. A tempting wrong route squares only the x. That gives plus a half x squared, which is wrong. The correct term is minus two x squared. So the expansion is one, minus two x, minus two x squared, minus four x cubed. A quick check: x equal to nought point nought one gives about nought point nine eight, the same as the root of nought point nine six. Common mistake. A common mistake is a sign slip. Do not guess the pattern. Work each sign out term by term, from n minus one, n minus two, and from X.

Answer. Here n is minus five, and X is p x. The coefficients are one, minus five, fifteen, minus thirty five, and seventy. Now raise p x to each power. The terms are one, minus five p x, plus fifteen p squared x squared, minus thirty five p cubed x cubed, plus seventy p to the fourth x to the fourth. The common wrong answer squares only the x, giving fifteen p x squared. The p needs the same power. Remember: write a power, find n and X, use the formula with brackets, then simplify every term.
