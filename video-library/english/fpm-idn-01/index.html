Idea. A fairground sign says: you must be taller than one point two metres. That is an inequality. Heights above one point two are allowed. One point two itself is not. We solve an inequality like an equation, with one new rule. Divide both sides by a negative number and the sign flips. Minus three x less than six becomes x greater than minus two. A question may say both. Both means true at the same time. That is the overlap. If x is less than five and more than two, both are true between two and five. These questions say: find the set of values of x. That means all the numbers that work. A surd, a root like root two, may be the coefficient. Check its sign first.

Method. Step one: expand any brackets. Step two: collect the x terms on one side and the numbers on the other. This never changes the sign. Step three: divide by the coefficient of x. If it is negative, flip the sign. For a surd, decide its sign first. Step four: rationalise the denominator, which means remove the surd from the bottom. Step five: for both, draw each set on a number line and keep only the overlap. The trap is giving either set.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. Find the values of x for which: a, two times, three x minus one, is less than four minus three x. And c, both that and three x squared minus eight x minus three is less than nought. Three marks. Part a, step one: expand. Two times three x minus one is six x minus two. A tempting wrong route is six x minus one. Both terms in the bracket are multiplied. Step two: add three x and two to both sides. That gives nine x less than six. Step three: divide by nine. It is positive, so the sign stays. x is less than two thirds. Check. At x equals nought, minus two is less than four: true. At x equals one, four is not less than one: false. Part c. Part b gives x between minus a third and three. Part a gives x less than two thirds. The overlap runs from minus a third to two thirds. A tempting wrong route is giving either set, which is x less than three. Both means the overlap, so the lower end comes from part b. So part a gives x less than two thirds. Part c gives x between minus a third and two thirds. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is forgetting to flip the sign when dividing by a negative. Test x equals nought. It works in minus three x less than six, but not in x less than minus two.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty four. Without a calculator, solve root fifty times x, minus root eighteen, is greater than six x plus five. Give an exact answer with a rationalised denominator. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Root fifty is five root two. Root eighteen is three root two. Collect: five root two minus six, times x, is greater than five plus three root two. Five root two is about seven, so the coefficient is positive and the sign stays. Rationalise: multiply top and bottom by five root two plus six. The bottom is fourteen. The top is sixty plus forty three root two. The most common wrong answer flips the sign by mistake, because of the minus six. But five root two minus six is positive, so flipping is wrong. Remember: collect, check the sign of the divisor, flip only if negative, rationalise, and for both keep the overlap.
