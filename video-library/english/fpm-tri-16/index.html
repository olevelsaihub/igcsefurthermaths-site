Angle between two planes using a right-angled triangle

Idea. Open a book on a table. The cover and the table are two planes, meaning flat surfaces. They meet along the spine. The angle between the planes is how far the cover has opened. Pick a point on the common line. In each plane, draw a perpendicular, a line at right angles to the common line, through that point. The angle between those two lines is the angle between the planes. A sloping face of a pyramid meets the base along a base edge. Take the midpoint M of that edge. V M and O M are both perpendicular to it. So the angle is V M O. This type asks for the angle between a sloping face and the base. Find the midpoint of the edge, then use trigonometry.

Method. Step one: find the line where the planes meet. Step two: take its midpoint, and draw a perpendicular in each plane. Name the angle. Step three: find two sides of triangle V O M. Step four: use tan, sin or cos. The trap is using a sloping edge, instead of the midpoint of the edge.

Worked example. June twenty twenty three. A right pyramid has a regular pentagon base A B C D E, with sides two x. The sloping edges are three x. Find the angle between the plane V B C and the base. Six marks. Step one: the common line is B C. Let M be its midpoint, and O the centre. V M and O M are both perpendicular to B C. So we need angle V M O. Step two: in triangle V B M, V B is three x and B M is x. So V M squared is nine x squared minus x squared. V M is two root two x. Step three: O M. The angle at the centre is three hundred and sixty over five, seventy two degrees. Angle B O M is half of it, thirty six. So O M is x over tan thirty six, one point three seven six x. Step four: cosine of angle V M O is O M over V M. That is nought point four eight six six. The angle is sixty point nine degrees. A tempting route uses the sloping edge V B instead of V M. That gives fifty five point five degrees, the angle between the edge and the base, not between the planes. So the angle between the plane V B C and the base is sixty point nine degrees. Common mistake. A common mistake is using the whole side instead of half in the slant height. Then V M is root five x, and the angle is fifty two degrees.

Answer. Triangle A V C has a right angle at V, and V A equals V C. So V O is half of A C, which is eight root two. The common line is A B. M is its midpoint, so O M is eight. The angle is V M O. Tan is V O over O M, which is root two. The angle is fifty four point seven degrees. The common wrong answer uses A O instead of O M. That gives tan equal to one, and forty five degrees. Remember: common line, midpoint, two perpendiculars, then trigonometry.
