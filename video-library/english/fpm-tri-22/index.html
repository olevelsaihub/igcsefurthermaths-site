Idea. Five plus five is the same as two times five. One is a sum, because things are added. The other is a product, because things are multiplied. Trigonometry has the same trick, and it works in both directions. On the left of the second line, two cosines are added. On the right, two cosines are multiplied. So a sum of cosines has become a product of cosines. These identities come from page two. Write the formula for cosine of A plus B, and for cosine of A minus B. Add them. The sine terms cancel, and two cosine A cosine B is left. So the question says show that, or hence show that. Write the expansions, add or subtract, and then choose the angles.

Method. Step one: write the formulae for A plus B and A minus B. Step two: add or subtract so that the unwanted terms cancel. Step three: match your angles. Choose A and B directly, or set P equal to A plus B, and Q equal to A minus B. Then solve for A and B. Step four: substitute and simplify. The trap is forgetting to halve. P plus Q is two A, not A.

Worked example. November twenty twenty. Part a showed that cosine of A plus B, plus cosine of A minus B, equals two cosine A cosine B. Hence show that cosine P plus cosine Q equals two cosine of P plus Q over two, times cosine of P minus Q over two. Three marks. Step one: match the angles. The two angles on the left are A plus B and A minus B. Call them P and Q. So P is A plus B, and Q is A minus B. Step two: find A. Add the two equations. P plus Q is two A. So A is P plus Q, over two. Step three: find B. Subtract. P minus Q is two B. So B is P minus Q, over two. Step four: put A and B into two cosine A cosine B. The left side is cosine P plus cosine Q. That is the printed result. A tempting route forgets to halve, so A is P plus Q. Try P as sixty degrees and Q as nought. The left side is one and a half. The wrong version gives one half. So state P and Q, find A and B, substitute, and finish on the printed result. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common slip is the sign in cosine of A minus B. It is plus, not minus. With A as sixty and B as thirty, the wrong sign gives nought.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty two. This time a product becomes a sum. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Write the two sine formulae. Sine of A plus B has plus cosine A sine B. Sine of A minus B has minus cosine A sine B. Add them. Those terms cancel, leaving two sine A cosine B. Now match the angles. The product has sine of four x and cosine of x. So A is four x and B is x. A plus B is five x, and A minus B is three x. A common wrong choice swaps A and B. Then A minus B is minus three x, and sine of minus three x is minus sine three x. That gives a different result. Remember: write the expansions, add or subtract, choose the angles, and halve when you solve for them.
