Idea. Pour juice into a cone-shaped paper cup. As the juice rises, its surface gets wider. So the radius and the height of the juice are linked by the shape of the cone. The vertical angle is the angle at the tip. Cut the cone in half, and the angle is halved. In this right-angled triangle, r is h tan of the half-angle. For sixty degrees, the half-angle is thirty, so r is h over root three. A cone has volume a third pi r squared h. A pyramid is a third of base area times height. A prism is cross-section area times length. Put r in terms of h, so the volume has only h in it. Liquid filling at a constant rate from empty has volume rate times time. This type asks you to use trigonometry to write the volume in one letter, then solve.

Method. Step one: draw the right-angled cross-section and use trig to write one length using the other. Step two: put it into the volume formula. Step three: simplify, or set the volume equal to rate times time. Step four: rearrange for the unknown, such as h. The trap is using the full vertical angle instead of half of it.

Worked example. November twenty twenty five. A cone with its tip at the bottom has vertical angle sixty degrees. It starts empty, and liquid flows in at nought point two cubic centimetres per second. Show that h is the cube root of nine t over five pi. Five marks. Here is the cone. The liquid surface is a circle of radius r, at height h above the tip. The half-angle is thirty degrees. Step two: r over h is tan thirty. So r is h over root three, and r squared is h squared over three. Step three: the volume of the liquid is a third pi r squared h. Replace r squared. That gives pi h cubed over nine. Step four: the liquid fills at nought point two per second from empty. So V is nought point two t, which is t over five. Step five: put the two volumes equal. Pi h cubed over nine equals t over five. So h cubed is nine t over five pi. Take the cube root. That is the printed result. A tempting route uses the full angle, sixty degrees. Then r is root three h, and the volume is pi h cubed. That gives the cube root of t over five pi, with no nine. That is wrong. So h is the cube root of nine t over five pi, as required. Common mistake. A common mistake is forgetting the one third in the volume formula. Then V is pi h cubed over three, and h cubed is three t over five pi. That is not the printed result.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty four. Use the volume to find x. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The base is a rectangle, two x by x, so its area is two x squared. The height is root five over two, times x. The volume is a third, times two x squared, times that height. That is root five over three, times x cubed. Set it equal to nine root five. Divide both sides by root five, then multiply by three. x cubed is twenty seven, so x is three. The common wrong answer forgets the one third. Then root five x cubed is nine root five, so x cubed is nine, and x is two point zero eight. That is too small. Remember: link the lengths with trig, write the volume in one letter, then equate and solve.
