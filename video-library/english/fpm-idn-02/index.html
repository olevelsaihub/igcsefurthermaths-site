Idea. A half pipe is a U shape. A skater is low only between the two edges, and high outside them. A quadratic graph is the same: below the axis between two crossing points, above it outside them. A quadratic inequality has x squared in it, like three x squared minus eight x minus three is less than nought. The critical values are the x values where it equals nought. They are the boundaries. Which region do we want? For a U shape, less than nought means between the two roots. Greater than nought means outside them, written as two separate inequalities. This question type asks: find the set of values of x. Give one interval for inside, or two inequalities for outside.

Method. Step one: move every term to one side, so the other side is nought. Keep the x squared positive if you can. Collect carefully. This is where slips happen. Step two: solve the equals nought version, by factorising or the formula. These are the critical values. Step three: sketch the parabola through them. The sketch shows the region. Steps four and five: choose inside for less than nought, or two outside parts for greater than nought. The trap is joining outside parts into one inequality.

Worked example. January twenty twenty three. Find the set of values of x for which three x squared, minus eight x, minus three, is less than nought. Four marks. Step one: one side is already nought, and the x squared coefficient, three, is positive. So the graph is a U shape. Step two: solve three x squared minus eight x minus three equals nought. It factorises as three x plus one, times x minus three. So x is minus a third, or three. These are the critical values. Step three: sketch the U through minus a third and three. We want the expression to be less than nought. That is where the curve is below the axis, shaded here. Step four: choose the region. The curve is below the axis between the roots. So the answer is x between minus a third and three. A tempting wrong route is the outside region, x less than minus a third, or x greater than three. That is the answer to greater than nought. We need less than. Check. At x equals nought the value is minus three, less than nought, and nought is between the roots. At x equals four the value is thirteen, so it fails. So the set of values is x between minus a third and three. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is joining outside regions into one inequality. Three is less than x is less than minus a half is impossible. Write two inequalities with or.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty five. Find the set of values of x for which ten x squared, plus seven x, is less than twelve. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Move twelve across: ten x squared plus seven x minus twelve is less than nought. It factorises as two x plus three, times five x minus four. The critical values are minus three halves and four fifths. The coefficient is positive, so the graph is a U. Less than nought means below the axis, between the roots: x between minus three halves and four fifths. The common wrong answer factorises without moving the twelve across, giving x less than twelve. That is wrong. One side must be nought first. Remember: one side zero, find the critical values, sketch the U, choose the region, and write the set.
