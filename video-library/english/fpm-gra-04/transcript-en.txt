Idea. Share a twelve pound bill between x friends. Each pays twelve over x. You cannot share between nought friends. With a thousand friends each pays almost nothing, but never exactly nothing. These two lines are called asymptotes. A rational curve is a fraction with x on the top and bottom, like a x plus b over c x plus d. Its vertical asymptote is parallel to the y axis. You cannot divide by zero, so the denominator is not zero there. The horizontal asymptote, parallel to the x axis, is where y settles when x is huge. Then plain numbers hardly matter. Take y equals two x plus one, over x plus one. As x grows, y gets closer to two, which is two over one. The question says: write down an equation of the asymptote parallel to an axis. Answer with a full equation, x equals something or y equals something.

Method. Step one: for the vertical asymptote, set the denominator equal to zero and solve. It must match the value the question says x cannot be. Step two: for the horizontal asymptote, divide the coefficient of x on top by the coefficient of x below. Watch the signs. Seven minus x has coefficient minus one. Steps three and four: write each answer as an equation, and check the vertical one against the question. The trap is a bare number. Three over two is not an asymptote. x equals three over two is.

Worked example. June twenty twenty two. A curve C is y equals seven x minus two, over, two x minus three, where x is not three over two. Write down the asymptote parallel to the y axis and the one parallel to the x axis. Two marks. Step one: parallel to the y axis. Set the denominator to zero. Two x minus three is nought, so x is three over two. This matches the question. Step two: parallel to the x axis. The coefficient of x on top is seven. On the bottom it is two. So y is seven over two. Check with big values of x. At ten, y is four. At a hundred, three point five four. At a thousand, three point five. It settles near seven over two. A tempting wrong route is to divide the constants: minus two over minus three. That is two thirds, where the curve crosses the y axis, not its asymptote. Here is the curve. Each branch hugs both dashed lines but never touches them. So the asymptote parallel to the y axis is x equals three over two. The asymptote parallel to the x axis is y equals seven over two. Common mistake. A common mistake is a sign slip. If x plus four is nought, x is minus four, not four. Moving a number across changes its sign.

Answer. Parallel to the y axis: two x plus three is nought, so x is minus three over two. Parallel to the x axis: on top, seven minus x has x coefficient minus one. On the bottom it is two. So y is minus a half. The most common wrong answer drops the minus sign and gives y equals a half. The top is seven minus x, so the coefficient is minus one. Remember: denominator zero for the vertical line, ratio of x coefficients for the horizontal line, then full equations.
