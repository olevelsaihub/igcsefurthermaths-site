Idea. At a theme park you must be taller than one metre, and shorter than two metres. Only people who pass every rule ride. On a graph, an inequality is a rule about x and y. The region is every point that passes all the rules. One inequality splits the grid in two. Its line is the boundary. Points on one side make the inequality true. Points on the other side make it false. To find the true side, use a test point. Pick a point that is not on the line, such as the origin. Put it in the inequality. If it is true, shade the side with that point. The question says: show, by shading, the region R. You shade the one region where every inequality is true, and write R in it.

Method. Step one: take each inequality in turn and pick a test point not on its line. The origin is best. Step two: say whether the test point is true, and mark the wanted side. Step three: find the part of the grid on the wanted side of every line. It is usually a triangle. Step four: shade it and label it R. The trap is testing a point on the line. It always fits a less than or equal sign, so it tells you nothing.

Worked example. June twenty twenty three, part b. Shade the region R defined by these three inequalities. The lines are drawn already, from part a. One mark. Test the origin in each. Nought is less than or equal to five: true. Nought is greater than or equal to minus eight: true. Nought is less than or equal to thirty: true. So R is on the origin side. On the grid, the origin is below line one, above line two, and below line three. R is the region on that side of all three. The lines cross at three corners: minus four, minus three; five over thirteen, seventy five over thirteen; and one hundred and sixty over seventeen, six over seventeen. You do not need them to shade. A tempting wrong route is shading below all three lines. But the origin is above line two, and the test said true. So the origin must be inside R. So shade the triangle and write R in it. Common mistake. A common mistake is to test a point on the line. Try nought, five. Five is less than or equal to five, which is true on both sides, so it proves nothing.

Your turn. Now you try, from January twenty twenty. Part a drew five x plus two y equals ten, and y equals x. Shade and label the region R for these four inequalities. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. First draw the missing lines, y equals minus two and x equals one. The origin lies on y equals x, so test the point two, nought for that line. Nought is less than or equal to two: true. For the other three, the origin gives true, true, and false for x greater than or equal to one. So R is below y equals x, below the line five x plus two y equals ten, above y equals minus two, and to the right of x equals one. It has four sides. Shade it and write R inside it. The common wrong answer tests the origin in y less than or equal to x. The origin lies on that line, so the test proves nothing, and the region is often shaded above the line. Forgetting to write R also loses a mark. Remember: test a point off the line for each inequality, find the overlap, shade it and label R.
