Idea. Picture a ladder against a wall. Its length comes from how far out the foot is, and how high it reaches. That is Pythagoras. A vector works the same way. The length of a vector is called its magnitude. For a vector with i part x and j part y, the magnitude is the square root of x squared plus y squared. We write the magnitude of v with two straight lines, like this. To avoid a square root, work with magnitude squared. Magnitude squared is x squared plus y squared. If the length is given, set this equal to the length squared, and an unknown inside the vector can be found. This question type gives a length and asks for an unknown such as p. Often there are two possible values, because squaring loses the sign.

Method. Step one: write the vector in components, subtracting position vectors if needed. Step two: write magnitude squared as the sum of the squares of the components. Step three: set it equal to the given length squared. Step four: expand and solve, often a quadratic. Then use any condition, or give all possible values. The trap is forgetting to square the given length. If the length is three root ten, the right side is ninety, not three root ten.

Worked example. January twenty twenty. Vector A B is seven i plus p j. Vector A D is four i minus two p j. The length of B D is three root ten. Find the possible values of p. Three marks. First, vector B D. End minus start: vector A D minus vector A B. Four i minus two p j, minus, seven i plus p j, is minus three i minus three p j. Magnitude squared is the sum of the squares. Minus three, squared, is nine. Minus three p, squared, is nine p squared. This equals the given length squared. Three root ten, squared, is nine times ten, which is ninety. So nine plus nine p squared equals ninety. Nine p squared is eighty one, so p squared is nine. Then p is three, or minus three. Both give the same length, because squaring loses the sign. A tempting wrong route forgets to square the right side: nine plus nine p squared equals three root ten. That gives p of about nought point two three, and the check fails, because the length of B D would be only about three. So p is three or minus three. Common mistake. A common mistake is squaring badly. Minus three p, squared, is nine p squared, not minus nine p squared. The wrong sign gives p squared equals minus nine, which is impossible.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty three. Vector O A is five i plus seven j. Vector A B is a i plus sixteen j. The length of O B is five root twenty nine. Find the possible values of a. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. First vector O B: O A plus A B, which is five plus a in the i part, and twenty three in the j part. Magnitude squared equals the length squared, twenty five times twenty nine, which is seven hundred and twenty five. Take away five twenty nine: five plus a, squared, is one ninety six. Take the square root, positive and negative. Five plus a is fourteen, or minus fourteen. So a is nine, or minus nineteen. The most common wrong answer gives only a equals nine. The question says possible values, so you need both roots. Remember: find the vector, square its components, set equal to the length squared, and solve.
