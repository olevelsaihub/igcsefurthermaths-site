Differentiating twice and proving a relation between y and its derivatives

Idea. In a car, speed is the rate of change of distance, and acceleration is the rate of change of speed. In maths, differentiating twice gives the second derivative, d two y by d x squared. Some functions link to their own derivatives. Take y equals sine two x. Its derivative is two cosine two x. Differentiate again: minus four sine two x. That is minus four y. In an exam the relation is printed. Show that means every step must be on the page: differentiate twice, substitute, and collect until you reach exactly the printed result.

Method. Step one: find d y by d x with the product rule and take out the common factor. Step two: differentiate again. A tidy first derivative makes the second easier. Step three: substitute into the left side. Step four: collect the sine and cosine coefficients. They must give exactly the printed result. The trap: in the second derivative, differentiate the factor and the bracket, not only the bracket.

Worked example. A real question from January twenty twenty. Given y equals e to the three x times sine two x, show that d two y by d x squared, minus six d y by d x, plus thirteen y, equals nought. Eight marks. First derivative, by the product rule. Three e to the three x sine two x, plus e to the three x times two cosine two x. Take out e to the three x. Differentiate again. Three e to the three x times the bracket, plus e to the three x times the bracket's derivative: six cosine two x minus four sine two x. Collect inside the bracket. Sine terms: nine minus four is five. Cosine terms: six plus six is twelve. Now substitute into the left side. The second derivative, minus six times the first derivative, plus thirteen times y. Collect. Sine: five minus eighteen plus thirteen is nought. Cosine: twelve minus twelve is nought. So the whole expression is nought, as required. A tempting wrong route is to differentiate only the bracket. That misses the first product term, so the sines and cosines would not cancel. So the left side equals nought, as required. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is the sign of the derivative of e to the minus t. It is minus e to the minus t, not plus.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty. Given x equals e to the minus t sine two t, show that d two x by d t squared, plus two d x by d t, plus five x, equals nought. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Product rule, then factorise. Differentiate again: minus e to the minus t times the bracket, plus e to the minus t times the bracket's derivative. Substitute. Cosine: minus four plus four is nought. Sine: minus three, minus two, plus five is nought. So the expression is nought, as required. The most common wrong answer uses plus e to the minus t for the derivative. The cosine terms then add to eight, not nought, and the proof fails. Remember: differentiate twice, factorising each time, substitute all three terms, then collect sines and cosines to reach the printed result.
