When is the particle at rest (or at a given velocity)?

Idea. A car stops at a red light. For that moment its speed is zero. Velocity is speed with a direction. A particle is instantaneously at rest when its velocity is zero at one exact moment. Displacement s is where the particle is. Velocity v is how fast s changes. Acceleration a is how fast v changes. To go from s to v, or v to a, we differentiate. Here is a velocity graph. Rest means the velocity is zero, so the graph touches the t axis. These two crossing points are the times when the particle is at rest. These questions ask for the time when the particle is at rest, or has a given velocity. Sometimes they also ask where it is then.

Method. Step one: choose the right expression. Rest is about velocity, so use v, and differentiate first if given s. Step two: set it equal to zero, or to the given velocity. Step three: solve the quadratic. Time cannot be negative, so reject any negative time. Step four: if a position is asked, substitute t into s. The trap is solving the wrong condition. Rest means v equals zero, not s equals zero and not a equals zero.

Worked example. January twenty twenty two. A particle P moves along the x axis. At time t seconds its velocity is three t squared, minus twenty three t, plus thirty. Find the values of t when P is instantaneously at rest. Three marks. Step one: at rest the velocity is zero. So three t squared, minus twenty three t, plus thirty, equals zero. Write this line, because it earns the method mark. Step two: factorise. Three t minus five, times, t minus six, equals zero. Multiply it out to check: three t squared, minus eighteen t, minus five t, plus thirty. Step three: each bracket can be zero. Three t minus five is zero gives t equals five over three. T minus six is zero gives t equals six. Both answers are positive, so both are allowed. The graph meets the t axis at those two times. A tempting wrong route is to set the acceleration to zero. That gives t equals twenty three over six, but that is when a is zero, not when the particle is at rest. So P is at rest when t equals five thirds seconds, and when t equals six seconds. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is solving a bracket. From three t minus five equals zero, some students write t equals three fifths. Add five first, then divide by three: t equals five over three.

Your turn. Now you try, from November twenty twenty. The displacement of P from O is three, plus eight t, plus t squared, minus one third t cubed. Find the distance of P from O when P is at rest. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Rest is about velocity, so differentiate s. Velocity is eight, plus two t, minus t squared. Set it to zero, which gives t squared, minus two t, minus eight, equals zero. That is t minus four, times, t plus two. So t is four or minus two. Time cannot be negative, so t equals four seconds. Now the question asks for a distance, so substitute four into s: three, plus thirty two, plus sixteen, minus sixty four over three. The distance is eighty nine over three metres. The most common wrong answer is t equals four. That is the time, not the distance, so it loses marks. Remember: choose v, set it to the value, solve, reject negative times, then use s if asked.
