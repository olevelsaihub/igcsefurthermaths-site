Small changes and percentage changes

Idea. Zoom in far enough on a smooth curve and it looks like its tangent. Gradient is rise over run, so the rise is about the gradient times the run. A small change in y is about d y by d x times the small change in x. The rule: delta y, a small change in y, is about d y by d x times delta x, a small change in x. For y equals x squared at x equals two, d y by d x is four. A step of nought point nought one in x gives about nought point nought four in y. Percentage change is the change divided by the original, times one hundred. If x goes up by three per cent, the small change in x is nought point nought three x, not three. So these questions ask you to estimate a change or a percentage change. Differentiate, write the small change in x, use the rule, and divide by y for a percentage.

Method. Step one: differentiate with respect to the variable that changes. Step two: write the small change in x as a percentage of x, not the bare number. Step three: delta y is d y by d x times delta x. Step four: for a percentage, divide by y and multiply by one hundred. The trap is forgetting to divide by y.

Worked example. November twenty twenty one. y equals x times e to the minus two x. The value of x increases by three per cent. Estimate the percentage change in y, in terms of x, in the form a times b minus c x. Six marks. Step one: differentiate the product. The derivative is e to the minus two x, plus x times minus two e to the minus two x. Take out e to the minus two x: one minus two x. Step two: a three per cent increase means delta x is nought point nought three x. A tempting wrong route is to use three. That is three units, not three per cent. Step three: delta y is e to the minus two x, times one minus two x, times nought point nought three x. Step four: divide delta y by y, and multiply by one hundred. The x and the e to the minus two x both cancel. Nought point nought three times one hundred is three. The estimate is three times one minus two x, per cent. So a is three, b is one, c is two. Common mistake. A common mistake is forgetting to divide by y. Then you have the small change in y, not the percentage change.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty nineteen. A circle has radius three r and area A. Given that r increases by nought point nought five per cent, estimate the percentage increase in A. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The area is pi times three r, squared, which is nine pi r squared. Differentiate: eighteen pi r. The small change in r is nought point zero zero zero five r. Delta A is eighteen pi r times nought point zero zero zero five r, which is nought point zero zero nine pi r squared. Divide by nine pi r squared, times one hundred: nought point one per cent. The most common wrong answer uses nought point nought five r for delta r. That gives ten per cent. The percentage must be divided by one hundred. Remember: differentiate, write delta x as a fraction of x, use delta y equals d y by d x times delta x, then divide by y.
