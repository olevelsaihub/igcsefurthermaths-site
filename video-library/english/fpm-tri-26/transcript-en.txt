Idea. A shop bill is price times quantity. If the bill is zero, the price is zero or the quantity is zero. In maths too: if two things multiply to give zero, one of them is zero. Factorising means writing an expression as a product. Look for a common factor, a part in every term. In two sine y cosine y plus sine y, sine y is common. Tan is sine over cosine, so it is undefined when cosine is zero. That means at ninety degrees and two hundred and seventy degrees. Those angles can never be solutions. This question type gives a trig equation with a common factor. You factorise, solve each factor, and keep every solution that is allowed.

Method. Step one: write tan as sine over cosine, or use the given identity, so every term has a common factor. Step two: factorise. Do not divide, because dividing loses solutions. Step three: set each factor equal to zero and solve each simple equation in the interval. Step four: combine all the solutions, and remove any angle where tan is undefined. The trap is dividing both sides by the common factor. It looks neat, but it loses the solutions where that factor is zero.
