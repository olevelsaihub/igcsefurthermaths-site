Estimating an integral using a series expansion

Idea. Suppose you want the area of a curvy lake. Hard to do exactly. But an easy shape gives a good estimate. An estimate is a close answer, not an exact one. A series expansion writes a function as a polynomial: a number, plus a number times x, plus a number times x squared, and so on. Near x equals nought it matches the curve. The series only works for small x. Here, the size of three x over two must be less than one, so x is between minus two thirds and two thirds. Our limit, nought point five, is inside. A polynomial is easy to integrate. Raise each power of x by one and divide by the new power. These questions say hence estimate. Use the series from an earlier part, integrate it between limits, and give the answer to the accuracy stated.

Method. Step one: take the series from the earlier part. Step two: integrate it term by term. No plus c is needed, because we have limits. Step three: put in the upper limit, then the lower limit, and subtract. Step four: round only at the very end, to the accuracy asked. The trap is rounding too early. Rounding each term first shifts the final answer.

Worked example. June twenty twenty two. f of x is one plus x, over two plus three x. Part c gives its series up to x cubed. Part d: estimate the integral from nought to nought point five of f of x, to four decimal places. Four marks. Step one: the series from part c. It is a half, minus a quarter x, plus three eighths x squared, minus nine sixteenths x cubed. Step two: integrate each term. A half gives x over two. Minus a quarter x gives minus x squared over eight. Three eighths x squared gives x cubed over eight. The last gives minus nine x to the four over sixty four. Step three: put in nought point five. The terms add to nought point two two five five eight six. The lower limit gives nought. A tempting route is to round each term to two decimal places. That gives nought point two three, which is wrong. Keep every digit, and round once at the end. So the estimate is nought point two two five six, to four decimal places. Common mistake. A common mistake is dividing by the wrong number. Minus a quarter x integrates to minus x squared over eight, not minus a quarter x squared.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty three. f of x is one plus four x, over three plus x, all cubed. Find the series up to x squared, then integrate it from nought to nought point two, to five significant figures. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Take out three cubed, which is twenty seven. The bracket expands to one minus x plus two thirds x squared. Multiply by one plus four x, then divide by twenty seven. Integrate: x over twenty seven, plus x squared over eighteen, minus ten x cubed over two forty three. At nought point two, that is nought point zero zero nine three zero zero four. The common wrong answer forgets the twenty seventh. Writing three plus x as one plus x over three makes every coefficient twenty seven times too big. Remember: take the series, integrate term by term, use the limits, then round once.
