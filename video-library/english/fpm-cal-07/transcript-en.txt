Idea. A curve can have letters like p and q standing for numbers you must find. Each clue gives one equation, so two letters need two clues. A stationary point is where the curve is flat for a moment: the top of a hill or the bottom of a valley. The gradient is zero there. So d y by d x equals nought at that x. A point on the curve is another clue. If the curve passes through three, minus five, then putting x equals three must give y equals minus five. That is a second equation. So this type asks for the values of letters in a curve. Read each clue, decide whether it is a gradient or a point, and write one equation for each.

Method. Step one: differentiate, keeping the letters. Step two: put the given x into d y by d x and set it equal to the known gradient, which is zero for a stationary point. Step three: put the known point into the curve. Step four: solve the equations. The trap is putting the point's coordinates into d y by d x, or setting y to zero instead of d y by d x.

Worked example. A real question from November twenty twenty one. The curve y equals two x squared plus p x plus q has a stationary point at three, minus five. p and q are integers. Show that p is minus twelve, and find q. Four marks. Step one: differentiate, treating p and q as constants. The derivative of two x squared is four x. The derivative of p x is p. The derivative of q is nought. Step two: the gradient clue. Stationary at x equals three, so d y by d x is zero there. Four times three plus p equals nought. So p is minus twelve, as required. A tempting wrong route is to say the gradient is minus five, because the point is three, minus five. But minus five is the height. The gradient is zero. Step three: the point clue. Put x equals three and y equals minus five into the curve, using p equals minus twelve. Eighteen minus thirty six is minus eighteen. So minus five equals minus eighteen plus q, and q is thirteen. Common mistake. A common mistake is putting the point's coordinates into d y by d x instead of into the curve. The gradient clue uses d y by d x. The point clue uses y.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty one. The curve is y equals a x minus three, over x plus five. The gradient where x is two is eighteen forty ninths. Show that a is three. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Quotient rule. The bottom times a, minus the top times one, over the bottom squared. That simplifies to five a plus three, over x plus five, squared. At x equals two, the bottom is seven squared, which is forty nine. So five a plus three, over forty nine, equals eighteen over forty nine. Five a plus three is eighteen, so a is three. The most common wrong answer sets y equal to eighteen forty ninths. But that number is a gradient, not a height. The clue belongs in d y by d x. Remember: differentiate with the letters, use the gradient clue in d y by d x, use a point clue in y, then solve.
