Idea. A drinks company wants a can that holds a fixed amount of juice with as little metal as possible. To optimise means to find the largest or smallest value of something. Here, the metal is the surface area. The surface area depends on two letters, r and h. To differentiate we need one. So we use the fixed volume to write h using r, and substitute. That is eliminating a variable. Cylinder volume is pi r squared times h. A closed cylinder has two circular ends and a curved side. Unrolled, the side is a rectangle, two pi r by h. These questions say show that S equals a given formula. The answer is printed, so show every line, ending exactly at the printed result.

Method. Step one: write the formula for the fixed amount, and the formula for the target, using both letters. Step two: rearrange the fixed one to make the unwanted letter the subject. Step three: substitute. Step four: simplify to the printed result. The trap is a missing face: a closed cylinder has two circles.

Worked example. January twenty twenty two. A solid cylinder has radius r and height h. Its volume is ninety pi and its total surface area is S. Show that S equals two pi r squared, plus one hundred and eighty pi over r. Three marks. Step one: the volume. Pi r squared times h equals ninety pi. Divide by pi r squared: h is ninety over r squared. Step two: the target. Total surface area is two circles, plus the curved side. Two pi r squared, plus two pi r h. Step three: substitute h equals ninety over r squared. Two pi r squared, plus two pi r times ninety over r squared. Step four: simplify. Two pi r times ninety is one hundred and eighty pi r. Over r squared, one r cancels, leaving one hundred and eighty pi over r. A tempting wrong route is to count only one circular end. That gives pi r squared, not the printed answer. Total surface area needs both ends. So S equals two pi r squared plus one hundred and eighty pi over r, as required. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is an algebra slip at the end: two pi r times ninety over r squared becomes one hundred and eighty pi over r squared. One r cancels, so the answer is over r.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty five. A solid cylinder has total surface area seven hundred pi. Show that the volume V is pi r times three hundred and fifty minus r squared. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. The surface area is fixed: two pi r squared plus two pi r h equals seven hundred pi. Divide by two pi: r squared plus r h equals three hundred and fifty. So h is three hundred and fifty minus r squared, over r. The target is the volume: pi r squared times h. Substitute: pi r squared times, three hundred and fifty minus r squared, over r. One r cancels. That is pi r times three hundred and fifty minus r squared. The most common wrong answer uses only one circle in the surface area. Then h comes out differently, and the printed result cannot be reached. Count every face. Remember: fixed formula, rearrange, substitute into the target, simplify to the printed result.
