Idea. Money in a bank can double every year. After x years you have two to the power x. When the unknown is in the power, it is an exponential equation. The special number e is about two point seven one eight. L N is the natural log key, the log to base e. L N of e to the k is just k. It undoes the power. To solve e to the two x equals four, take L N of both sides. Two x equals L N four, so x is L N two. An equation can hide a quadratic. E to the four x is e to the two x, squared. Let u be e to the two x. An exponential is always positive, so u is never zero or negative. In the exam you may meet two curves and an exact answer. Exact means an answer with L N in it, not a decimal.

Method. Step one: for two curves, set the equations equal, and multiply to clear any negative power. Step two: let u be the exponential, and solve the quadratic. Step three: reject any u that is zero or negative. Step four: take L N and simplify. The trap: L N of five plus four is not L N five plus L N four.

Worked example. November twenty twenty. Curve one is y equals five e to the minus two x plus four. Curve two is y equals e to the two x. They meet at A. Find the exact coordinates of A. Four marks. Step one: at A the y values are equal. Set the equations equal. Step two: multiply every term by e to the two x. This gives five, plus four e to the two x, equals e to the four x. Step three: let u be e to the two x. Then u squared minus four u minus five equals zero. This factorises as u minus five, times u plus one. Step four: u is five or minus one. But e to a power is always positive, so reject minus one. Then two x is L N five, so x is a half L N five. Step five: y is e to the two x, which is five. So A is a half L N five, five. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme. A tempting wrong route is taking L N of both sides straight away. L N of a sum cannot be split. Clear the negative power first.

Common mistake. A common mistake is keeping u equals minus one. You cannot take the log of a negative number. Reject it.

Your turn. Now you try, from June twenty twenty two. Two curves meet at the origin O and at a point A. Show the hidden quadratic, then show that x at A is a third of L N nine. Here is Figure two: curve C and curve D, crossing at O and at A. Write your answer, then choose Continue to Answer.

Answer. Part one. At A the y values are equal. Multiply every term by e to the three x, then collect the terms on one side. Part two. Let u be e to the three x. Then u is one or nine. u equals one gives x equals nought, which is the origin. So at A, e to the three x is nine, and x is a third of L N nine. The most common wrong answer forgets to multiply the one and the nine by e to the three x. Then the printed quadratic never appears. Remember: match bases or take L N, clear negative powers, let u, reject u not above zero.
