Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics · 4PM1

Where lines cross: two routes and comparing coefficients

Finding the position of a point where two lines cross, or a point fixed by a collinear or parallel condition, by writing its vector in two ways with unknown scalars and comparing coefficients of the two non-parallel base vectors.

Type 7 7 Vectors FPM-VEC-07

★★★★★ · 17 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Very Difficult.

'Using a vector method, find $AX:XN$' (or find $\overrightarrow{OP}$, or the value of $\lambda$ or $k$), usually after earlier parts that found side vectors; the point is where two lines cross, or is fixed by a 'straight line' or collinear condition.

Knowledge card

Where lines cross: two routes and comparing coefficients

Written
  • If $X$ lies on the line $AN$, then $\overrightarrow{AX}=\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}$ for some number $\lambda$ (between 0 and 1 if $X$ is between $A$ and $N$).
  • If $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel and $\alpha_1\mathbf{a}+\beta_1\mathbf{b}=\alpha_2\mathbf{a}+\beta_2\mathbf{b}$, then $\alpha_1=\alpha_2$ and $\beta_1=\beta_2$.
  • Use a different letter for each line the point lies on (for example $\lambda$ and $\mu$).
  • If $\overrightarrow{AX}=\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}$ then $AX:XN=\lambda:(1-\lambda)$.
  • Writing the same vector by two routes and comparing coefficients gives two simultaneous equations.
  • The final answer may be a ratio, a scalar or a position vector; read which one is wanted.

Before you begin

  • Writing vectors round a shape (FPM-VEC-04).
  • Solving simultaneous linear equations.
  • Ratio and fractions of a line.
  • Collinearity as a scalar multiple (FPM-VEC-06).
1

The method

  1. Give the point an unknown scalar on each line it lies on, e.g. $\overrightarrow{AX}=\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}$ and $\overrightarrow{BX}=\mu\overrightarrow{BM}$.

    Why: Each scalar says how far along its line the point is.

  2. Write the position vector of the point by two routes, e.g. $\overrightarrow{OX}=\overrightarrow{OA}+\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}$ and $\overrightarrow{OX}=\overrightarrow{OB}+\mu\overrightarrow{BM}$.

    Why: Both expressions describe the same point.

  3. Expand each into the form (number)$\mathbf{a}$ + (number)$\mathbf{b}$.

    Why: Coefficients can only be compared once the expressions are collected.

  4. Set the two expressions equal and compare the coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ and of $\mathbf{b}$.

    Why: Because $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are not parallel, this gives two equations.

  5. Solve the simultaneous equations for the scalars.

    Why: These fix exactly where the point is.

  6. Turn the scalar into what was asked: a ratio such as $\lambda:(1-\lambda)$, or substitute back for the vector.

    Why: The final accuracy mark is for the answer in the requested form.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using the same letter for both scalars, which forces a wrong answer.
  • ✗
    Putting a scalar on a vector the point does not lie on.
  • ✗
    Not collecting terms before comparing coefficients, or comparing only one coefficient.
  • ✗
    Converting $\lambda$ into the wrong ratio, e.g. giving $\lambda:1$ instead of $\lambda:(1-\lambda)$.
  • ✗
    Algebra slips when solving the simultaneous equations.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Marks build up through the solution: method marks for each correct expression containing a scalar, a method mark for equating coefficients of both base vectors, a method mark for solving, then accuracy marks for the scalar(s) and the final ratio or vector. A correct final answer without a vector method earns very little.

Where lines cross: two routes and comparing coefficients · January 2023 · Paper 1 · Question 11 · part (b) · 7 marks

Triangle $OAB$ has $\overrightarrow{OA}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}=\mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $OA$, and $N$ is the point on $OB$ such that $ON:NB=3:1$. The lines $AN$ and $BM$ intersect at $X$. (From part (a): $\overrightarrow{AN}=\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{BM}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}$.) (b) Using a vector method, find $AX:XN$. (7 marks)

Worked explanation

  1. Let $\overrightarrow{AX}=\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}$ and $\overrightarrow{BX}=\mu\overrightarrow{BM}$.
  2. Route 1: $\overrightarrow{OX}=\overrightarrow{OA}+\lambda\overrightarrow{AN}=\mathbf{a}+\lambda\left(\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a}\right)=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{a}+\frac{3\lambda}{4}\mathbf{b}$
  3. Route 2: $\overrightarrow{OX}=\overrightarrow{OB}+\mu\overrightarrow{BM}=\mathbf{b}+\mu\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\right)=\frac{\mu}{2}\mathbf{a}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{b}$
  4. Compare $\mathbf{a}$: $1-\lambda=\frac{\mu}{2}\Rightarrow \mu=2-2\lambda$
  5. Compare $\mathbf{b}$: $\frac{3\lambda}{4}=1-\mu$
  6. Substitute: $\frac{3\lambda}{4}=1-(2-2\lambda)=2\lambda-1\Rightarrow 1=\frac{5\lambda}{4}\Rightarrow \lambda=\frac{4}{5}$ (and $\mu=\frac{2}{5}$)
  7. $\overrightarrow{AX}=\frac{4}{5}\overrightarrow{AN}$, so $AX:XN=\frac{4}{5}:\frac{1}{5}=4:1$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$AX:XN=4:1$

How the marks are earned

Method marks for each expression for $\overrightarrow{OX}$ containing a scalar, a method mark for comparing the coefficients of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ to get two equations, a method mark for solving them, accuracy marks for $\lambda=\frac{4}{5}$ (and $\mu$), and an accuracy mark for the ratio $4:1$.

Calculation check

  • round(1-4 ÷ 5,10) = 0.2
  • round((2 ÷ 5) ÷ 2,10) = 0.2
  • round(3 × (4 ÷ 5) ÷ 4,10) = 0.6
  • round(1-2 ÷ 5,10) = 0.6
  • round((4 ÷ 5) ÷ (1-4 ÷ 5),10) = 4.0
