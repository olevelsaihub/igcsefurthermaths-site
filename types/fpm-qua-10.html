Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another

Building a quadratic equation, usually with integer coefficients, whose roots are given expressions in alpha and beta, using only the sum and product of the original roots.

Type 6 2 The quadratic function FPM-QUA-10

★★★★★ · 17 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

'Without solving the equation, form a quadratic equation with integer coefficients which has roots ...' followed by expressions in $\alpha$ and $\beta$, or a second equation with unknown coefficients whose roots are built from $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Knowledge card

Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another

Written
  • A quadratic with roots $r$ and $s$ is $x^2-(r+s)x+rs=0$
  • $\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}$ and $\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}$ for the original equation
  • Find the sum and the product of the new roots in terms of $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ (and $\alpha-\beta$ if needed)
  • Multiply through by a common denominator to get integer coefficients
  • If the new equation is given as $px^2+qx+r=0$, compare: sum $=-\frac{q}{p}$, product $=\frac{r}{p}$

Before you begin

  • Sum and product of roots and the standard identities (FPM-QUA-07)
  • Difference of the roots (FPM-QUA-08)
  • Combining algebraic fractions
1

The method

  1. Write down $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$.

    Why: All the new expressions are built from them.

  2. Find the sum of the new roots and simplify it in terms of the sum and product (and difference if given).

    Why: This gives the coefficient of $x$.

  3. Find the product of the new roots and simplify it the same way.

    Why: This gives the constant term.

  4. Write $x^2-(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0$.

    Why: This is the quadratic whose roots have that sum and product.

  5. Multiply through to clear fractions, or give the form asked for.

    Why: The question usually demands integer coefficients or a stated form.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing $x^2+(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0$ (wrong sign of the $x$ term)
  • ✗
    Not scaling to integer coefficients when the question asks for them
  • ✗
    Expanding the product of the new roots wrongly
  • ✗
    Giving an expression without '$=0$', so it is not an equation

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Form14Show the new sum and new product, then give the full equation with '$=0$' in the form asked (integer coefficients or $x^2+px+q=0$).
Hence4Use earlier results (such as $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ or $\alpha-\beta$) for the new sum and product, then write the equation.
Find3Find the new sum and product, compare with the given equation and state each unknown coefficient.
Show that1Show the new sum (or product) in terms of the original roots and compare with the coefficient to reach the given value exactly.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark and an accuracy mark for each of the new sum and the new product (method for a correct expression in $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$, accuracy for its value), then a final mark for the complete equation in the required form.

Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 6 · part (c) · 4 marks

The quadratic equation $x^2-7kx+k^2=0$, where $k$ is a positive constant, has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ where $\alpha>\beta$. Given that $\alpha-\beta=3k\sqrt{5}$ (part (b)), hence form a quadratic equation with roots $\alpha+1$ and $\beta-1$. Give your equation in the form $x^2+px+q=0$ where $p$ and $q$ should be given in terms of $k$. (4 marks)

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\alpha+\beta=7k$, $\alpha\beta=k^2$, and $\alpha-\beta=3\sqrt{5}k$ (since $(\alpha-\beta)^2=49k^2-4k^2=45k^2$)
  2. Sum of new roots: $(\alpha+1)+(\beta-1)=\alpha+\beta=7k$
  3. Product: $(\alpha+1)(\beta-1)=\alpha\beta-\alpha+\beta-1=\alpha\beta-(\alpha-\beta)-1=k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$
  4. Equation: $x^2-7kx+k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1=0$
  5. Check with $k=1$: $\alpha=\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}$, $\beta=\frac{7-3\sqrt{5}}{2}$, so $(\alpha+1)(\beta-1)=-3\sqrt{5}$, matching $1-3\sqrt{5}-1$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x^2-7kx+\left(k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1\right)=0$, so $p=-7k$ and $q=k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$

How the marks are earned

A mark for the new sum $7k$, a method mark for expanding the product and replacing $\beta-\alpha$ by $-3\sqrt{5}k$, an accuracy mark for $k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$, and a final mark for the equation in the form $x^2+px+q=0$.

Calculation check

  • round(((7+45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2+1) × ((7-45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-1)-(1-3 × 5 × × 0.5-1),9) = 0
  • round((7+45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-(7-45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-3 × 5 × × 0.5,9) = 0
  • 7 × × 2-4 × 1 = 45
