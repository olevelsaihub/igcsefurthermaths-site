Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another

Building a quadratic equation, usually with integer coefficients, whose roots are given expressions in alpha and beta, using only the sum and product of the original roots.

Type 6 2 The quadratic function FPM-QUA-10

★★★★★ · 17 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

'Without solving the equation, form a quadratic equation with integer coefficients which has roots ...' followed by expressions in $\alpha$ and $\beta$, or a second equation with unknown coefficients whose roots are built from $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Knowledge card

Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another

Calculation

The sum and product of the new roots are built from the original $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$. Then write $x^2-(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0$ and clear any fractions.

1

Build the new equation step by step

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 steps to find

Work out the new sum and product.

  1. Sum over product
  2. Product is the reciprocal
  3. Clear the fractions

Sum over product

Working
$\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha\beta}$

Product is the reciprocal

Trap
$\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}$ is not $\alpha\beta$: take the reciprocal.

Clear the fractions

Scores
Multiplying by $3$ gives $3x^2-5x+1=0$, with integer coefficients.
0 of 3 found

3 steps to find

Shifting each root by 2 changes the sum and the product.

  1. Sum rises by 4
  2. Expand the product
  3. Read off the new equation

Sum rises by 4

Why
The shift adds 2 twice, so the sum shifts by 4.

Expand the product

Working
$(\alpha+2)(\beta+2)=\alpha\beta+2(\alpha+\beta)+4$.

Read off the new equation

Check
The $x$ coefficient is minus the sum, $-9$; the constant is the product, $17$.
0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

$x^2-(\text{sum of new roots})\,x+(\text{product of new roots})=0$

Compare with $px^2+qx+r=0$: sum $=-\frac{q}{p}$ and product $=\frac{r}{p}$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Writing x^2 + (sum)x + (product), which flips the sign of the x term
  • Leaving fractions when integer coefficients are asked for

Try itForm the equation with integer coefficients whose roots are $\alpha+1$ and $\beta+1$, for $x^2+4x+1=0$.

  1. Sum $=(\alpha+\beta)+2=-4+2=-2$
  2. Product $=\alpha\beta+(\alpha+\beta)+1=1-4+1=-2$, so $x^2+2x-2=0$

5

The method

  1. Original sum and product

    Write $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ from the coefficients.

  2. New sum

    Simplify the sum of the new roots in terms of $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$.

  3. New product

    Simplify the product of the new roots the same way.

  4. Write the equation

    Use $x^2-(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0$.

  5. Clear fractions

    Multiply through to integer coefficients if the question asks for them.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign of the x term

    The middle term is minus the sum, so $x^2+(\text{sum})x$ is wrong.

  • ✗
    Expanding the product

    Expand $(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)$ fully, then substitute.

What a complete response includes

A method mark and an accuracy mark for each of the new sum and the new product (method for a correct expression in $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$, accuracy for its value), then a final mark for the complete equation in the required form.

Forming a new quadratic from the roots of another · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 6 · part (c) · 4 marks

The quadratic equation $x^2-7kx+k^2=0$, where $k$ is a positive constant, has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ where $\alpha>\beta$. Given that $\alpha-\beta=3k\sqrt{5}$ (part (b)), hence form a quadratic equation with roots $\alpha+1$ and $\beta-1$. Give your equation in the form $x^2+px+q=0$ where $p$ and $q$ should be given in terms of $k$. (4 marks)

Worked explanation

  1. $\alpha+\beta=7k$, $\alpha\beta=k^2$, and $\alpha-\beta=3\sqrt{5}k$ (since $(\alpha-\beta)^2=49k^2-4k^2=45k^2$)
  2. Sum of new roots: $(\alpha+1)+(\beta-1)=\alpha+\beta=7k$
  3. Product: $(\alpha+1)(\beta-1)=\alpha\beta-\alpha+\beta-1=\alpha\beta-(\alpha-\beta)-1=k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$
  4. Equation: $x^2-7kx+k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1=0$
  5. Check with $k=1$: $\alpha=\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}$, $\beta=\frac{7-3\sqrt{5}}{2}$, so $(\alpha+1)(\beta-1)=-3\sqrt{5}$, matching $1-3\sqrt{5}-1$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x^2-7kx+\left(k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1\right)=0$, so $p=-7k$ and $q=k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$

How the marks are earned

A mark for the new sum $7k$, a method mark for expanding the product and replacing $\beta-\alpha$ by $-3\sqrt{5}k$, an accuracy mark for $k^2-3\sqrt{5}k-1$, and a final mark for the equation in the form $x^2+px+q=0$.

Calculation check

  • round(((7+45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2+1) × ((7-45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-1)-(1-3 × 5 × × 0.5-1),9) = 0
  • round((7+45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-(7-45 × × 0.5) ÷ 2-3 × 5 × × 0.5,9) = 0
  • 7 × × 2-4 × 1 = 45
