Estimating an integral using a series expansion

Calculation

When a function cannot be integrated directly, use its series expansion, integrate the polynomial term by term, and keep every limit inside the range of validity.

3 steps to find

Integrate each term of the series.

  1. Integrate each power
  2. Lower limit 0 gives 0
  3. Round only at the end

Integrate each power

Working
Raise each power by one: $x^2$ gives $\frac{x^3}{3}$ before its coefficient.

Lower limit 0 gives 0

Why
At $x = 0$ every term is 0, so only the upper limit is needed.

Round only at the end

Scores

The calculation

$(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \cdots$, valid for $|x| < 1$

Integrate the polynomial term by term, then substitute both limits.

  • Integrating the original function, not the series
  • Rounding early: keep exact fractions until the last step

Try itExpand $(1 + 2x)^{-1}$ to $x^2$ and estimate $\int_0^{0.1}(1 + 2x)^{-1}\,dx$ to 3 d.p.

  1. $(1 + 2x)^{-1} = 1 - 2x + 4x^2$
  2. $\int_0^{0.1}\left(1 - 2x + 4x^2\right)dx = 0.1 - 0.01 + 0.00133 = 0.0913 \approx 0.091$

5

The method

  1. Write the series

    Expand the function up to the power asked.

  2. Integrate term by term

    Raise each power by one and divide by the new power.

  3. Substitute the limits

    Upper limit minus lower limit, keeping fractions.

  4. Round at the end

    Give the answer to the decimal places asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Series for the answer

    The series is for the function, which you then integrate.

  • ✗
    Limits outside the range

    Check $|x| < 1$ before trusting the estimate.

Estimating an integral using a series expansion · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 5 · part (d) · 4 marks

Given that $(2 + 3x)^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \frac{3}{2}x\right)^{-1}$ and $f(x) = \frac{1 + x}{2 + 3x}$, use the series expansion of $f(x)$ up to the term in $x^3$ and algebraic integration to obtain an estimate, to 4 decimal places, of $\int_0^{0.5} f(x)\,dx$.

Worked explanation

  1. $(2+3x)^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{3}{2}x + \frac{9}{4}x^2 - \frac{27}{8}x^3 + \ldots\right) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{9}{8}x^2 - \frac{27}{16}x^3 + \ldots$
  2. $f(x) = (1 + x)\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{9}{8}x^2 - \frac{27}{16}x^3\right) \approx \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}x + \frac{3}{8}x^2 - \frac{9}{16}x^3$
  3. $\int_0^{0.5}\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}x + \frac{3}{8}x^2 - \frac{9}{16}x^3\right)dx = \left[\frac{x}{2} - \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{x^3}{8} - \frac{9x^4}{64}\right]_0^{0.5}$
  4. $= 0.25 - 0.03125 + 0.015625 - 0.0087890625 - 0 = 0.2255859375$
  5. $\approx 0.2256$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\int_0^{0.5} f(x)\,dx \approx 0.2256$ (4 d.p.)

How the marks are earned

A method mark for integrating the series term by term (powers raised by one), accuracy for the integrated terms, a method mark for substituting the limit 0.5, and accuracy for 0.2256.

Calculation check

  • 0.5 ÷ 2 - 0.5 × × 2 ÷ 8 + 0.5 × × 3 ÷ 8 - 9 × 0.5 × × 4 ÷ 64 = 0.2255859375
