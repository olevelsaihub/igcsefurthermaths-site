Where a line meets a curve (simultaneous equations)

Solving one linear and one non-linear equation together, either stated as algebra or as a line and a curve (or two curves) on a diagram.

Type 6 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-13

★★★★★ · 15 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

'Use algebra to solve the simultaneous equations' with one linear and one quadratic (or xy) equation, or 'find the coordinates of the points of intersection of C and l', often before a sketch or an area/volume calculation.

5

The method

  1. Equate

    Write the line equal to the curve, or substitute y.

  2. Rearrange

    Make one side zero to get a quadratic.

  3. Solve

    Factorise or use the formula.

  4. Pair

    Put each x into the linear equation to find its y.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong pair

    Find y from the linear equation for each x.

  • ✗
    Missing a point

    A quadratic can give two points; give both.

The curve $C$ has equation $y=8x^2+10x-3$. The straight line $l$ has equation $y=2x+13$. Use algebra to find the coordinates of the two points of intersection of $C$ and $l$.

Worked explanation

  1. At the intersections $8x^2+10x-3=2x+13$.
  2. $8x^2+8x-16=0$
  3. $x^2+x-2=0$ (divide by $8$)
  4. $(x+2)(x-1)=0 \Rightarrow x=-2$ or $x=1$
  5. $x=-2$: $y=2(-2)+13=9$
  6. $x=1$: $y=2(1)+13=15$
  7. Check on $C$: $8(4)-20-3=9$ and $8+10-3=15$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$(-2,\ 9)$ and $(1,\ 15)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for equating and rearranging to a three-term quadratic, a method mark for solving it, an accuracy mark for $x=-2$ and $x=1$, and an accuracy mark for both correct points.

Calculation check

  • 8 × (-2) × × 2+10 × (-2)-3 = 9
  • 2 × (-2)+13 = 9
  • 8 × 1 × × 2+10 × 1-3 = 15
  • 2 × 1+13 = 15
