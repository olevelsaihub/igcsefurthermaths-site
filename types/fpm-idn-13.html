Where a line meets a curve (simultaneous equations)

Solving one linear and one non-linear equation together, either stated as algebra or as a line and a curve (or two curves) on a diagram.

Knowledge card

Where a line meets a curve (simultaneous equations)

Written
  • The intersection points of two graphs satisfy both equations at once.
  • With $y=$ expression in both, set the expressions equal; otherwise make $x$ or $y$ the subject of the linear equation and substitute.
  • Substituting a linear equation into a quadratic gives a quadratic equation in one variable.
  • Each solution for x must be paired with its own y, found from the linear equation.
  • A repeated root (discriminant 0) means the line is a tangent to the curve.

Before you begin

  • Rearranging linear equations
  • Solving quadratic equations
  • Substitution
1

The method

  1. Make one variable the subject of the linear equation, or equate the two expressions for y.

    Why: This removes one variable so you have one equation in one unknown.

  2. Substitute and rearrange to a quadratic equal to zero.

    Why: A quadratic in standard form can be factorised or solved with the formula.

  3. Solve the quadratic.

    Why: This gives the x (or y) values of the intersection points.

  4. Substitute each value back into the linear equation to get the other coordinate.

    Why: The linear equation is simplest and avoids wrong pairings.

  5. Write the answers as coordinate pairs.

    Why: When points are asked for, x-values alone do not earn the final mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Pairing x and y values wrongly.
  • ✗
    Giving only the x-coordinates when the points are asked for.
  • ✗
    Sign or expansion errors when substituting, e.g. squaring a bracket wrongly.
  • ✗
    Substituting into the quadratic for y instead of the linear equation and making extra arithmetic errors.
  • ✗
    Dividing through by a variable and losing a solution.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Solve2Show the substitution, the quadratic and its solution, then give each solution as a matched pair, e.g. x = 4, y = 9 and x = 9, y = 4.
Find13Give the coordinates (or the x-coordinates if that is all that is asked), with the algebraic working shown.
Hence1Use the earlier result (a relation or known solution) in the substitution, then solve and apply any given condition.
Use1'Use algebra' means a full algebraic method must be shown; reading from a graph is not accepted.
Show that2Solve the equations together and reach the printed point exactly, showing each line.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for substituting to get one equation in one variable, a method mark for solving the resulting quadratic, and accuracy marks for the values, usually one for both x-values and one for the matching y-values or pairs.

Where a line meets a curve (simultaneous equations) · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (d) · 4 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y=8x^2+10x-3$. The straight line $l$ has equation $y=2x+13$. Use algebra to find the coordinates of the two points of intersection of $C$ and $l$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. At the intersections $8x^2+10x-3=2x+13$.
  2. $8x^2+8x-16=0$
  3. $x^2+x-2=0$ (divide by $8$)
  4. $(x+2)(x-1)=0 \Rightarrow x=-2$ or $x=1$
  5. $x=-2$: $y=2(-2)+13=9$
  6. $x=1$: $y=2(1)+13=15$
  7. Check on $C$: $8(4)-20-3=9$ and $8+10-3=15$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$(-2,\ 9)$ and $(1,\ 15)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for equating and rearranging to a three-term quadratic, a method mark for solving it, an accuracy mark for $x=-2$ and $x=1$, and an accuracy mark for both correct points.

Calculation check

  • 8 × (-2) × × 2+10 × (-2)-3 = 9
  • 2 × (-2)+13 = 9
  • 8 × 1 × × 2+10 × 1-3 = 15
  • 2 × 1+13 = 15
