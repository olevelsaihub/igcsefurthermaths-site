Knowledge card

Sketching curves and where they cross the axes

Written
  • A curve crosses the $y$-axis where $x = 0$ and the $x$-axis where $y = 0$
  • $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a > 0$ is a U-shape; its turning point comes from completing the square, $a(x+p)^2 + q$ has minimum $(-p, q)$
  • A cubic $y = (x-a)(x-b)(x-c)$ with positive $x^3$ coefficient rises from bottom left to top right through its three roots
  • $y = a^x$ (with $a > 1$) passes through $(0, 1)$ and has asymptote $y = 0$; $y = \log_b x$ passes through $(1, 0)$ and has asymptote $x = 0$
  • A log curve $y = \ln(x + k)$ has vertical asymptote $x = -k$; an exponential $y = e^x + k$ has horizontal asymptote $y = k$
  • A sketch shows the correct shape and labels the key points with coordinates; it need not be to scale

Before you begin

  • Factorising quadratics and the quadratic formula
  • Completing the square
  • Shapes of exponential and log graphs
  • Solving simultaneous equations (line and curve)
1

The method

  1. Put $x = 0$ to get the $y$-intercept.

    Why: It is the easiest key point and fixes where the curve cuts the vertical axis.

  2. Put $y = 0$ and solve (factorise, use the formula, or turn a log or exponential equation round) to get the $x$-intercepts.

    Why: These are the roots, which the marks usually ask to see labelled.

  3. Find any turning point (completed square) or asymptote (from the log or exponential form).

    Why: These fix the shape and position of the curve.

  4. Draw the correct shape through the points and label every intercept, turning point and asymptote.

    Why: Unlabelled points usually earn no credit.

  5. If a line is asked for too, draw it through its own intercept and the intersection points found earlier.

    Why: The sketch should agree with the algebra already done.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing a log or exponential curve that touches or crosses its asymptote
  • ✗
    Leaving intercepts or the turning point unlabelled
  • ✗
    Turning point in the wrong place, e.g. the parabola's lowest point not between the roots
  • ✗
    Sign slip in an intercept, e.g. giving $x = \frac{3}{2}$ instead of $x = -\frac{3}{2}$
  • ✗
    Drawing the line so that it does not pass through the found intersection points

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Sketch4A clear freehand curve of the right shape with intercepts, turning points and asymptotes labelled with coordinates or equations.
Find3Show the substitution ($x = 0$ or $y = 0$) and the solving, then give the coordinates or $x$-values.
Write down2Give the value or coordinates directly; it should need only a one-step substitution.
Show that2Every step from the given information to the printed result, with no gaps.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Sketch parts give a mark for the correct shape and position and further marks for the labelled intercepts, turning points or asymptotes. Find parts give a method mark for setting $y = 0$ (or $x = 0$) and solving, and an accuracy mark for the values.

Sketching curves and where they cross the axes · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (e) · 2 marks

$f(x) = 8x^2 + 10x - 3$. The curve $C$ has equation $y = f(x)$ and the line $l$ has equation $y = 2x + 13$. Earlier parts found the minimum point, the $x$-intercepts of $C$ and the intersections of $C$ and $l$. Using the same axes and these results, sketch the curve $C$ and the straight line $l$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Minimum: $8x^2 + 10x - 3 = 8\left(x + \frac{5}{8}\right)^2 - \frac{49}{8}$, so the minimum point is $\left(-\frac{5}{8}, -\frac{49}{8}\right)$
  2. $x$-intercepts: $8x^2 + 10x - 3 = (4x - 1)(2x + 3) = 0$, so $x = \frac{1}{4}$ and $x = -\frac{3}{2}$
  3. $y$-intercept of $C$: $x = 0$ gives $y = -3$, so $(0, -3)$
  4. Intersections: $8x^2 + 10x - 3 = 2x + 13 \Rightarrow 8x^2 + 8x - 16 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow (x + 2)(x - 1) = 0$
  5. $x = -2$: $y = 2(-2) + 13 = 9$; $x = 1$: $y = 2(1) + 13 = 15$; so $(-2, 9)$ and $(1, 15)$
  6. Sketch: a U-shaped parabola with lowest point $\left(-\frac{5}{8}, -\frac{49}{8}\right)$, crossing the $x$-axis at $-\frac{3}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$ and the $y$-axis at $-3$
  7. Draw $l$ as a straight line with positive gradient, crossing the $y$-axis at $(0, 13)$ and cutting $C$ at $(-2, 9)$ and $(1, 15)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A U-shaped parabola through $\left(-\frac{3}{2}, 0\right)$, $\left(\frac{1}{4}, 0\right)$ and $(0, -3)$ with minimum $\left(-\frac{5}{8}, -\frac{49}{8}\right)$, and the line $y = 2x + 13$ meeting it at $(-2, 9)$ and $(1, 15)$.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the parabola of the correct shape and position (minimum between the two roots, below the $x$-axis), and one for the line drawn crossing the parabola twice in the right places, with the key points consistent with the earlier answers.

Calculation check

  • 8 × (-5 ÷ 8) × × 2+10 × (-5 ÷ 8)-3 = -6.125
  • -49 ÷ 8 = -6.125
  • 8 × (1 ÷ 4) × × 2+10 × (1 ÷ 4)-3 = 0.0
  • 8 × (-3 ÷ 2) × × 2+10 × (-3 ÷ 2)-3 = 0.0
  • 8 × 1 × × 2+10 × 1-3 = 15
  • 8 × (-2) × × 2+10 × (-2)-3 = 9
