Knowledge card

Area between a curve and the x-axis

Calculation
  • Area between $y = f(x)$ and the $x$-axis from $a$ to $b$ (curve above the axis) is $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
  • An integral over a part below the $x$-axis comes out negative; its area is the size (modulus) of that value.
  • If the curve crosses the axis inside the interval, split the integral at each crossing point and add the sizes.
  • Find crossing points by solving $f(x) = 0$.
  • $\int \sin ax\,dx = -\frac{1}{a}\cos ax$; $\int \cos ax\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\sin ax$.

Before you begin

  • Integrating polynomials and trig functions.
  • Solving $f(x) = 0$ (factorising, trig equations).
  • Exact trig values.
1

The method

  1. Find where the curve crosses the $x$-axis.

    Why: These are the limits and the split points.

  2. Integrate over each section separately.

    Why: Integrating across a crossing would let positive and negative parts cancel.

  3. Take the size of any negative result.

    Why: Area is always positive.

  4. Add the areas and give the answer in the required form.

    Why: The total is the sum of the separate pieces.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Integrating straight across a root, so the pieces partly cancel.
  • ✗
    Keeping a negative sign on an area below the axis.
  • ✗
    Wrong crossing points from solving the trig equation in the wrong interval.
  • ✗
    Sign slips when integrating $\sin$ (it gives $-\cos$).

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Area between a curve and the x-axis · November 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 10 · part (d) · 4 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{1}{2} + \sin 3x$, $0 \leqslant x < \frac{2\pi}{3}$. $C$ crosses the $x$-axis at $M\left(\frac{7\pi}{18}, 0\right)$ and at $N$. The finite region bounded by $C$, the $y$-axis and the part of the $x$-axis from $O$ to $N$ is shaded (part of it is above the axis, from $O$ to $M$, and part is below, from $M$ to $N$). Use algebraic integration to find, to 3 significant figures, the total area of the shaded region.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $N$: $\sin 3x = -\frac{1}{2}$ gives $3x = \frac{7\pi}{6}$ or $\frac{11\pi}{6}$, so $x = \frac{7\pi}{18}$ ($M$) and $x = \frac{11\pi}{18}$ ($N$).
  2. $\int\left(\frac{1}{2} + \sin 3x\right)dx = \frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\cos 3x$
  3. Above the axis: $\left[\frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\cos 3x\right]_0^{\frac{7\pi}{18}} = \left(\frac{7\pi}{36} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\right) - \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = 1.2329\ldots$
  4. Below the axis: $\left[\frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{3}\cos 3x\right]_{\frac{7\pi}{18}}^{\frac{11\pi}{18}} = \left(\frac{11\pi}{36} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\right) - \left(\frac{7\pi}{36} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\right) = \frac{\pi}{9} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = -0.2283\ldots$
  5. Total area $= 1.2329\ldots + 0.2283\ldots = 1.4612\ldots$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Total area $\approx 1.46$ (3 s.f.)

How the marks are earned

A method mark for integrating $\frac{1}{2} + \sin 3x$, a method mark for using the limits $0$, $\frac{7\pi}{18}$ and $\frac{11\pi}{18}$ with the region split at $M$, a method mark for adding the size of the negative part, and accuracy for 1.46.

Calculation check

  • round(7 × 3.141592653589793 ÷ 36 + 3 × × 0.5 ÷ 6 + 1 ÷ 3, 4) = 1.2329
  • round(3.141592653589793 ÷ 9 - 3 × × 0.5 ÷ 3, 4) = -0.2283
  • round(7 × 3.141592653589793 ÷ 36 + 3 × × 0.5 ÷ 6 + 1 ÷ 3 - (3.141592653589793 ÷ 9 - 3 × × 0.5 ÷ 3), 2) = 1.46
