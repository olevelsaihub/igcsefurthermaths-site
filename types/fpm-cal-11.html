Knowledge card

Optimisation part 2: finding, justifying and evaluating the maximum or minimum

Calculation
  • At the optimum, the derivative of the quantity is zero, e.g. $\frac{dS}{dr} = 0$.
  • Justify with the second derivative: $\frac{d^2S}{dr^2} > 0$ gives a minimum, $< 0$ a maximum.
  • Write $\frac{180\pi}{r}$ as $180\pi r^{-1}$ before differentiating.
  • Keep only the physically sensible value (lengths are positive).
  • Keep the critical value unrounded (or to several figures) when substituting back for the optimum.

Before you begin

  • Differentiating negative and fractional powers.
  • Solving equations such as $r^3 = 45$.
  • The second-derivative test (FPM-CAL-09).
1

The method

  1. Write the expression with negative powers and differentiate.

    Why: Powers of $x$ in the denominator must be rewritten to differentiate them.

  2. Set the derivative to zero and solve for the variable.

    Why: This finds the stationary value.

  3. Find the second derivative, substitute the value and state its sign with the conclusion.

    Why: Justification is required for full marks.

  4. Substitute the unrounded value into the original expression (or constraint) for the optimum value or the other dimension.

    Why: Early rounding can make the final answer wrong.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Differentiating $\frac{180\pi}{r}$ as $\frac{180\pi}{1}$ or $180\pi \ln r$ instead of $-\frac{180\pi}{r^2}$.
  • ✗
    Not justifying the nature, or justifying with the first derivative only.
  • ✗
    Rounding the critical value too early, which changes the optimum value.
  • ✗
    Substituting into the derivative instead of the original expression for the optimum value.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Optimisation part 2: finding, justifying and evaluating the maximum or minimum · January 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 8 · part (b) · 5 marks

A solid cylinder has volume $90\pi$ cm$^3$ and total surface area $S = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{180\pi}{r}$ cm$^2$. Given that $r$ can vary, use calculus to find, to 3 significant figures, the value of $r$ for which $S$ is a minimum, justifying that this value of $r$ gives a minimum value of $S$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $S = 2\pi r^2 + 180\pi r^{-1}$
  2. $\frac{dS}{dr} = 4\pi r - 180\pi r^{-2} = 4\pi r - \frac{180\pi}{r^2}$
  3. $\frac{dS}{dr} = 0 \Rightarrow 4\pi r = \frac{180\pi}{r^2} \Rightarrow r^3 = 45$
  4. $r = \sqrt[3]{45} = 3.5569\ldots \approx 3.56$
  5. $\frac{d^2S}{dr^2} = 4\pi + \frac{360\pi}{r^3}$. At $r^3 = 45$: $4\pi + 8\pi = 12\pi > 0$.
  6. So this value of $r$ gives a minimum value of $S$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$r = 3.56$ (3 s.f.); $\frac{d^2S}{dr^2} = 12\pi > 0$, so $S$ is a minimum.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for differentiating, a method mark for setting $\frac{dS}{dr} = 0$ and solving, accuracy for $r = 3.56$, a method mark for the second derivative evaluated (or its sign argued) at this $r$, and accuracy for the conclusion 'minimum' with the positive value as reason.

Calculation check

  • round(45 × × (1 ÷ 3), 3) = 3.557
  • 4 + 360 ÷ 45 = 12.0
