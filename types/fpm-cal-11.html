Knowledge card

Optimisation part 2: finding, justifying and evaluating the maximum or minimum

Calculation

For a minimum or maximum, differentiate the quantity, set $\frac{dS}{dx} = 0$ and solve. The second derivative then justifies the nature.

The calculation

$\frac{dS}{dx} = 0$, then $\frac{d^2S}{dx^2} > 0$ gives a minimum

Try itFind the minimum of $S = x + \frac{16}{x}$ for $x > 0$.

  1. $\frac{dS}{dx} = 1 - 16x^{-2} = 0$ gives $x = 4$
  2. $\frac{d^2S}{dx^2} = 32x^{-3} = \frac{1}{2} > 0$, so the minimum is $S = 8$

Exam question

The surface area of a container is $S = x^2 + \frac{16}{x}$ cm$^2$ for $x > 0$. Use calculus to find the value of $x$ that gives the minimum $S$, justify that it is a minimum, and find that minimum value of $S$. (5 marks)

5

The method

  1. Negative powers

    Write the expression with negative powers, then differentiate.

  2. Set to zero

    Solve $\frac{dS}{dx} = 0$ for $x$.

  3. Second derivative

    Find $\frac{d^2S}{dx^2}$, substitute the value and state its sign.

  4. Substitute back

    Put the unrounded value into the original expression for the optimum.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Differentiating 16/x wrongly

    $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{16}{x}\right) = -\frac{16}{x^2}$, not $16$.

  • ✗
    Rounding early

    Keep the critical value exact until the final substitution.

Optimisation part 2: finding, justifying and evaluating the maximum or minimum · January 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 8 · part (b) · 5 marks

A solid cylinder has volume $90\pi$ cm$^3$ and total surface area $S = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{180\pi}{r}$ cm$^2$. Given that $r$ can vary, use calculus to find, to 3 significant figures, the value of $r$ for which $S$ is a minimum, justifying that this value of $r$ gives a minimum value of $S$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $S = 2\pi r^2 + 180\pi r^{-1}$
  2. $\frac{dS}{dr} = 4\pi r - 180\pi r^{-2} = 4\pi r - \frac{180\pi}{r^2}$
  3. $\frac{dS}{dr} = 0 \Rightarrow 4\pi r = \frac{180\pi}{r^2} \Rightarrow r^3 = 45$
  4. $r = \sqrt[3]{45} = 3.5569\ldots \approx 3.56$
  5. $\frac{d^2S}{dr^2} = 4\pi + \frac{360\pi}{r^3}$. At $r^3 = 45$: $4\pi + 8\pi = 12\pi > 0$.
  6. So this value of $r$ gives a minimum value of $S$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$r = 3.56$ (3 s.f.); $\frac{d^2S}{dr^2} = 12\pi > 0$, so $S$ is a minimum.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for differentiating, a method mark for setting $\frac{dS}{dr} = 0$ and solving, accuracy for $r = 3.56$, a method mark for the second derivative evaluated (or its sign argued) at this $r$, and accuracy for the conclusion 'minimum' with the positive value as reason.

Calculation check

  • round(45 × × (1 ÷ 3), 3) = 3.557
  • 4 + 360 ÷ 45 = 12.0
