Proving shapes and straight lines with vectors

Using the fact that parallel vectors are scalar multiples of each other to prove lines parallel, points collinear or a quadrilateral a parallelogram or trapezium, or to find an unknown that makes two vectors parallel.

Knowledge card

Proving shapes and straight lines with vectors

Written
  • Two vectors are parallel when one is a number times the other: $\mathbf{u}=k\mathbf{v}$.
  • Collinear points: if $\overrightarrow{PQ}=k\overrightarrow{QR}$ and the vectors share the point $Q$, then $P$, $Q$ and $R$ lie on one straight line.
  • Parallelogram: one pair of opposite sides are equal vectors, e.g. $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{DC}$ (parallel and the same length).
  • Trapezium: one pair of opposite sides parallel (a scalar multiple) and the other pair not parallel.
  • To find an unknown that makes two vectors parallel, write $x\mathbf{i}+y\mathbf{j}=k(c\mathbf{i}+d\mathbf{j})$ and compare components (equivalently $\frac{x}{c}=\frac{y}{d}$).
  • A proof must end with a written conclusion that says why the property holds.

Before you begin

  • Finding vectors between points (FPM-VEC-01) and round a figure (FPM-VEC-04).
  • Properties of parallelograms and trapeziums.
  • Solving simple simultaneous equations.
1

The method

  1. Decide which vectors to compare: opposite sides for a shape, or two vectors through a common point for collinearity.

    Why: The property is about those particular pairs of lines.

  2. Find each vector in simplified form (end minus start, or a route round the figure).

    Why: You can only compare vectors written in the same base vectors.

  3. Show that one is a number times the other, writing the multiple explicitly.

    Why: The scalar multiple is the evidence for 'parallel'.

  4. For a parallelogram show the vectors are equal; for a trapezium show the other pair is not a multiple; for collinearity name the common point.

    Why: Parallel alone does not prove these; the extra fact completes the proof.

  5. Write a conclusion sentence (e.g. 'so $ABCD$ is a parallelogram').

    Why: The final mark is for a correct, reasoned conclusion.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'they are parallel' without showing the scalar multiple.
  • ✗
    Proving collinearity without stating the common point.
  • ✗
    For a trapezium, not showing that the other pair of sides is not parallel.
  • ✗
    Comparing vectors that are not opposite sides (e.g. $\overrightarrow{AB}$ with $\overrightarrow{CD}$ but drawing the wrong conclusion about direction).
  • ✗
    Missing out the conclusion sentence.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Show that7Every vector must be found and shown, the equality or multiple must be written out, and a conclusion sentence must finish the answer.
Prove2As for Show that: show the vectors, state the scalar multiple and conclude with the reason (parallel, common point).
Find1Set the vector equal to $k$ times the given direction, compare components to make equations, solve, and give the value of the unknown.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Method marks for finding each needed vector, a mark for showing they are equal or a scalar multiple, and a final accuracy mark for a fully reasoned conclusion. 'Find' versions give marks for equating components and solving for the unknown.

Proving shapes and straight lines with vectors · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 4 · part (a) · 3 marks

The points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ are the vertices of a quadrilateral $ABCD$ such that $\overrightarrow{AB}=7\mathbf{i}+p\mathbf{j}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}=11\mathbf{i}-p\mathbf{j}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}=4\mathbf{i}-2p\mathbf{j}$. (a) Show that, for all values of $p$, $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. (3 marks)

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\overrightarrow{BC}=\overrightarrow{AC}-\overrightarrow{AB}=(11\mathbf{i}-p\mathbf{j})-(7\mathbf{i}+p\mathbf{j})=4\mathbf{i}-2p\mathbf{j}$
  2. So $\overrightarrow{BC}=4\mathbf{i}-2p\mathbf{j}=\overrightarrow{AD}$
  3. Also $\overrightarrow{DC}=\overrightarrow{AC}-\overrightarrow{AD}=(11\mathbf{i}-p\mathbf{j})-(4\mathbf{i}-2p\mathbf{j})=7\mathbf{i}+p\mathbf{j}=\overrightarrow{AB}$
  4. Opposite sides $AD$ and $BC$ (and $AB$ and $DC$) are equal vectors, so they are parallel and equal in length for every value of $p$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\overrightarrow{BC}=\overrightarrow{AD}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{DC}$, so both pairs of opposite sides are parallel and equal: $ABCD$ is a parallelogram for all values of $p$.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for finding a side vector such as $\overrightarrow{BC}$ by subtraction, an accuracy mark for showing it equals $\overrightarrow{AD}$ (or $\overrightarrow{DC}=\overrightarrow{AB}$), and a final mark for the conclusion that opposite sides are parallel and equal so $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

Calculation check

  • 11-7 = 4
  • -5-5 = -10
  • -2 × 5 = -10
  • 11-4 = 7
  • -5+2 × 5 = 5
