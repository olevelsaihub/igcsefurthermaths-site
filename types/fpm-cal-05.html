Finding the equation of a normal

Finding the gradient of the curve at a point, turning it into the normal gradient with the negative reciprocal and writing the normal's equation.

Finding the equation of a normal

Calculation

The normal is perpendicular to the tangent, so its gradient is the negative reciprocal $-\frac{1}{m}$ of the curve's gradient $m$ at the point.

3 steps to find

Use the negative reciprocal of the tangent gradient.

  1. Get y from the curve
  2. Negative reciprocal
  3. Clear the fractions

Get y from the curve

Working
$3(1)^2 - 2(1) = 1$

Negative reciprocal

Trap
Using $\frac{1}{4}$ without the minus sign is wrong.

Clear the fractions

Working
Multiply by $4$: $4(y-1) = -(x-1)$, so $x + 4y - 5 = 0$.
0 of 3 found

3 steps to find

Find the normal gradient first, then the line through the point.

  1. Value of the gradient
  2. Keep the minus sign
  3. Integer coefficients

Value of the gradient

Working
$\frac{dy}{dx} = 3e^{3x}$, so $m = 3$ at $x = 0$.

Keep the minus sign

Trap
$\frac{1}{3}$ is not the normal gradient: it must be $-\frac{1}{3}$.

Integer coefficients

Working
$3(y - 1) = -x$, so $x + 3y - 3 = 0$.
0 of 3 found

The calculation

$m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{m_{\text{tangent}}}$, so $y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_1)$

Exam question

The curve $y = 2x^2 - 3x$ has a point $P$ where $x = 2$. Find an equation of the normal to the curve at $P$ in the form $ax + by + c = 0$, where $a$, $b$, $c$ are integers. (5 marks)

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

5

The method

  1. Find the point

    Substitute $x = 2$ into the curve to get $y$.

  2. Tangent gradient

    Differentiate and put in $x = 2$.

  3. Negative reciprocal

    The normal gradient is $-\frac{1}{m}$.

  4. Line in the form asked

    Use $y - y_1 = -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_1)$ and clear fractions.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Tangent gradient used

    Check you have turned $m$ into $-\frac{1}{m}$.

  • ✗
    Minus sign lost

    The reciprocal of $4$ is $-\frac{1}{4}$, not $\frac{1}{4}$.

What a complete response includes

Method marks for differentiating, for evaluating the gradient at the point and for using the negative reciprocal in a straight-line equation through the point, then an accuracy mark for the final equation in the required form.

Finding the equation of a normal · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 9 · part (whole) · 5 marks

Find an equation of the normal to the curve with equation $y = (x^3 - 2x)e^{1-x}$ at the point on the curve with coordinates $(1, -1)$.

Worked explanation

  1. Check the point: at $x = 1$, $y = (1 - 2)e^{0} = -1$, so the point is on the curve.
  2. Product rule with $u = x^3 - 2x$, $v = e^{1-x}$: $\frac{du}{dx} = 3x^2 - 2$, $\frac{dv}{dx} = -e^{1-x}$.
  3. $\frac{dy}{dx} = (3x^2 - 2)e^{1-x} - (x^3 - 2x)e^{1-x}$
  4. At $x = 1$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = (3 - 2)(1) - (1 - 2)(1) = 1 + 1 = 2$.
  5. Normal gradient $= -\frac{1}{2}$.
  6. $y + 1 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 1) \Rightarrow 2y + 2 = -x + 1 \Rightarrow x + 2y + 1 = 0$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x + 2y + 1 = 0$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the product rule with the chain rule on $e^{1-x}$, accuracy for the gradient 2 at $x = 1$, a method mark for a line through $(1, -1)$ with gradient $-\frac{1}{2}$, and accuracy for an equivalent correct equation.

Calculation check

  • (3 × 1 × × 2 - 2) × 1 - (1 × × 3 - 2 × 1) × 1 = 2
  • -1 ÷ 2 = -0.5
  • 1 + 2 × (-1) + 1 = 0
