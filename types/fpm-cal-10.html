Try itA cuboid has a square base of side $x$, height $h$ and volume $8$. Show that its closed surface area is $2x^2 + \frac{32}{x}$.

  1. $x^2 h = 8$, so $h = \frac{8}{x^2}$
  2. $S = 2x^2 + 4xh = 2x^2 + \frac{32}{x}$

5

The method

  1. Write the two formulae

    Write the volume (the constraint) and the surface area, with $h$.

  2. Rearrange the constraint

    Make $h$ the subject of the volume equation.

  3. Substitute

    Put $h$ into the surface area so only $r$ is left.

  4. Simplify to the printed form

    Show each line until it matches exactly.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong surface area

    Count every face the shape has: no more and no fewer.

  • ✗
    Jumping to the answer

    A show that needs every line, so show the substitution.

Optimisation part 1: building the formula to maximise or minimise · January 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 8 · part (a) · 3 marks

A solid right circular cylinder has base radius $r$ cm and height $h$ cm. The cylinder has a volume of $90\pi$ cm$^3$ and a total surface area of $S$ cm$^2$. Show that $S = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{180\pi}{r}$.

Worked explanation

  1. Volume: $\pi r^2 h = 90\pi \Rightarrow h = \frac{90}{r^2}$.
  2. Surface area (two circles plus the curved surface): $S = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$.
  3. Substitute: $S = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r \times \frac{90}{r^2}$.
  4. $S = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{180\pi}{r}$, as required.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$S = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{180\pi}{r}$ (shown)

How the marks are earned

A method mark for $h = \frac{90}{r^2}$ from the volume, a method mark for the surface-area formula with both circular ends, and an accuracy mark for substituting and reaching the printed result. Check at $r = 3$: $h = 10$, and $2\pi(9) + 2\pi(3)(10) = 78\pi$ equals $18\pi + 60\pi = 78\pi$.

Calculation check

  • 90 ÷ 3 × × 2 = 10.0
  • 2 × 3 × × 2 + 2 × 3 × 10 = 78
  • 2 × 3 × × 2 + 180 ÷ 3 = 78.0
