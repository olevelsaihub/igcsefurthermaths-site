Product rule and chain rule: differentiating products and 'function of a function'

Using the chain rule on composite functions and the product rule on products such as an exponential times a polynomial, a trig function or a root.

Type 7 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-01

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

'Differentiate with respect to $x$', 'Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$' or 'Show that $\frac{dy}{dx} = \ldots$' where $y$ is two functions multiplied together (such as $(4x-3)e^{2x}$ or $e^{2x}\cos 2x$) or a function of a bracket (such as $\sin(3x-2)$ or $\sqrt{x^2+1}$).

Product rule and chain rule: differentiating products and 'function of a function'

Calculation

A product $uv$ differentiates to $u\,v' + v\,u'$. A function of a function differentiates to outside derivative × inside derivative.

The calculation

$\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx}$

Chain rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \times \frac{du}{dx}$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Differentiating each factor and multiplying: $(uv)' \ne u'v'$
  • Losing the inside derivative: $\frac{d}{dx}e^{2x}$ is $2e^{2x}$, not $e^{2x}$

Try itDifferentiate $y = x^2\sin 3x$.

  1. $u = x^2,\ v = \sin 3x$; $u' = 2x,\ v' = 3\cos 3x$
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2\cos 3x + 2x\sin 3x$

5

The method

  1. Name u and v

    Or, for a chain, name the inside function.

  2. Differentiate each

    Use the chain rule inside u′ or v′ where needed.

  3. Put it together

    Write $uv' + vu'$ before simplifying.

  4. Simplify to the form asked

    Factor out the common part, such as $e^{2x}$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Multiplying derivatives

    $u'v'$ earns nothing.

  • ✗
    Dropping the inside factor

    Check every $e^{kx}$, $\sin kx$ and bracket.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for using the product rule with two terms added (or the chain rule correctly), often B or M marks for each correct factor derivative, then an accuracy mark for a fully correct, simplified answer. In 'show that' parts the last mark needs the printed form reached with no gaps.

Given that $y = (4x-3)e^{2x}$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams

Your answer

Write your answer first. Feedback and progress stay on this device.

Figures check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Let $u = 4x - 3$ and $v = e^{2x}$.
  2. $\frac{du}{dx} = 4$ and, by the chain rule, $\frac{dv}{dx} = 2e^{2x}$.
  3. $\frac{dy}{dx} = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx} = (4x-3)(2e^{2x}) + 4e^{2x}$
  4. $= e^{2x}(8x - 6 + 4)$
  5. $= (8x-2)e^{2x}$, which can also be written $2(4x-1)e^{2x}$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{dy}{dx} = (8x-2)e^{2x}$

How the marks are earned

Method credit for a product-rule expression with both terms present; credit for the correct derivative $2e^{2x}$ of the exponential; accuracy for a correct simplified answer. Check at $x = 0$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = (0-3)(2) + 4 = -2$, and $(8(0)-2)e^0 = -2$.

Calculation check

  • (0-3) × 2 + 4 × 1 = -2
  • 8 × 0 - 2 = -2
  • (4 × 1-3) × 2 + 4 = 6
  • 8 × 1 - 2 = 6
