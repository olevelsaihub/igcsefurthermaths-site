Knowledge card

Finding the first term and common ratio of a geometric series

Calculation
  • A geometric series is multiplied by the same number each time: the common ratio $r$.
  • The $n$th term is $u_n = ar^{n-1}$, where $a$ is the first term.
  • Dividing one term by another cancels $a$: $\frac{ar^6}{ar^2} = r^4$.
  • An even power has two real roots: $r^2 = 2$ gives $r = \pm\sqrt{2}$; use the information in the question to choose.
  • Three consecutive terms $x, y, z$ of a geometric series satisfy $\frac{y}{x} = \frac{z}{y}$, so $y^2 = xz$.
  • Exact answers may need surds simplified or a denominator rationalised.

Before you begin

  • Laws of indices.
  • Surds and rationalising denominators.
  • Solving quadratic equations.
1

The method

  1. Write each given term as $ar^{n-1}$, e.g. 3rd term $ar^2 = 10$.

    Why: Every term is fixed by a and r.

  2. Divide the later term by the earlier one so that $a$ cancels.

    Why: This leaves an equation in r alone.

  3. Solve for $r$ (take the right root, including $\pm$ for even roots) and use any condition given to choose.

    Why: Conditions like 'the second term is negative' or 'r > 0' decide the sign.

  4. Substitute $r$ back into one equation to find $a$.

    Why: Now both defining numbers are known.

  5. For three terms in an unknown, use (middle)$^2$ = first $\times$ third and solve the quadratic.

    Why: This is the condition for a constant ratio.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using $ar^n$ for the $n$th term instead of $ar^{n-1}$.
  • ✗
    Taking only the positive root of $r^2 = k$, or ignoring the condition that chooses the sign.
  • ✗
    Subtracting the terms instead of dividing them.
  • ✗
    Leaving surds unsimplified when an exact form is asked for.

What a complete response includes

Method marks for writing the terms with the correct powers of r and for dividing to eliminate a; accuracy marks for r (with the correct sign) and for a. With three terms in an unknown, a method mark for the ratio condition, one for solving the quadratic, and accuracy for the values.

Finding the first term and common ratio of a geometric series · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 4 · part (a)(ii) · 5 marks

A geometric series $G$ has first term $a$ and common ratio $r$. The third term of $G$ is $10$ and the seventh term of $G$ is $40$. Given that the second term of $G$ is negative, find (i) the exact value of $r$, (ii) the value of $a$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $ar^2 = 10$ and $ar^6 = 40$
  2. Divide: $\frac{ar^6}{ar^2} = \frac{40}{10} \Rightarrow r^4 = 4$
  3. $r^2 = 2$ (as $r^2$ cannot be $-2$), so $r = \pm\sqrt{2}$
  4. Second term $ar = \frac{ar^2}{r} = \frac{10}{r}$ is negative, so $r < 0$: $r = -\sqrt{2}$
  5. $a = \frac{10}{r^2} = \frac{10}{2} = 5$
  6. Check: $ar^6 = 5 \times 8 = 40$ and $ar = -5\sqrt{2} < 0$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

(i) $r = -\sqrt{2}$ (ii) $a = 5$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for writing both terms as ar^2 and ar^6, a method mark for dividing to get r^4 = 4, an accuracy mark for r = -sqrt(2) with the sign justified by the negative second term, then a method mark for substituting back and an accuracy mark for a = 5.

Calculation check

  • 40 ÷ 10 = 4
  • 4 × × 0.5 = 2
  • 10 ÷ 2 = 5
  • 5 × 2 × × 3 = 40
  • round(5 × (-(2 × × 0.5)),4) = -7.0711
