Knowledge card

Factorising and solving a cubic

Written
  • If $(x-a)$ is a factor of a cubic $f(x)$, then $f(x)=(x-a)(\text{quadratic})$.
  • The quadratic quotient can be found by long division, synthetic division or comparing coefficients.
  • Compare coefficients quickly: the $x^2$ term of the quotient comes from the leading term, and its constant term comes from the constant of $f(x)$ divided by $-a$.
  • Factorise the quadratic if possible; otherwise use the quadratic formula for exact (surd) roots.
  • Solving f(x) = 0 means setting each factor equal to zero; include the known root.
  • The roots of f(x) = 0 are the x-coordinates where y = f(x) crosses the x-axis.

Before you begin

  • Factor theorem (FPM-IDN-08)
  • Polynomial division or comparing coefficients
  • Factorising quadratics and the quadratic formula
1

The method

  1. Write down the known factor (found or given earlier).

    Why: Dividing by it reduces the cubic to a quadratic, which you already know how to solve.

  2. Divide f(x) by the factor, or compare coefficients, to find the quadratic quotient.

    Why: This is the main method mark; check it by expanding back.

  3. Factorise the quadratic, or use the formula if it does not factorise.

    Why: This gives the remaining factors or roots.

  4. Write f(x) as a product of all its factors, or set each factor to zero.

    Why: 'Completely' means every factor must be linear where possible.

  5. List all the roots, including the one from the known factor.

    Why: Leaving out the known root is a common way to lose the final mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Arithmetic errors in the division, giving the wrong quotient.
  • ✗
    Leaving out the known root from the final list.
  • ✗
    Sign errors in roots, e.g. writing x = 1/3 from the factor (3x + 1).
  • ✗
    Stopping at (x - a)(quadratic) when 'completely' is asked.
  • ✗
    Giving decimals when exact roots are requested.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Express2Write f(x) as a product of linear factors, with the division or comparison shown.
Solve7Show the division and factorisation, then list every root; exact fractions or surds unless a rounding is asked for.
Hence4Use the factor from the previous part directly, then factorise or solve the quotient and give all the roots.
Find3Give the requested values (roots, constants or coordinates) with the division working shown; exact forms when asked.
Write down1Read the values directly from the factorised form, e.g. the x-intercepts are the roots; no extra working needed.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for a correct quadratic quotient (by any valid division), a method mark for factorising or solving that quadratic, and accuracy marks for the full factorisation or for all the roots. Longer versions include marks for exact surd roots or for a follow-on coordinate.

Factorising and solving a cubic · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Question 3 · part (b) · 4 marks

Solve the equation $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=0$. Show clear algebraic working. (From part (a), $(x-4)$ is a factor.)

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $(x-4)$ is a factor, so $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=(x-4)(6x^2+bx+c)$.
  2. Constant terms: $-4c=12 \Rightarrow c=-3$.
  3. $x^2$ terms: $b-24=-31 \Rightarrow b=-7$.
  4. Check the $x$ terms: $c-4b=-3+28=25$. Correct.
  5. So $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=(x-4)(6x^2-7x-3)$.
  6. $6x^2-7x-3=(3x+1)(2x-3)$
  7. $(x-4)(3x+1)(2x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=4,\ x=-\frac{1}{3},\ x=\frac{3}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x=4$, $x=-\frac{1}{3}$, $x=\frac{3}{2}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for dividing by $(x-4)$ to get a quadratic quotient, an accuracy mark for $6x^2-7x-3$, a method mark for factorising or solving that quadratic, and an accuracy mark for all three roots.

Calculation check

  • 6 × 4 × × 3-31 × 4 × × 2+25 × 4+12 = 0
  • 6 × 1.5 × × 3-31 × 1.5 × × 2+25 × 1.5+12 = 0
  • round(6 × (-1 ÷ 3) × × 3-31 × (-1 ÷ 3) × × 2+25 × (-1 ÷ 3)+12, 9) = 0
  • (2-4) × (6 × 2 × × 2-7 × 2-3) = -14
