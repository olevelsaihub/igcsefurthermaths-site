Factorising and solving a cubic

Calculation

Divide the cubic by a known factor to get a quadratic, then factorise it to write $f(x)$ as a product and find all the roots.

The known factor

Why

Quotient from division

Working

Factorise the quadratic

Trap

3 steps to find

Which factor does each root give?

  1. A root gives a factor
  2. Careful with the 2
  3. Include every root

A root gives a factor

Why
A root $x = 2$ gives the factor $(x - 2)$.

Careful with the 2

Working
$2x - 1 = 0$, so $x = \frac{1}{2}$

Include every root

Scores
Three roots are needed, including $x = 2$.

2

The calculation

If $(x - a)$ is a factor, then $f(x) = (x - a)(\text{quadratic})$

Solve f(x) = 0 by setting each factor to zero

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Arithmetic slips in the division
  • Leaving out the known root

Try itGiven $(x + 1)$ is a factor of $x^3 - 7x - 6$, solve $x^3 - 7x - 6 = 0$.

  1. $x^3 - 7x - 6 = (x + 1)(x^2 - x - 6) = (x + 1)(x - 3)(x + 2)$
  2. Roots: $x = -1$, $x = 3$ and $x = -2$

5

The method

  1. Start from the known factor

    Write it down, and check it with f(a) = 0 if needed.

  2. Divide

    Divide by the factor, or compare coefficients, to find the quadratic.

  3. Factorise the quadratic

    Factorise, or use the formula if it does not factorise.

  4. List the roots

    Set each factor to 0 and list every root.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Stopping early

    When "completely" is asked, factorise the quadratic too.

  • ✗
    Sign in a root

    $(3x + 1)$ gives $x = -\frac{1}{3}$.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for a correct quadratic quotient (by any valid division), a method mark for factorising or solving that quadratic, and accuracy marks for the full factorisation or for all the roots. Longer versions include marks for exact surd roots or for a follow-on coordinate.

Factorising and solving a cubic · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Question 3 · part (b) · 4 marks

Solve the equation $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=0$. Show clear algebraic working. (From part (a), $(x-4)$ is a factor.)

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $(x-4)$ is a factor, so $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=(x-4)(6x^2+bx+c)$.
  2. Constant terms: $-4c=12 \Rightarrow c=-3$.
  3. $x^2$ terms: $b-24=-31 \Rightarrow b=-7$.
  4. Check the $x$ terms: $c-4b=-3+28=25$. Correct.
  5. So $6x^3-31x^2+25x+12=(x-4)(6x^2-7x-3)$.
  6. $6x^2-7x-3=(3x+1)(2x-3)$
  7. $(x-4)(3x+1)(2x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=4,\ x=-\frac{1}{3},\ x=\frac{3}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x=4$, $x=-\frac{1}{3}$, $x=\frac{3}{2}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for dividing by $(x-4)$ to get a quadratic quotient, an accuracy mark for $6x^2-7x-3$, a method mark for factorising or solving that quadratic, and an accuracy mark for all three roots.

Calculation check

  • 6 × 4 × × 3-31 × 4 × × 2+25 × 4+12 = 0
  • 6 × 1.5 × × 3-31 × 1.5 × × 2+25 × 1.5+12 = 0
  • round(6 × (-1 ÷ 3) × × 3-31 × (-1 ÷ 3) × × 2+25 × (-1 ÷ 3)+12, 9) = 0
  • (2-4) × (6 × 2 × × 2-7 × 2-3) = -14
