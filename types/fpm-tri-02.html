Getting one exact trig ratio from another

Using sin^2 + cos^2 = 1 (and tan = sin/cos) to turn one known trig ratio of an angle into the exact value of another, with the right sign.

Type 6 10 Trigonometry FPM-TRI-02

★☆☆☆☆ · 2 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

You are told one ratio exactly (e.g. $\sin\beta = \frac{1}{3}$ with $\beta$ obtuse) or the sides of a triangle, and asked for the exact value of a different ratio, usually 'show that $\sin\theta = \frac{3\sqrt{15}}{16}$'.

Knowledge card

Getting one exact trig ratio from another

Written
  • $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ for every angle.
  • $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$.
  • For an acute angle all three ratios are positive; for an obtuse angle $\sin\theta > 0$ but $\cos\theta < 0$ and $\tan\theta < 0$.
  • Taking a square root gives $\pm$; the quadrant decides the sign.
  • The cosine rule rearranged: $\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$.
  • Simplify surds ($\sqrt{135} = 3\sqrt{15}$) and rationalise denominators for exact answers.

Before you begin

  • Simplifying surds and rationalising denominators.
  • Signs of trig ratios for acute and obtuse angles.
  • The cosine rule.
  • Fraction arithmetic.
1

The method

  1. Write down the ratio you know exactly (or find it, e.g. cos from the cosine rule).

    Why: Everything else is built from this one value.

  2. Use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to find the square of the other ratio, then square root.

    Why: This identity links sine and cosine of the same angle.

  3. Choose the sign from the size of the angle (obtuse: cosine and tangent negative).

    Why: The identity alone cannot tell you the sign.

  4. If tangent is wanted, divide: $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$, then simplify or rationalise.

    Why: Exact answers must be in simplest surd form.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Taking the positive square root for the cosine of an obtuse angle.
  • ✗
    Writing $\sqrt{1 - \frac{121}{256}}$ wrongly, e.g. subtracting numerators without a common denominator.
  • ✗
    Leaving $\sqrt{135}$ unsimplified or giving a decimal.
  • ✗
    Using a calculator to find the angle and then rounding, which cannot give an exact surd.
  • ✗
    In the cosine rule, putting the wrong side as the one opposite the angle.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for a correct use of the cosine rule or the identity, a further method mark for finding the second ratio (with the right sign), and an accuracy mark for the exact simplified form. In a show-that the final mark needs every step visible.

Getting one exact trig ratio from another · June 2019 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 2 · part (a) · 4 marks

Triangle $ABC$ has $AB = 2x$ cm, $AC = 3x$ cm and $BC = 4x$ cm. (a) Show that $\sin ABC = \frac{3\sqrt{15}}{16}$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Angle $B$ lies between $AB$ and $BC$; the side opposite it is $AC$.
  2. $\cos B = \frac{AB^2 + BC^2 - AC^2}{2 \times AB \times BC} = \frac{4x^2 + 16x^2 - 9x^2}{2(2x)(4x)} = \frac{11x^2}{16x^2} = \frac{11}{16}$
  3. $\sin^2 B = 1 - \cos^2 B = 1 - \frac{121}{256} = \frac{135}{256}$
  4. $B$ is an angle of a triangle so $\sin B > 0$: $\sin B = \frac{\sqrt{135}}{16}$
  5. $\sqrt{135} = \sqrt{9 \times 15} = 3\sqrt{15}$, so $\sin ABC = \frac{3\sqrt{15}}{16}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\sin ABC = \frac{3\sqrt{15}}{16}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a correct cosine-rule statement, an accuracy mark for cos B = 11/16, a method mark for using sin^2 + cos^2 = 1, and an accuracy mark for reaching the given surd with the simplification shown.

Calculation check

  • (4+16-9) ÷ 16 = 0.6875
  • (1-(11 ÷ 16) × × 2) × × 0.5 = 0.7261843774138906
  • 3 × 15 × × 0.5 ÷ 16 = 0.7261843774138907
