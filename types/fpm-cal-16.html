Knowledge card

Finding a curve from its gradient function

Calculation
  • Integration reverses differentiation: $\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$ for $n \neq -1$.
  • Integrate term by term; a constant $k$ integrates to $kx$.
  • Always add the constant of integration $+c$ when finding $y$ from $\frac{dy}{dx}$.
  • Find $c$ (and any other unknowns) by substituting a point that lies on the curve.
  • If $(x - a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ then $f(a) = 0$ (factor theorem), which gives another equation.

Before you begin

  • Differentiation of powers (to check the integral).
  • Fractions arithmetic.
  • The factor theorem and solving simultaneous equations.
1

The method

  1. Integrate each term: raise the power by one and divide by the new power.

    Why: This undoes differentiation term by term.

  2. Add $+c$.

    Why: Differentiation loses constants, so integration must allow for one.

  3. Substitute the known point (or use the factor theorem) to form equations.

    Why: The point fixes which of the family of curves is the right one.

  4. Solve for $c$ (and any other unknowns) and write the curve.

    Why: This gives the specific equation asked for.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Forgetting $+c$, or assuming $c = 0$ without checking.
  • ✗
    Integrating a constant to 0 instead of $kx$.
  • ✗
    Multiplying by the new power instead of dividing (e.g. $\int 12x^2\,dx = 36x^3$).
  • ✗
    Arithmetic slips with fractions such as $\frac{19}{2}x^2$.

Finding a curve from its gradient function · June 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 7 · part (a)(i) · 4 marks

The point with coordinates $(4, -104)$ lies on the curve $C$ with equation $y = f(x)$. Given that $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 - 19x + 12$, show that $C$ passes through the origin.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $f(x) = \int(4x^3 - 12x^2 - 19x + 12)\,dx = x^4 - 4x^3 - \frac{19}{2}x^2 + 12x + c$
  2. At $(4, -104)$: $-104 = 4^4 - 4(4^3) - \frac{19}{2}(4^2) + 12(4) + c$
  3. $-104 = 256 - 256 - 152 + 48 + c = -104 + c \Rightarrow c = 0$
  4. So $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3 - \frac{19}{2}x^2 + 12x$ and $f(0) = 0$: $C$ passes through the origin.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$c = 0$, so $f(0) = 0$ and $C$ passes through $(0, 0)$.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for integrating (powers raised by one), accuracy for all terms correct, a method mark for substituting $(4, -104)$ with $+c$, and accuracy for $c = 0$ with the conclusion stated.

Calculation check

  • 4 × × 4 - 4 × 4 × × 3 - 19 ÷ 2 × 4 × × 2 + 12 × 4 = -104.0
