Getting a bracket ready for the binomial series: a(1 + bx)^n

Calculation

Before the binomial series, take the first term out of the bracket so it starts with $1$: $(c + dx)^n = c^n\left(1 + \frac{d}{c}x\right)^n$.

Take the first term out

2 steps to find

Which step moves a number outside the bracket?

  1. The power acts on the 9
  2. Divide the x term by 9

The power acts on the 9

Why
$(ab)^n = a^n b^n$, so the power $\frac{1}{2}$ also applies to the $9$.

Divide the x term by 9

Working
$6x = 9 \times \frac{2}{3}x$, so $q = \frac{2}{3}$.
0 of 2 found

2 steps to find

Where does the minus sign go?

  1. Negative power: a root
  2. Keep the minus in q

Negative power: a root

Why
$4^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $p = \frac{1}{2}$.

Keep the minus in q

Trap
Writing $q = +\frac{3}{4}$ loses the sign from $4 - 3x$.

5

The method

  1. Write as one power

    Turn roots and fractions into a single power $(\ldots)^n$.

  2. Take the first term out

    Write the bracket as $c(1 + \ldots)$.

  3. Apply the power to both

    The outside factor is raised to $n$ too: $c^n$.

  4. Find the new x-coefficient

    Divide $d$ by $c$, keeping its sign.

  5. State p and q

    Give each simplified, as the question asks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Power missing outside

    $c$ must be raised to the same power $n$.

  • ✗
    Sign lost

    A minus in the bracket stays in q.

What a complete response includes

Usually two marks: a method mark for taking the first term out with the correct power applied to it (or for one constant correct), and an accuracy mark for both constants correct and simplified. In 'show that' versions the final line must match the given form.

Given that $(2 + 3x)^{-1}$ can be expressed in the form $p(1 + qx)^{-1}$ where $p$ and $q$ are constants, (a) find the value of $p$ and the value of $q$.

Worked explanation

  1. $(2 + 3x)^{-1} = \left[2\left(1 + \frac{3}{2}x\right)\right]^{-1}$
  2. $= 2^{-1}\left(1 + \frac{3}{2}x\right)^{-1}$
  3. $= \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \frac{3}{2}x\right)^{-1}$
  4. So $p = \frac{1}{2}$ and $q = \frac{3}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$p = \frac{1}{2}$, $q = \frac{3}{2}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for taking 2 out and raising it to the power $-1$ (or for one correct constant), and an accuracy mark for both $p = \frac{1}{2}$ and $q = \frac{3}{2}$.
