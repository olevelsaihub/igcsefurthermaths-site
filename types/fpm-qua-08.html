Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics · 4PM1

The difference of the roots

Using (alpha - beta)^2 = (alpha + beta)^2 - 4alpha*beta to find alpha - beta, and using it in difference-of-squares factorisations such as alpha^4 - beta^4 or alpha^2 - beta^2 to get exact surd values.

Type 7 2 The quadratic function FPM-QUA-08

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The question mentions $\alpha-\beta$, $\alpha^2-\beta^2$ or $\alpha^4-\beta^4$, often with a condition such as $\alpha>\beta$, and asks for an exact (surd) value or to factorise completely.

The difference of the roots

Calculation

The difference of the roots comes from $(\alpha-\beta)^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-4\alpha\beta$, so $\alpha-\beta$ is found without solving. Higher powers then factorise as differences of squares.

5

The method

  1. Sum and product

    Find $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha\beta$ from the coefficients.

  2. Square the difference

    Use $(\alpha-\beta)^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-4\alpha\beta$.

  3. Take the root

    Choose the sign from the condition on the roots, usually $\alpha>\beta$.

  4. Simplify the surd

    Write $\sqrt{n}$ with the largest square factor taken out.

  5. Higher powers

    Use differences of squares for $\alpha^4-\beta^4$ or similar.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong multiple

    It is $-4\alpha\beta$, not $-2\alpha\beta$.

  • ✗
    Unsimplified surd

    $\sqrt{24}$ is not finished: write $2\sqrt{6}$.

The difference of the roots · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 11 · part (b)(ii) · 2 marks

The roots of a quadratic equation $E$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$ where $\alpha>\beta>0$, with $\alpha-\beta=2\sqrt{6}$, $\alpha^2+\beta^2=30$, $\alpha\beta=3$ and $\alpha+\beta=6$. Without solving $E$, find the exact value of $\alpha^4-\beta^4$. (2 marks)

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\alpha^4-\beta^4=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)(\alpha^2-\beta^2)=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)(\alpha+\beta)(\alpha-\beta)$
  2. $=30\times 6\times 2\sqrt{6}$
  3. $=360\sqrt{6}$
  4. Check: $\alpha=3+\sqrt{6}$, $\beta=3-\sqrt{6}$ give $(3+\sqrt{6})^4-(3-\sqrt{6})^4=360\sqrt{6}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\alpha^4-\beta^4=360\sqrt{6}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a correct factorisation with the values substituted, and an accuracy mark for $360\sqrt{6}$.

Calculation check

  • 30 × 6 × 2 = 360
  • round((3+6 × × 0.5) × × 4-(3-6 × × 0.5) × × 4-360 × 6 × × 0.5,6) = 0
  • (3+6 × × 0.5) × (3-6 × × 0.5) = 3
