Using a line's equation to find a missing coordinate or intercept

Working with a line whose equation (or gradient) is already known to find a missing coordinate, an axis crossing point, or to draw the line on a grid.

Type 1 8 Coordinate geometry FPM-COO-04

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

'The point $P(a, 6)$ lies on the line ...', 'find the value of $a$', 'show that $p = 3$', 'write down the coordinates of where the line crosses the $y$-axis'. Usually 1 to 3 marks at the start of a coordinate question.

Try itThe line $2x - 3y = 6$ meets the $x$-axis at $A$ and the $y$-axis at $B$. Find $A$ and $B$.

  1. $y = 0$: $2x = 6$, so $A = (3, 0)$
  2. $x = 0$: $-3y = 6$, so $B = (0, -2)$

5

The method

  1. Write the equation

    Use the line equation given, or find it first.

  2. Substitute the known value

    Put the known coordinate into the equation, not the unknown.

  3. Solve

    Rearrange to get the unknown on its own.

  4. Check

    Substitute back to confirm.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Swapping coordinates

    Put $x$ into $x$, not $y$.

  • ✗
    Missing the pair

    For an intercept give the full point $(0, c)$.

What a complete response includes

One-mark parts are a B mark for the correct value or point. Two- or three-mark parts give a method mark for a correct substitution into the line (or gradient) equation and accuracy marks for each correct value.

Using a line's equation to find a missing coordinate or intercept · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 9 · part (a) · 2 marks

The line $l_1$ with equation $y + 2x - 4 = 0$ passes through the point $P$ with coordinates $(a, 6)$ and the point $Q$ with coordinates $(3, b)$. (a) Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $P(a, 6)$ on $l_1$: $6 + 2a - 4 = 0$
  2. $2a + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow a = -1$
  3. $Q(3, b)$ on $l_1$: $b + 2(3) - 4 = 0$
  4. $b + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow b = -2$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$a = -1$, $b = -2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for substituting one point into the line equation and an accuracy mark for both values correct.

Calculation check

  • 6+2 × (-1)-4 = 0
  • -2+2 × 3-4 = 0
