Knowledge card

Equation of a perpendicular line (including perpendicular bisectors)

Calculation
  • If a line has gradient $m$, a perpendicular line has gradient $-\frac{1}{m}$.
  • A line through $(x_1, y_1)$ with gradient $m$ is $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
  • The perpendicular bisector of $AB$ passes through the midpoint of $AB$ and is perpendicular to $AB$.
  • If the line passes through a point dividing $AB$ in a ratio, find that point first with the section formula.
  • To read a gradient from $ax + by + c = 0$, rearrange to $y = -\frac{a}{b}x - \frac{c}{b}$.

Before you begin

  • Gradient from two points or an equation.
  • Midpoint and section formula.
  • Rearranging linear equations.
1

The method

  1. Find the gradient of the given line or segment.

    Why: The new gradient comes from it.

  2. Take the negative reciprocal: $m_\perp = -\frac{1}{m}$.

    Why: Perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$.

  3. Find the point the new line passes through (a given point, the midpoint, or a ratio point).

    Why: A line needs a gradient and a point.

  4. Use $y - y_1 = m_\perp(x - x_1)$, clear fractions and rearrange to the required form.

    Why: The accuracy mark is for the exact form asked for.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using $\frac{1}{m}$ or $-m$ instead of $-\frac{1}{m}$.
  • ✗
    Using an end point instead of the midpoint for a perpendicular bisector.
  • ✗
    Using the wrong point: the line must go through the stated point, not a point on the original line chosen at random.
  • ✗
    Stopping before reaching the exact printed equation in a show-that.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Method marks are spread across the stages: the original gradient, the negative reciprocal, finding the point (midpoint or ratio point) and forming the line, followed by an accuracy mark for the final equation in the required or printed form.

Equation of a perpendicular line (including perpendicular bisectors) · January 2022 · Paper 1 · Question 10 · part (b) · 4 marks

The equation of the line $L_1$ is $y - 2x - 6 = 0$. The point $P$ with coordinates $(2, a)$ lies on $L_1$. The line $L_2$ is perpendicular to $L_1$ and passes through $P$. (b) Show that an equation of $L_2$ is $x + 2y - 22 = 0$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. From part (a): $a - 2(2) - 6 = 0$, so $a = 10$ and $P$ is $(2, 10)$.
  2. $L_1$: $y = 2x + 6$, so its gradient is $2$.
  3. Gradient of $L_2 = -\frac{1}{2}$
  4. $y - 10 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 2)$
  5. $2y - 20 = -x + 2$
  6. $x + 2y - 22 = 0$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x + 2y - 22 = 0$, as required.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the gradient of $L_1$, a method mark for the negative reciprocal $-\frac{1}{2}$, a method mark for a line through $(2, 10)$ with that gradient, and an accuracy mark for reaching $x + 2y - 22 = 0$ with no errors.

Calculation check

  • 10-2 × 2-6 = 0
  • 2+2 × 10-22 = 0
  • 2 × (-1 ÷ 2) = -1
