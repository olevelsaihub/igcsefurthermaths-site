When is the particle at rest (or at a given velocity)?

Calculation

At rest means $v = 0$, not $s = 0$. Set the right expression to zero, solve, and reject any negative time.

The calculation

Rest means $v = 0$; the particle is at the origin when $s = 0$

Reject any negative time when $t \geqslant 0$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Keeping a negative time when $t \geqslant 0$
  • Solving $s = 0$ when rest, $v = 0$, is asked

Try itA particle has $v = t^2 - 4$ for $t \geqslant 0$. Find when it is instantaneously at rest.

  1. $t^2 - 4 = 0$ gives $t = \pm 2$
  2. Reject $t = -2$: the particle is at rest only at $t = 2$ s.

5

The method

  1. Choose the right expression

    Rest is $v = 0$, not $s = 0$.

  2. Differentiate if needed

    If only $s$ is given, differentiate once to get $v$.

  3. Solve

    Factorise the quadratic or use the formula.

  4. Reject times

    Keep only the solutions with $t \geqslant 0$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Negative times

    A negative root for $t \geqslant 0$ is not an answer.

  • ✗
    Wrong expression

    Setting $s$ or $a$ to zero does not give rest.

When is the particle at rest (or at a given velocity)? · January 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (a) · 3 marks

A particle $P$ is moving along the $x$-axis. At time $t$ seconds ($t \geqslant 0$) the velocity, $v$ m/s, of $P$ is given by $v = 3t^2 - 23t + 30$. Find the values of $t$ when $P$ is instantaneously at rest.

Worked explanation

  1. At rest: $v = 0$, so $3t^2 - 23t + 30 = 0$.
  2. $(3t - 5)(t - 6) = 0$
  3. $t = \frac{5}{3}$ or $t = 6$ (both $\geqslant 0$).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$t = \frac{5}{3}$ s and $t = 6$ s

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting $v = 0$, a method mark for solving the quadratic, and accuracy for both values.

Calculation check

  • 3 × 6 × × 2 - 23 × 6 + 30 = 0
  • round(3 × (5 ÷ 3) × × 2 - 23 × (5 ÷ 3) + 30, 9) = 0
