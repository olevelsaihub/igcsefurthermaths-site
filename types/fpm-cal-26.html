Knowledge card

When is the particle at rest (or at a given velocity)?

Calculation
  • Instantaneous rest means $v = 0$.
  • Returning to the start (origin) means $s = 0$ (for $t > 0$).
  • A given velocity or acceleration: set $v$ or $a$ equal to that value.
  • Acceleration positive: solve the inequality $a > 0$.
  • Reject negative times when $t \geqslant 0$.

Before you begin

  • Differentiating polynomials.
  • Solving quadratics and linear inequalities.
  • Understanding displacement, velocity and acceleration.
1

The method

  1. Get the right expression ($v$, $a$ or $s$), differentiating if necessary.

    Why: Rest is a velocity condition; using $s$ by mistake gives nonsense.

  2. Set it equal to the given value (or make the inequality).

    Why: This forms the equation in $t$.

  3. Solve, usually a quadratic, and reject any negative time.

    Why: Time cannot be negative in these questions.

  4. Substitute back into $s$ if a position is asked.

    Why: Some questions want where, not just when.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Keeping a negative value of $t$.
  • ✗
    Solving $s = 0$ or $a = 0$ when rest ($v = 0$) is asked.
  • ✗
    Factorising errors in the quadratic.
  • ✗
    Reversing the inequality direction when solving $a > 0$.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

When is the particle at rest (or at a given velocity)? · January 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (a) · 3 marks

A particle $P$ is moving along the $x$-axis. At time $t$ seconds ($t \geqslant 0$) the velocity, $v$ m/s, of $P$ is given by $v = 3t^2 - 23t + 30$. Find the values of $t$ when $P$ is instantaneously at rest.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. At rest: $v = 0$, so $3t^2 - 23t + 30 = 0$.
  2. $(3t - 5)(t - 6) = 0$
  3. $t = \frac{5}{3}$ or $t = 6$ (both $\geqslant 0$).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$t = \frac{5}{3}$ s and $t = 6$ s

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting $v = 0$, a method mark for solving the quadratic, and accuracy for both values.

Calculation check

  • 3 × 6 × × 2 - 23 × 6 + 30 = 0
  • round(3 × (5 ÷ 3) × × 2 - 23 × (5 ÷ 3) + 30, 9) = 0
