Rectangle and shape problems with inequalities

Word problems where lengths in a diagram give expressions for perimeter or area, which are then used to form and solve equations or inequalities.

Type 10 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-04

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

A diagram or description of a rectangle (or flag, cross or similar shape) with side lengths in terms of x or w, and conditions such as 'the perimeter is less than 30' and 'the area is greater than 8', asking for the set of possible values in the form a < x < b.

Knowledge card

Rectangle and shape problems with inequalities

Calculation
  • Perimeter of a rectangle $=2(\text{length}+\text{width})$; area $=\text{length}\times\text{width}$.
  • 'Greater than' gives >, 'less than' gives <; translate each sentence into one inequality.
  • The perimeter condition gives a linear inequality and the area condition gives a quadratic inequality.
  • A length must be positive, so always include $x>0$ (and any other length $>0$) when combining.
  • The final answer is the overlap of all the conditions, written as a < x < b.
  • A shaded or cross-shaped area can be found by adding the strips or by subtracting the unshaded parts from the whole.

The method

  1. Write each side length in terms of the unknown, then write the perimeter and area expressions.

    Why: Every later step uses these expressions, so they must be right first.

  2. Turn the perimeter condition into a linear inequality and solve it.

    Why: This usually gives the upper limit for the length.

  3. Turn the area condition into a quadratic inequality, rearrange to compare with 0, factorise and choose the correct region.

    Why: This usually gives the lower limit.

  4. Remove any values that make a length zero or negative.

    Why: A negative length is not possible, so the negative region of the quadratic is thrown away.

  5. Combine everything on a number line and write the overlap as a < x < b.

    Why: All the conditions have to be true together.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Forgetting that the length must be positive and keeping the region $w<-4$.
  • ✗
    Using perimeter $=$ length $+$ width instead of $2(\text{length}+\text{width})$.
  • ✗
    Combining the two solution sets incorrectly (taking the union instead of the overlap).
  • ✗
    Choosing the inside region of the quadratic when the area condition needs the outside one.
  • ✗
    Giving a decimal instead of an exact rational end value.

Find4Show both inequalities being formed and solved, then give a single interval a < x < b with a and b as exact rational numbers (fractions are fine).
Write down1Give the expression directly, for example P = 6x + 6 or H = (2x + y)(3x + 2y); no working is needed but it must be simplified sensibly.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for each correct expression (perimeter and area), a method mark and an accuracy mark for solving the linear inequality, method marks for forming, solving and choosing the region of the quadratic inequality, and a final accuracy mark for the combined interval. Expression parts are often 1 mark each.

Rectangle and shape problems with inequalities · November 2024 · Paper 2 · Question 2 · part (whole) · 6 marks

The length of rectangle $R$ is $2$ cm greater than its width. The area of $R$ is greater than $8\text{ cm}^2$ and the perimeter of $R$ is less than $30$ cm. Given that the width of $R$ is $w$ cm, find the set of possible values of $w$. Give your answer in the form $a<w<b$ where $a$ and $b$ are rational numbers.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Width $=w$, length $=w+2$.
  2. Perimeter: $2(w+w+2)<30 \Rightarrow 4w+4<30 \Rightarrow 4w<26 \Rightarrow w<\frac{13}{2}$
  3. Area: $w(w+2)>8 \Rightarrow w^2+2w-8>0$
  4. $(w+4)(w-2)>0$, critical values $w=-4$ and $w=2$
  5. U-shaped graph, $>0$ means outside: $w<-4$ or $w>2$
  6. A width must be positive, so $w<-4$ is rejected, leaving $w>2$.
  7. Combine $w>2$ and $w<\frac{13}{2}$: $2<w<\frac{13}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$2<w<\frac{13}{2}$

How the marks are earned

Credit is given for the perimeter inequality and its solution $w<\frac{13}{2}$, for forming the area inequality $w^2+2w-8>0$, for the critical values $-4$ and $2$ and the outside region, and a final accuracy mark for $2<w<\frac{13}{2}$, which requires rejecting the negative region.

Calculation check

  • 2 × (6.5+6.5+2) = 30
  • 26 ÷ 4 = 6.5
  • 2 × (2+2) = 8
  • (-4) × × 2+2 × (-4)-8 = 0
  • 3 × (3+2) = 15
