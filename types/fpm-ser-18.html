Knowledge card

Expanding a bracket with a negative or fractional power

Written
  • $(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3 + \dots$
  • For $n$ not a positive whole number, the series is infinite and valid only for $|x| < 1$; for $(1 + bx)^n$ this means $|bx| < 1$, i.e. $|x| < \frac{1}{|b|}$.
  • Replace $x$ by the whole term: for $(1 - 4x)^{1/2}$ use $(-4x)$, so the $x^2$ term contains $(-4x)^2 = 16x^2$.
  • $\sqrt{1 - 4x} = (1 - 4x)^{\frac{1}{2}}$ and $\frac{1}{(1 + 3x)^2} = (1 + 3x)^{-2}$.
  • For $(a + bx)^n$ take out $a^n$ first: $(a + bx)^n = a^n\left(1 + \frac{b}{a}x\right)^n$.
  • $2! = 2$, $3! = 6$.

Before you begin

  • Index laws: roots and reciprocals as powers.
  • Fraction arithmetic with negatives.
  • Factorials.
1

The method

  1. Rewrite roots and fractions as powers, and make the bracket start with 1 (take out a constant if needed).

    Why: The series formula is for $(1 + \text{something})^n$.

  2. Identify $n$ and the 'something' (e.g. $n = \frac{1}{2}$, something $= -4x$).

    Why: Every term uses these two.

  3. Write the terms $1 + n(\ldots) + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}(\ldots)^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}(\ldots)^3$ with brackets.

    Why: Brackets make sure the coefficient of x is raised to the power too.

  4. Simplify each coefficient carefully, watching signs.

    Why: Negative n and negative terms produce alternating signs.

  5. Multiply by any constant taken outside the bracket.

    Why: Otherwise the expansion is of the wrong expression.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Squaring or cubing only $x$ and not its coefficient (writing $-4x^2$ instead of $16x^2$).
  • ✗
    Sign errors with negative $n$ or a negative term.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to multiply by the constant taken out of the bracket.
  • ✗
    Leaving coefficients unsimplified when lowest terms are required.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for the correct structure of the binomial terms (correct n, powers and factorials), then accuracy marks for the simplified terms, often one for two correct terms and another for all terms correct.

Expanding a bracket with a negative or fractional power · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Question 9 · part (a) · 3 marks

(a) Expand $\sqrt{1 - 4x}$ in ascending powers of $x$ up to and including the term in $x^3$, giving each coefficient as an integer.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\sqrt{1 - 4x} = (1 - 4x)^{\frac{1}{2}}$, with $n = \frac{1}{2}$ and the term $(-4x)$
  2. $= 1 + \frac{1}{2}(-4x) + \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2}(-4x)^2 + \frac{\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)}{6}(-4x)^3 + \dots$
  3. $= 1 - 2x + \left(-\frac{1}{8}\right)(16x^2) + \left(\frac{1}{16}\right)(-64x^3) + \dots$
  4. $= 1 - 2x - 2x^2 - 4x^3 + \dots$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\sqrt{1 - 4x} = 1 - 2x - 2x^2 - 4x^3 + \dots$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the correct binomial structure with n = 1/2 and -4x, an accuracy mark for 1 - 2x, and further accuracy for the x^2 and x^3 terms -2x^2 and -4x^3.

Calculation check

  • (1 ÷ 2) × (-1 ÷ 2) ÷ 2 × 16 = -2
  • (1 ÷ 2) × (-1 ÷ 2) × (-3 ÷ 2) ÷ 6 × (-64) = -4
  • 1-2 × 0.06 = 0.88
