Volume formulas for cones, pyramids and prisms using trig

Written

Use trig to write one dimension in terms of another, substitute into the volume formula, then show that the result reaches the printed form. Here $r = h\tan\alpha$ links radius and height.

The calculation

Cone: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, with $r = h\tan\alpha$ and $\alpha$ the half-angle

Filling from empty at a constant rate $k$ gives $V = kt$.

Exam question

A cone has vertical angle $90^\circ$, vertex at the bottom. It starts empty and fills at $2$ cm$^3$/s. At time $t$ seconds the liquid height is $h$ cm. Show that $h^3 = \frac{6t}{\pi}$.

5

The method

  1. Draw the cross-section

    Show the half angle in a right-angled triangle through the axis.

  2. Link r and h

    Use $r = h\tan\alpha$ with $\alpha$ the half angle.

  3. Write the volume in h

    Substitute into $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ and simplify.

  4. Equate to the filling

    Set $V = 2t$ and rearrange to the printed form.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Full angle used

    Use half the vertical angle in the trigonometry.

  • ✗
    Missing the one third

    The cone volume is $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, so keep the one third.

Volume formulas for cones, pyramids and prisms using trig · November 2025 · Paper 1 · Question 9 · part (a) · 5 marks

A hollow right circular cone is fixed with its axis vertical and its vertex $X$ at the bottom. Its vertical angle is $60^\circ$. It starts empty and at time $t = 0$ liquid starts to fill it at a constant rate of $0.2$ cm$^3$/s. At time $t$ seconds the height of the liquid is $h$ cm above $X$. (a) Show that $h = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9t}{5\pi}}$.

Worked explanation

  1. The half-angle of the cone is $30^\circ$, so the radius of the liquid surface is $r = h\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}$.
  2. $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(\frac{h^2}{3}\right)h = \frac{\pi h^3}{9}$
  3. From empty at a constant rate: $V = 0.2t = \frac{t}{5}$
  4. $\frac{\pi h^3}{9} = \frac{t}{5} \Rightarrow h^3 = \frac{9t}{5\pi}$
  5. $h = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9t}{5\pi}}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$h = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9t}{5\pi}}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for r = h tan 30, a method mark for the cone volume in terms of h, an accuracy mark for V = pi h^3/9, a method mark for V = 0.2t, and the accuracy mark for making h the subject.
