The quotient rule: differentiating fractions

Differentiating a fraction u/v, simplifying the result and using it to give a gradient or to show the gradient always has one sign.

Type 8 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-02

★★★★★ · 13 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

$y$ is a fraction with $x$ in the bottom (such as $\frac{x+7}{2x-3}$, $\frac{e^{3x}}{x}$ or $\frac{\sin 3x}{x-2}$) and the question asks you to differentiate it, find the gradient at a point, or show that the gradient is always positive or always negative.

The quotient rule: differentiating fractions

Calculation

To differentiate a quotient $\frac{u}{v}$, use $\frac{v\,u' - u\,v'}{v^2}$. The order of the terms on top decides the sign.

The calculation

$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) = \frac{v\,\frac{du}{dx} - u\,\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Reversing the top: $u'v - uv'$ flips the sign
  • Forgetting to square the bottom: $v$ instead of $v^2$

Try itDifferentiate $y = \frac{x^2}{x+1}$.

  1. $u = x^2$, $v = x + 1$, so $u' = 2x$, $v' = 1$
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(x+1)(2x) - x^2}{(x+1)^2} = \frac{x^2 + 2x}{(x+1)^2}$

5

The method

  1. Name the top and bottom

    Write $u$, $v$ and their derivatives $u'$, $v'$.

  2. Use the formula

    Write $\frac{vu' - uv'}{v^2}$ with your pieces in, in that order.

  3. Expand and collect

    Simplify the top carefully; leave the bottom squared.

  4. Substitute if asked

    For a gradient, put the $x$-value into $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reversed order

    $u'v - uv'$ gives the wrong sign.

  • ✗
    Bottom not squared

    Keep $v^2$, not $v$.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for the quotient-rule structure with the numerator in the right order and the denominator squared, credit for correct derivatives of top and bottom, then an accuracy mark for the simplified derivative or the value at the point. Sign arguments need a stated reason for the final mark.

The quotient rule: differentiating fractions · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 10 · part (d) · 3 marks

A curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{x+7}{2x-3}$, $x \neq \frac{3}{2}$. $C$ passes through the point $A\left(-\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{13}{8}\right)$. Show that the gradient of $C$ at $A$ is $-\frac{17}{16}$.

Worked explanation

  1. Let $u = x + 7$, so $\frac{du}{dx} = 1$; let $v = 2x - 3$, so $\frac{dv}{dx} = 2$.
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(2x-3)(1) - (x+7)(2)}{(2x-3)^2}$
  3. $= \frac{2x - 3 - 2x - 14}{(2x-3)^2} = \frac{-17}{(2x-3)^2}$
  4. At $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $2x - 3 = -1 - 3 = -4$, so $(2x-3)^2 = 16$.
  5. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-17}{16} = -\frac{17}{16}$, as required.

Gradient at $A$ $= -\frac{17}{16}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the quotient rule in the right order with the denominator squared, an accuracy mark for the simplified derivative $\frac{-17}{(2x-3)^2}$, and a final mark for substituting $x = -\frac{1}{2}$ and reaching $-\frac{17}{16}$ with the working shown.

Calculation check

  • (-0.5+7) ÷ (2 × (-0.5)-3) = -1.625
  • -13 ÷ 8 = -1.625
  • ((2 × (-0.5)-3) × 1 - (-0.5+7) × 2) ÷ (2 × (-0.5)-3) × × 2 = -1.0625
  • -17 ÷ 16 = -1.0625
