Knowledge card

The discriminant: equal, real or no real roots

Calculation

The discriminant $b^2-4ac$ of $ax^2+bx+c=0$ decides the roots: positive gives two distinct roots, zero gives equal roots, negative gives none. "Real roots" uses $\ge 0$.

Check each line of the discriminant

3 lines to find

Which line uses the discriminant correctly?

  1. Write b^2 − 4ac
  2. Simplify with the sign
  3. Critical values ±3

Write b^2 − 4ac

Formula
$b^2-4ac$ with $a=k$, $b=6$ and $c=k$.

Simplify with the sign

Trap
$-4\cdot k\cdot k$ is $-4k^2$, not $-4k$.

Critical values ±3

Working
$k^2\le 9$ gives $-3\le k\le 3$, then $k\ne 0$.
0 of 3 found

3 lines to find

A line touches a curve when the discriminant is zero.

  1. Move everything to one side
  2. a = 1 and c = 1
  3. Touch means zero

Move everything to one side

Working
$x^2-2x+3-mx-2=x^2-(2+m)x+1$.

a = 1 and c = 1

Why
Then $b^2-4ac=(2+m)^2-4$.

Touch means zero

Trap
Meeting twice is $>0$; touching is $=0$.
0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

$b^2-4ac$ for $ax^2+bx+c=0$

Two distinct: $>0$. Equal: $=0$. Real (any): $\ge 0$. None: $<0$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Using $>$ when the question says real roots (use $\ge$)
  • Dropping a restriction such as $k\ne 0$ from the final answer

Try itFind the values of $k$ for which $x^2+kx+4=0$ has equal roots.

  1. $b^2-4ac=k^2-4(1)(4)=k^2-16=0$
  2. So $k=4$ or $k=-4$

5

The method

  1. Find a, b and c

    Write the equation as $ax^2+bx+c=0$, with $c$ including any constant terms.

  2. Write the discriminant

    Work out $b^2-4ac$ with the correct signs.

  3. Pick the condition

    Use $>0$, $=0$, $\ge 0$ or $<0$, from the wording of the question.

  4. Solve and sketch

    Find the critical values, then choose the region from the sign of the quadratic.

  5. Apply restrictions

    Remove any value the question excludes, such as $k=0$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong c

    Check c after moving terms across the equals sign.

  • ✗
    Inequality sign

    Real roots use $\ge$; two distinct roots use $>$.

Find the set of values of $k$ for which the equation $2kx^2+5kx+5k-3=0$, where $k\ne 0$, has real roots. (4 marks)

Figures check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $a=2k$, $b=5k$, $c=5k-3$
  2. Real roots: $b^2-4ac\ge 0$, so $(5k)^2-4(2k)(5k-3)\ge 0$
  3. $25k^2-40k^2+24k\ge 0 \Rightarrow -15k^2+24k\ge 0 \Rightarrow 15k^2-24k\le 0$
  4. $3k(5k-8)\le 0$: critical values $k=0$ and $k=\frac{8}{5}$
  5. A positive quadratic in $k$ is $\le 0$ between its roots: $0\le k\le\frac{8}{5}$
  6. Since $k\ne 0$: $0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a correct discriminant expression, a method mark for simplifying and solving to get the critical values $0$ and $\frac{8}{5}$, an accuracy mark for these values and a final accuracy mark for $0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$ (strict at $0$, inclusive at $\frac{8}{5}$).

Calculation check

  • 25 × (8 ÷ 5) × × 2-8 × (8 ÷ 5) × (5 × (8 ÷ 5)-3) = 0
  • 25 × 1 × × 2-8 × 1 × (5 × 1-3) = 9
  • 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6
