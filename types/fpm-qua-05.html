Knowledge card

The discriminant: equal, real or no real roots

Written
  • For $ax^2+bx+c=0$ the discriminant is $b^2-4ac$
  • Two distinct (unequal) real roots: $b^2-4ac>0$
  • Equal roots (one repeated root): $b^2-4ac=0$
  • Real roots (equal or distinct): $b^2-4ac\ge 0$
  • No real roots: $b^2-4ac<0$
  • A line meets a curve where their equations are equal; substitute to get a quadratic, then: meets twice $>0$, touches $=0$, misses $<0$
  • To solve a quadratic inequality, find the critical values, sketch the parabola: $(k-p)(k-q)\le 0$ gives $p\le k\le q$; $\ge 0$ gives $k\le p$ or $k\ge q$ (for $p<q$)

Before you begin

  • Solving quadratic equations (factorising and the formula)
  • Solving quadratic inequalities
  • Substituting one equation into another
  • Expanding and simplifying brackets
1

The method

  1. Get a quadratic equal to zero: rearrange, or substitute the line into the curve and collect terms.

    Why: The discriminant only applies to the form $ax^2+bx+c=0$.

  2. Identify $a$, $b$ and $c$ carefully, including any expressions in the constant.

    Why: Most errors come from a wrong coefficient, especially $c$ when a number has been moved across.

  3. Write $b^2-4ac$ with the correct sign: $>0$, $=0$, $\ge 0$ or $<0$.

    Why: The words in the question decide which condition is needed.

  4. Simplify to a quadratic (in)equation in the constant and find the critical values by factorising or the formula.

    Why: The boundary values are where the discriminant is exactly zero.

  5. Choose the region using a sketch of the quadratic in the constant, then apply any extra condition such as $k\ne 0$.

    Why: A positive quadratic is below zero between its roots and above zero outside them; $a\ne 0$ is needed for a quadratic at all.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using $>$ instead of $\ge$ for 'real roots' (or $\ge$ for 'distinct')
  • ✗
    Choosing the wrong region, e.g. 'between' the critical values when it should be 'outside'
  • ✗
    Wrong $c$ after moving a term across the equals sign (for example forgetting the $-7$ in $kx^2-4x+2k-7=0$)
  • ✗
    Forgetting a stated restriction such as $k\ne 0$
  • ✗
    Algebra slips expanding $4ac$ when $a$ and $c$ both contain the constant

How each command word is answered

Find7Show the discriminant condition, the critical values and the final set clearly, e.g. $0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$.
Show that3Every step from the substitution to the stated range must be shown, with the given form reached exactly.
Hence1Use the quadratic from the previous part directly and apply the discriminant condition to it.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for using $b^2-4ac$ with the right coefficients (and, in line-and-curve questions, for the substituted quadratic), a method mark for solving the resulting quadratic in the constant, an accuracy mark for the critical values and a final accuracy mark for the correct region with the correct inequality signs.

The discriminant: equal, real or no real roots · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 1 · part (whole) · 4 marks

Find the set of values of $k$ for which the equation $2kx^2+5kx+5k-3=0$, where $k\ne 0$, has real roots. (4 marks)

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $a=2k$, $b=5k$, $c=5k-3$
  2. Real roots: $b^2-4ac\ge 0$, so $(5k)^2-4(2k)(5k-3)\ge 0$
  3. $25k^2-40k^2+24k\ge 0 \Rightarrow -15k^2+24k\ge 0 \Rightarrow 15k^2-24k\le 0$
  4. $3k(5k-8)\le 0$: critical values $k=0$ and $k=\frac{8}{5}$
  5. A positive quadratic in $k$ is $\le 0$ between its roots: $0\le k\le\frac{8}{5}$
  6. Since $k\ne 0$: $0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a correct discriminant expression, a method mark for simplifying and solving to get the critical values $0$ and $\frac{8}{5}$, an accuracy mark for these values and a final accuracy mark for $0<k\le\frac{8}{5}$ (strict at $0$, inclusive at $\frac{8}{5}$).

Calculation check

  • 25 × (8 ÷ 5) × × 2-8 × (8 ÷ 5) × (5 × (8 ÷ 5)-3) = 0
  • 25 × 1 × × 2-8 × 1 × (5 × 1-3) = 9
  • 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6
