Showing a cubic has only one real root

Proving that a cubic equation has exactly one real root by showing its quadratic factor has a negative discriminant.

Type 11 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-12

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Showing a cubic has only one real root

Divide by the known linear factor, then show the quadratic quotient has a negative discriminant $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, so the cubic has only one real root.

5

The method

  1. Divide

    Divide by the known linear factor to get the quadratic.

  2. Discriminant

    Write $b^2 - 4ac$ with the numbers substituted.

  3. State the sign

    Say it is negative, so the quadratic has no real roots.

  4. Conclude

    Name the single real root from the linear factor.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong quotient

    An error in the quotient gives the wrong discriminant.

  • ✗
    Sign in the discriminant

    Use $-4ac$, not $+4ac$.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for dividing to get the quadratic quotient (with an accuracy mark for it in longer versions), a method mark for using the discriminant of that quadratic, and a final accuracy mark for a complete conclusion that there is only one real root. The 'range of p' version adds marks for solving the discriminant inequality.

Showing a cubic has only one real root · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Question 4 · part (b) · 3 marks

$f(x)=x^3+px^2+qx+7$ where $p$ and $q$ are integers. $(x+1)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ and the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $(x+2)$ is $-5$. Part (a) gives $p=4$ and $q=10$. Hence show that $f(x)=0$ has only one real root.

Worked explanation

  1. From (a): $f(-1)=0 \Rightarrow -1+p-q+7=0 \Rightarrow p-q=-6$; $f(-2)=-5 \Rightarrow -8+4p-2q+7=-5 \Rightarrow 2p-q=-2$. So $p=4$, $q=10$.
  2. $f(x)=x^3+4x^2+10x+7=(x+1)(x^2+bx+c)$
  3. Constant: $c=7$; $x^2$ terms: $b+1=4 \Rightarrow b=3$; check $x$ terms: $c+b=10$. Correct.
  4. $f(x)=(x+1)(x^2+3x+7)$
  5. Discriminant of $x^2+3x+7$: $b^2-4ac=3^2-4(1)(7)=9-28=-19$
  6. $-19<0$, so $x^2+3x+7=0$ has no real roots.
  7. Therefore the only real root of $f(x)=0$ comes from $x+1=0$, i.e. $x=-1$: only one real root.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$f(x)=(x+1)(x^2+3x+7)$; $b^2-4ac=-19<0$ so the quadratic has no real roots, and $f(x)=0$ has only one real root, $x=-1$.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for dividing by $(x+1)$ to get $x^2+3x+7$, a method mark for working out its discriminant, and an accuracy mark for $-19<0$ with the conclusion that there is only one real root.

Calculation check

  • (-1) × × 3+4 × (-1) × × 2+10 × (-1)+7 = 0
  • (-2) × × 3+4 × (-2) × × 2+10 × (-2)+7 = -5
  • 3 × × 2-4 × 1 × 7 = -19
  • (2+1) × (2 × × 2+3 × 2+7) = 51
