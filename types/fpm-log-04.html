Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator

Exact surd arithmetic without a calculator: taking square factors out of roots and clearing surds from denominators.

Type 3 1 Logarithms, indices and surds FPM-LOG-04

★★★★☆ · 10 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Usually Question 1 of a paper: 'without using a calculator', 'show your working clearly', a fraction with a surd in the denominator, and an answer required in a form such as $a + b\sqrt{3}$ with integers or primes. It may be dressed up as a rectangle or triangle with a given area and one unknown side.

Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator

Calculation

To simplify surds and find exact values, take out square factors, and multiply top and bottom by the conjugate to clear a surd from a denominator.

1

Clear the surd, step by step

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 steps to find

Which step clears the surd from the bottom?

  1. Root times itself
  2. Difference of squares
  3. Split into two terms

Root times itself

Working
$\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2$, so the top is $6 + 3\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} + 2 = 8 + 5\sqrt{2}$.

Difference of squares

Working
$2^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = 4 - 2 = 2$, with no surd left.

Split into two terms

Why
Divide both terms by $2$: $4 + \frac{5}{2}\sqrt{2}$.

2 steps to find

Which surd is simplified first?

  1. Take out a square factor
  2. Like surds add

Take out a square factor

Why
$72 = 36 \times 2$, so $\sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$.

Like surds add

Why
$6\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 9\sqrt{2}$: only like surds combine.

The calculation

$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$ and $(a + b\sqrt{c})(a - b\sqrt{c}) = a^2 - b^2c$

  • Multiplying by the denominator itself, not its conjugate
  • Squaring a surd wrongly: $(2\sqrt{5})^2 = 20$

Try itRationalise $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5} + 2}$.

  1. $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5} + 2} = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 2}{(\sqrt{5})^2 - 2^2}$
  2. $= \frac{\sqrt{5} - 2}{1} = \sqrt{5} - 2$

Express $\frac{2}{3 + \sqrt{5}}$ in the form $a + b\sqrt{5}$, where $a$ and $b$ are rational. (3 marks)

5

The method

  1. Simplify the surds

    Take out square factors so like surds show.

  2. Pick the conjugate

    Change the sign between the two terms of the denominator.

  3. Multiply top and bottom

    Multiply both by the conjugate, not the denominator alone.

  4. Expand carefully

    Use $\sqrt{c} \times \sqrt{c} = c$ and check each sign.

  5. Divide and collect

    Split into the rational part and the surd part.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong multiplier

    Multiplying by the denominator leaves a surd underneath.

  • ✗
    Unsimplified answer

    Divide each term and cancel the common factor.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for multiplying by the correct conjugate (or for simplifying the surds correctly), a method mark for a correct expansion with a rational denominator, then accuracy marks for each constant. Area versions add a first method mark for forming the correct division from the area formula.

Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator · November 2023 · Paper 1 · Question 1 · part (whole) · 4 marks

Figure 1 shows triangle $ABC$ with angle $ABC = 90^\circ$, $AB = (2 + 4\sqrt{5})$ cm and $BC = (a + b\sqrt{5})$ cm, where $a$ and $b$ are integers. The area of triangle $ABC$ is $(34 + 11\sqrt{5})$ cm$^2$. Without using a calculator, find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

Worked explanation

  1. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times AB \times BC$, so $\frac{1}{2}(2 + 4\sqrt{5})(a + b\sqrt{5}) = 34 + 11\sqrt{5}$
  2. $(1 + 2\sqrt{5})(a + b\sqrt{5}) = 34 + 11\sqrt{5}$
  3. $a + b\sqrt{5} = \frac{34 + 11\sqrt{5}}{1 + 2\sqrt{5}} \times \frac{2\sqrt{5} - 1}{2\sqrt{5} - 1}$
  4. Numerator: $(34 + 11\sqrt{5})(2\sqrt{5} - 1) = 68\sqrt{5} - 34 + 22 \times 5 - 11\sqrt{5} = 76 + 57\sqrt{5}$
  5. Denominator: $(2\sqrt{5} + 1)(2\sqrt{5} - 1) = 20 - 1 = 19$
  6. $a + b\sqrt{5} = \frac{76 + 57\sqrt{5}}{19} = 4 + 3\sqrt{5}$
  7. So $a = 4$ and $b = 3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$a = 4$, $b = 3$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for using the area formula to write $BC$ as the area divided by $\frac{1}{2}AB$, a method mark for multiplying by the conjugate $2\sqrt{5} - 1$, a method mark for a correct expansion over the rational denominator 19, and an accuracy mark for both $a = 4$ and $b = 3$.
