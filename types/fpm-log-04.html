Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator

Exact surd arithmetic without a calculator: taking square factors out of roots and clearing surds from denominators.

Type 3 1 Logarithms, indices and surds FPM-LOG-04

★★★★☆ · 10 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Usually Question 1 of a paper: 'without using a calculator', 'show your working clearly', a fraction with a surd in the denominator, and an answer required in a form such as $a + b\sqrt{3}$ with integers or primes. It may be dressed up as a rectangle or triangle with a given area and one unknown side.

Knowledge card

Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator

Written
  • $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$, so take out square factors: $\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16}\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$.
  • $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$, and a decimal can be written as a fraction first: $\sqrt{0.96} = \sqrt{\frac{96}{100}} = \frac{4\sqrt{6}}{10}$.
  • Like surds add like algebra: $5\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} = 7\sqrt{3}$.
  • To rationalise $\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}$, multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{a}$: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$.
  • To rationalise $\frac{1}{a + b\sqrt{c}}$, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $a - b\sqrt{c}$, since $(a + b\sqrt{c})(a - b\sqrt{c}) = a^2 - b^2c$.
  • Comparing $p + q\sqrt{c} = r + s\sqrt{c}$ (with $p, q, r, s$ rational): $p = r$ and $q = s$.

Before you begin

  • Square numbers up to at least 144 and spotting square factors.
  • Expanding brackets, including the difference of two squares $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$.
  • Area of a rectangle and of a triangle ($\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$).
1

The method

  1. Simplify every surd by taking out square factors (and turn any decimal under the root into a fraction).

    Why: Simplified surds show which terms are 'like' and keep numbers small.

  2. If a side must be found from an area, write the unknown as area divided by the known length(s) first.

    Why: This turns the geometry into a surd division.

  3. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator (change the sign between the two terms).

    Why: The product of conjugates has no surd: $(a + b\sqrt{c})(a - b\sqrt{c}) = a^2 - b^2c$.

  4. Expand the numerator carefully, term by term, and work out the new denominator.

    Why: Most lost marks are expansion and sign slips here.

  5. Collect the rational part and the surd part, divide by the denominator, and read off the constants by comparing with the required form.

    Why: The rational and surd parts must match separately.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Multiplying by the denominator itself instead of the conjugate, so the surd does not disappear.
  • ✗
    Squaring a surd term wrongly: $(2\sqrt{5})^2 = 20$, not 10 or $4\sqrt{5}$.
  • ✗
    Sign errors when expanding with the conjugate, especially the product of the two surd terms.
  • ✗
    Not simplifying fully, e.g. leaving $\sqrt{12}$ instead of $2\sqrt{3}$, or not cancelling the common factor with the denominator.
  • ✗
    Using a calculator and writing decimals, which loses the method marks.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for multiplying by the correct conjugate (or for simplifying the surds correctly), a method mark for a correct expansion with a rational denominator, then accuracy marks for each constant. Area versions add a first method mark for forming the correct division from the area formula.

Surds: simplifying and rationalising the denominator · November 2023 · Paper 1 · Question 1 · part (whole) · 4 marks

Figure 1 shows triangle $ABC$ with angle $ABC = 90^\circ$, $AB = (2 + 4\sqrt{5})$ cm and $BC = (a + b\sqrt{5})$ cm, where $a$ and $b$ are integers. The area of triangle $ABC$ is $(34 + 11\sqrt{5})$ cm$^2$. Without using a calculator, find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times AB \times BC$, so $\frac{1}{2}(2 + 4\sqrt{5})(a + b\sqrt{5}) = 34 + 11\sqrt{5}$
  2. $(1 + 2\sqrt{5})(a + b\sqrt{5}) = 34 + 11\sqrt{5}$
  3. $a + b\sqrt{5} = \frac{34 + 11\sqrt{5}}{1 + 2\sqrt{5}} \times \frac{2\sqrt{5} - 1}{2\sqrt{5} - 1}$
  4. Numerator: $(34 + 11\sqrt{5})(2\sqrt{5} - 1) = 68\sqrt{5} - 34 + 22 \times 5 - 11\sqrt{5} = 76 + 57\sqrt{5}$
  5. Denominator: $(2\sqrt{5} + 1)(2\sqrt{5} - 1) = 20 - 1 = 19$
  6. $a + b\sqrt{5} = \frac{76 + 57\sqrt{5}}{19} = 4 + 3\sqrt{5}$
  7. So $a = 4$ and $b = 3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$a = 4$, $b = 3$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for using the area formula to write $BC$ as the area divided by $\frac{1}{2}AB$, a method mark for multiplying by the conjugate $2\sqrt{5} - 1$, a method mark for a correct expansion over the rational denominator 19, and an accuracy mark for both $a = 4$ and $b = 3$.

Calculation check
