Rates of change with one chain-rule link

Linking a rate with respect to time to the rate of a length using one chain, such as dV/dt = dV/dh times dh/dt.

Type 22 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-14

★★★★★ · 16 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Rates of change with one chain-rule link

Calculation

A rate of change links through one chain: $\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$. Find the derivative, then divide the given rate by it.

Find $\frac{dV}{dt}$ through the chain rule for rates.

  2. Chain link, multiply

Derivative of r³

Working
$\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\right) = 4\pi r^2$

Chain link, multiply

Trap
Multiply: $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$, not divide.

Final rate with units

3 steps to find

Find $\frac{dr}{dt}$: this time you divide the rates.

  1. Derivative at r = 5
  2. Divide, do not multiply
  3. Three significant figures

Derivative at r = 5

Why
Differentiate first, then substitute $r = 5$.

Divide, do not multiply

Trap
Multiplying gives $100\pi$, a wrong answer.

Three significant figures

Scores
Give $\frac{1}{\pi}$ as a decimal to 3 s.f. if asked.
0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$, so $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{dA/dt}{dA/dr}$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Multiplying where you should divide: $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dt} \times \frac{dA}{dr}$ is wrong
  • Substituting the instant's values before differentiating

Try itA circle has area $A = \pi r^2$. When $r = 2$, $A$ is increasing at $4\pi$ cm²/s. Find $\frac{dr}{dt}$.

  1. $\frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r = 4\pi$ when $r = 2$
  2. $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{4\pi}{4\pi} = 1$ cm/s

A sphere has radius $r$ cm and volume $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. Air is pumped in so that $V$ increases at $200$ cm³/s. Find the rate at which $r$ increases when $r = 5$. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (3 marks)

5

The method

  1. Volume in one variable

    Write $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ in terms of $r$.

  2. Differentiate

    Find $\frac{dV}{dr}$.

  3. Chain rule

    Write $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt}$, then rearrange for $\frac{dr}{dt}$.

  4. Substitute and answer

    Find $r$ at the instant, then evaluate with units.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Multiplying instead of dividing

    To find $\frac{dr}{dt}$, divide $\frac{dV}{dt}$ by $\frac{dV}{dr}$.

  • ✗
    Units

    A rate of a volume is cm³/s; a rate of a radius is cm/s.

Rates of change with one chain-rule link · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Question 2 · part (whole) · 6 marks

Oil is leaking from a pipe and forms a circular pool on a horizontal surface. The area of the surface of the pool is increasing at a constant rate of 8 cm$^2$/s. Find, in cm/s to 3 significant figures, the rate at which the radius of the pool is increasing when the area of the pool is 50 cm$^2$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $A = \pi r^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r$
  2. When $A = 50$: $\pi r^2 = 50 \Rightarrow r = \sqrt{\frac{50}{\pi}} = 3.9894\ldots$
  3. $\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dr} \times \frac{dr}{dt} \Rightarrow 8 = 2\pi r \times \frac{dr}{dt}$
  4. $\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{8}{2\pi r} = \frac{8}{2\pi(3.9894\ldots)} = 0.31915\ldots$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{dr}{dt} = 0.319$ cm/s (3 s.f.)

How the marks are earned

A mark for $\frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi r$, a method mark for finding $r$ when $A = 50$, a method mark for the chain rule used correctly (dividing 8 by $2\pi r$), and accuracy for 0.319.

Calculation check

  • round((50 ÷ 3.141592653589793) × × 0.5, 4) = 3.9894
  • round(8 ÷ (2 × 3.141592653589793 × (50 ÷ 3.141592653589793) × × 0.5), 3) = 0.319
