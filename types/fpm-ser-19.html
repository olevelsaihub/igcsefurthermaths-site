Knowledge card

When is a binomial expansion valid?

Written
  • For $n$ not a positive whole number, the series is infinite and valid only for $|x| < 1$; for $(1 + bx)^n$ this means $|bx| < 1$, i.e. $|x| < \frac{1}{|b|}$.
  • Make the bracket start with 1 first: $(3 + x)^{-3} = \frac{1}{27}\left(1 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^{-3}$, valid for $\left|\frac{x}{3}\right| < 1$.
  • $|bx| < 1 \iff |x| < \frac{1}{|b|}$, which means $-\frac{1}{|b|} < x < \frac{1}{|b|}$.
  • The sign inside the bracket does not change the range.

Before you begin

  • Modulus inequalities.
  • Writing a bracket in the form 1 + bx.
1

The method

  1. Look at the bracket in the form $(1 + bx)^n$ that you expanded.

    Why: The condition applies to the term after the 1.

  2. Write $|bx| < 1$.

    Why: The series converges only when this term is smaller than 1 in size.

  3. Divide by $|b|$: $|x| < \frac{1}{|b|}$.

    Why: This is the range asked for.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Forgetting the modulus and writing $x < 3$.
  • ✗
    Using the bracket before the constant was taken out, e.g. $|x| < 1$ for $(3 + x)^{-3}$.
  • ✗
    Inverting $b$ wrongly, e.g. $|x| < \frac{2}{3}$ instead of $|x| < \frac{3}{2}$.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

When is a binomial expansion valid? · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 7 · part (b) · 1 mark

(a) Expand $\left(1 + \frac{x}{3}\right)^{-3}$ in ascending powers of $x$ up to and including the term in $x^3$. (b) Write down the range of values of $x$ for which your expansion is valid.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The expansion is of $(1 + bx)^{-3}$ with $b = \frac{1}{3}$
  2. Valid when $\left|\frac{x}{3}\right| < 1$
  3. $|x| < 3$, i.e. $-3 < x < 3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$|x| < 3$

How the marks are earned

One mark for |x| < 3 (or -3 < x < 3).

Calculation check

  • 1 ÷ (1 ÷ 3) = 3
