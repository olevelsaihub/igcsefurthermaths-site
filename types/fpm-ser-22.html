Finding unknown constants from binomial coefficients

Writing coefficients of a binomial expansion in terms of unknown constants (such as n and a) and using given information to solve for them.

Type 15 5–6 Series and the binomial series FPM-SER-22

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

An expansion with letters, such as $(1 + ax)^n$ or $(1 + px)^{-5}$, with information like \"the coefficient of $x$ is 15 and the coefficient of $x^2$ equals the coefficient of $x^3$\" or \"$c_4 = 2c_3$\", and a request to find the constants.

Knowledge card

Finding unknown constants from binomial coefficients

Written
  • $(1 + ax)^n = 1 + nax + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}a^3x^3 + \dots$
  • The coefficient of $x^k$ contains $a^k$: do not forget to raise $a$ to the power.
  • Each piece of information about a coefficient gives one equation.
  • Dividing one coefficient equation by another often cancels common factors such as $n(n-1)a^2$.
  • Check any condition given (such as $n > 2$) when choosing a solution.

Before you begin

  • Binomial series.
  • Solving simultaneous non-linear equations.
  • Factorising and cancelling.
1

The method

  1. Write the general coefficients of x, x^2 and x^3 with the letters left in.

    Why: These are what the information refers to.

  2. Turn each fact into an equation.

    Why: Two unknowns need two equations.

  3. Divide the equations or cancel common factors to get a simpler equation in one letter.

    Why: This avoids solving complicated non-linear equations directly.

  4. Solve, substitute back for the other letter, and check the conditions.

    Why: Ensures the answer satisfies all the information.

  5. Use the values to find any further coefficient asked for.

    Why: Later parts often want another coefficient.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing the $x^2$ coefficient as $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}a$ instead of $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2$.
  • ✗
    Cancelling $n$ or $(n-1)$ incorrectly when dividing equations.
  • ✗
    Ignoring a condition such as n > 2 and keeping an invalid value.
  • ✗
    Arithmetic slips with fractional n.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Method marks for correct general coefficients and for forming the equations from the information, a method mark for a valid way of solving them (usually by dividing), and accuracy marks for each constant; a further coefficient earns a method mark for substitution and an accuracy mark for the value.

Finding unknown constants from binomial coefficients · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 5 · part (b) · 6 marks

$(1 + ax)^n$ is expanded in ascending powers of $x$, where $a$ and $n$ are constants and $n > 2$. The coefficient of $x$ is $15$ and the coefficient of $x^2$ is equal to the coefficient of $x^3$. (b) Find the value of $a$ and the value of $n$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Coefficients: $x$: $na$; $x^2$: $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2$; $x^3$: $\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}a^3$
  2. $na = 15$
  3. $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}a^2 = \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{6}a^3$
  4. Divide by $\frac{n(n-1)a^2}{2}$ (non-zero since $n > 2$, $a \neq 0$): $1 = \frac{(n-2)a}{3} \Rightarrow (n-2)a = 3$
  5. $na - 2a = 3 \Rightarrow 15 - 2a = 3 \Rightarrow a = 6$
  6. $n = \frac{15}{6} = \frac{5}{2}$, which satisfies $n > 2$
  7. Check: $x^2$ coefficient $\frac{\frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2}}{2} \times 36 = \frac{135}{2}$ and $x^3$ coefficient $\frac{\frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}}{6} \times 216 = \frac{135}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$a = 6$, $n = \frac{5}{2}$

How the marks are earned

Method marks for using na = 15 and for equating the x^2 and x^3 coefficients, a method mark for simplifying to (n - 2)a = 3, a method mark for solving the pair, and accuracy marks for a = 6 and n = 5/2.

Calculation check

  • 2.5 × 6 = 15
  • 2.5 × 1.5 ÷ 2 × 6 × × 2 = 67.5
  • 2.5 × 1.5 × 0.5 ÷ 6 × 6 × × 3 = 67.5
