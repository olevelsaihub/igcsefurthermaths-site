Area between two curves (or a curve and a line)

Calculation

The area between two graphs is the top graph minus the bottom graph, integrated between their intersection x-values.

The calculation

$A = \int_a^b \left(y_{\text{top}} - y_{\text{bottom}}\right)dx$

The limits are the $x$-values where the two graphs meet; test a value to see which is on top.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Subtracting the wrong way round: the area comes out negative
  • Using axis crossings as limits instead of the meeting points

Try itFind the area between $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x$.

  1. $x^2 = 2x$ gives $x = 0$ or $2$, and $2x$ is on top
  2. $\int_0^2 (2x - x^2)\,dx = \left[x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2 = \frac{4}{3}$

5

The method

  1. Find the meeting points

    Set the two equations equal and solve for $x$.

  2. Find the top graph

    Test a value between the limits in each equation.

  3. Integrate top minus bottom

    Simplify first, then integrate once.

  4. Substitute the limits

    Upper minus lower, keeping the answer exact.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Subtracting the wrong way

    Top minus bottom keeps the area positive.

  • ✗
    Wrong limits

    Use the x-values where the graphs meet, not where they meet the axes.

Area between two curves (or a curve and a line) · November 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (c) · 7 marks

The curve $S$ has equation $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2x - 6$ (it passes through $(-2, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and $P(4, -6)$). The line $l$, the normal to $S$ at $P$, has equation $2y + x + 8 = 0$. The finite region bounded by $S$ and $l$ is shaded. Use algebraic integration to find the exact area of the shaded region.

Worked explanation

  1. $S$: $y = p(x+2)(x-6)$; at $(4,-6)$: $-12p = -6$, so $p = \frac{1}{2}$ and $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2x - 6$.
  2. Intersections: $-\frac{x+8}{2} = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2x - 6 \Rightarrow -x - 8 = x^2 - 4x - 12 \Rightarrow x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$, so $x = -1$ or $x = 4$.
  3. Between the limits the line is above the curve, so area $= \int_{-1}^{4}\left[-\frac{x+8}{2} - \left(\frac{1}{2}x^2 - 2x - 6\right)\right]dx = \int_{-1}^{4}\left(2 + \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{1}{2}x^2\right)dx$
  4. $= \left[2x + \frac{3}{4}x^2 - \frac{1}{6}x^3\right]_{-1}^{4} = \left(8 + 12 - \frac{32}{3}\right) - \left(-2 + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{6}\right)$
  5. $= \frac{28}{3} - \left(-\frac{13}{12}\right) = \frac{112}{12} + \frac{13}{12} = \frac{125}{12}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Area $= \frac{125}{12}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for solving the line and curve together, accuracy for $x = -1$ and $x = 4$, a method mark for integrating line minus curve, a method mark for substituting the limits, and accuracy for $\frac{125}{12}$. Check: $\frac{\frac{1}{2}(4-(-1))^3}{6} = \frac{125}{12}$.

Calculation check

  • (8 + 12 - 32 ÷ 3) - (-2 + 3 ÷ 4 + 1 ÷ 6) = 10.416666666666666
  • 125 ÷ 12 = 10.416666666666666
  • 0.5 × (4-(-1)) × × 3 ÷ 6 = 10.416666666666666
