A rational curve has two branches, one in each corner made by its asymptotes. Find both asymptotes and the intercepts, then draw each branch smoothly towards the asymptotes.

5

The method

  1. Draw the asymptotes

    Dashed lines $x = \ldots$ and $y = \ldots$, labelled.

  2. Mark the intercepts

    Put the coordinates on the axes.

  3. Choose the corners

    Test one point on each side of the vertical asymptote.

  4. Draw both branches

    Smooth curves getting closer to both asymptotes, never touching them.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One branch only

    This type of curve has two branches.

  • ✗
    Crossing the asymptote

    A branch must not cross or bend away from its asymptote.

Sketching a rational curve with its asymptotes · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (b) · 5 marks

A curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{qx - 2}{x - p}$, $x \neq p$. In part (a) it was shown that $p = 1$ and found that $q = 3$, so $C$ is $y = \frac{3x - 2}{x - 1}$. Sketch $C$, showing clearly the asymptotes and the coordinates of the points where $C$ crosses the coordinate axes.

Worked explanation

  1. Check of part (a): $A = \left(0, \frac{-2}{-p}\right) = \left(0, \frac{2}{p}\right)$ lies on $y = x + 2$, so $\frac{2}{p} = 2$, $p = 1$
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{q(x - 1) - (qx - 2)}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{2 - q}{(x - 1)^2}$; at $x = 0$ the tangent gradient is $-1$ (normal gradient $1$), so $2 - q = -1$, $q = 3$
  3. Vertical asymptote: $x - 1 = 0$, so $x = 1$
  4. Horizontal asymptote: $y = \frac{3}{1} = 3$
  5. $y$-intercept: $x = 0$ gives $y = \frac{-2}{-1} = 2$, so $(0, 2)$
  6. $x$-intercept: $3x - 2 = 0$, so $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$
  7. Corners: the intercepts are left of $x = 1$ and below $y = 3$, so one branch is bottom-left; test $x = 2$: $y = \frac{4}{1} = 4 > 3$, so the other branch is top-right
  8. Draw the bottom-left branch coming in just under $y = 3$ from the left, passing through $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ and plunging down beside $x = 1$; draw the top-right branch coming down beside $x = 1$ and levelling out just above $y = 3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Two branches with dashed asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 3$; the lower-left branch passes through $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$, the upper-right branch lies above $y = 3$ to the right of $x = 1$.

How the marks are earned

Marks are earned for two branches of the correct shape in the bottom-left and top-right regions, for the asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 3$ shown with their equations, and for the intercepts $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ labelled.

Calculation check

  • (3 × 0-2) ÷ (0-1) = 2.0
  • 3 × (2 ÷ 3)-2 = 0.0
  • (3 × 2-2) ÷ (2-1) = 4.0
  • (3 × (-1)-2) ÷ (-1-1) = 2.5
  • 2-3 = -1
