Knowledge card

Sketching a rational curve with its asymptotes

Written
  • $y = \frac{ax + b}{cx + d}$ has two separate branches, one on each side of the vertical asymptote $x = -\frac{d}{c}$
  • Both branches approach the horizontal asymptote $y = \frac{a}{c}$ as $x \to \pm\infty$
  • The branches lie in opposite 'corners' made by the two asymptotes
  • The intercepts $\left(0, \frac{b}{d}\right)$ and $\left(-\frac{b}{a}, 0\right)$ tell you which pair of opposite corners is used
  • Asymptotes are drawn as dashed lines and labelled with their equations
  • The curve never crosses the vertical asymptote, and a branch must not bend away from an asymptote

Before you begin

  • Asymptotes of a rational curve (FPM-GRA-04)
  • Intercepts of a rational curve (FPM-GRA-05)
  • Substituting a test value into a fraction
1

The method

  1. Draw both asymptotes as dashed lines and label them, e.g. $x = 1$ and $y = 3$.

    Why: They are the framework the curve hangs on, and they carry marks.

  2. Mark the $x$- and $y$-intercepts with their coordinates.

    Why: These are required by the question and fix which branch goes where.

  3. Decide which corners the branches are in: the intercepts lie on one branch; the other branch is in the opposite corner. Test a point if unsure.

    Why: A curve in the wrong corners loses the shape mark.

  4. Draw each branch smoothly, getting closer and closer to both asymptotes without touching or crossing them.

    Why: This is what 'asymptote' means, and it is what the shape mark checks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Only one branch drawn
  • ✗
    Branches in the wrong pair of corners
  • ✗
    A branch crossing or curling away from an asymptote
  • ✗
    Asymptotes drawn but not labelled with their equations
  • ✗
    Intercepts shown as rounded decimals or left unlabelled

What a complete response includes

Marks for the correct shape (two branches in the right regions approaching the asymptotes), for both asymptotes shown with equations, and for the intercepts labelled. Longer versions add marks for each feature separately or for turning points.

Sketching a rational curve with its asymptotes · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (b) · 5 marks

A curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{qx - 2}{x - p}$, $x \neq p$. In part (a) it was shown that $p = 1$ and found that $q = 3$, so $C$ is $y = \frac{3x - 2}{x - 1}$. Sketch $C$, showing clearly the asymptotes and the coordinates of the points where $C$ crosses the coordinate axes.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Check of part (a): $A = \left(0, \frac{-2}{-p}\right) = \left(0, \frac{2}{p}\right)$ lies on $y = x + 2$, so $\frac{2}{p} = 2$, $p = 1$
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{q(x - 1) - (qx - 2)}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{2 - q}{(x - 1)^2}$; at $x = 0$ the tangent gradient is $-1$ (normal gradient $1$), so $2 - q = -1$, $q = 3$
  3. Vertical asymptote: $x - 1 = 0$, so $x = 1$
  4. Horizontal asymptote: $y = \frac{3}{1} = 3$
  5. $y$-intercept: $x = 0$ gives $y = \frac{-2}{-1} = 2$, so $(0, 2)$
  6. $x$-intercept: $3x - 2 = 0$, so $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$
  7. Corners: the intercepts are left of $x = 1$ and below $y = 3$, so one branch is bottom-left; test $x = 2$: $y = \frac{4}{1} = 4 > 3$, so the other branch is top-right
  8. Draw the bottom-left branch coming in just under $y = 3$ from the left, passing through $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ and plunging down beside $x = 1$; draw the top-right branch coming down beside $x = 1$ and levelling out just above $y = 3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Two branches with dashed asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 3$; the lower-left branch passes through $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$, the upper-right branch lies above $y = 3$ to the right of $x = 1$.

How the marks are earned

Marks are earned for two branches of the correct shape in the bottom-left and top-right regions, for the asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 3$ shown with their equations, and for the intercepts $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ labelled.

Calculation check

  • (3 × 0-2) ÷ (0-1) = 2.0
  • 3 × (2 ÷ 3)-2 = 0.0
  • (3 × 2-2) ÷ (2-1) = 4.0
  • (3 × (-1)-2) ÷ (-1-1) = 2.5
  • 2-3 = -1
