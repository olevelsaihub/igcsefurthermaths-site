Knowledge card

Finding stationary points

Calculation
  • A stationary (turning) point is where the gradient is zero: $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
  • Solve $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ for $x$, then substitute each $x$ into the equation of the curve to get $y$.
  • A cubic derivative can be factorised using a known root and the factor theorem.
  • For a quotient $\frac{u}{v}$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ exactly when the numerator $vu' - uv' = 0$.
  • For trig curves, solve the trig equation only in the stated interval and give exact values (e.g. $x = \frac{\pi}{6}$).

Before you begin

  • Differentiation rules, including the quotient rule.
  • Solving quadratics, the factor theorem, and simple trig equations.
  • Exact values of sin and cos.
1

The method

  1. Differentiate the curve.

    Why: Stationary points are defined by the derivative.

  2. Set $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ (for a fraction, set the numerator to 0).

    Why: A fraction is zero only when its numerator is zero.

  3. Solve: factorise a quadratic, use a known root for a cubic, or solve the trig equation in the interval.

    Why: Every solution is a stationary point; missing one loses marks.

  4. Substitute each $x$ into the original curve for the exact $y$-value.

    Why: Coordinates need $y$ from the curve, not from the derivative.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Substituting the $x$-values into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ (which gives 0) instead of into $y$.
  • ✗
    Missing a root, e.g. solving $x^2 = 4$ as $x = 2$ only.
  • ✗
    Giving decimals when exact coordinates are expected.
  • ✗
    Setting the whole quotient-rule expression (including the denominator) to zero and getting lost in algebra.

Finding stationary points · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 7 · part (a) · 5 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{x}{x^2 + 4}$. Using calculus, find the coordinates of the stationary points on $C$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Quotient rule with $u = x$, $v = x^2 + 4$: $u' = 1$, $v' = 2x$.
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(x^2 + 4)(1) - x(2x)}{(x^2 + 4)^2} = \frac{4 - x^2}{(x^2 + 4)^2}$
  3. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow 4 - x^2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$ or $x = -2$.
  4. $x = 2$: $y = \frac{2}{4 + 4} = \frac{1}{4}$.
  5. $x = -2$: $y = \frac{-2}{4 + 4} = -\frac{1}{4}$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\left(2, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ and $\left(-2, -\frac{1}{4}\right)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the quotient rule, accuracy for the simplified derivative, a method mark for setting the numerator to zero and solving, then accuracy for both $x$-values and for both coordinate pairs.

Calculation check

  • ((2 × × 2+4) × 1 - 2 × (2 × 2)) = 0
  • 2 ÷ (2 × × 2+4) = 0.25
  • -2 ÷ ((-2) × × 2+4) = -0.25
