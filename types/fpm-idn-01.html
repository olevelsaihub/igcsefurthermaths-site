Linear inequalities and the 'both' overlap

Solving a single inequality in which x appears only to the first power, including one with surd coefficients.

Type 3 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-01

★★☆☆☆ · 7 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Usually Question 1 or 2 of a paper: 'Find the set of values of x for which' followed by (a) an inequality where x appears only as x (not x squared), (b) a quadratic inequality and (c) 'both ... and ...'. The surd version says 'Without using a calculator, solve the inequality' and asks for a rationalised denominator.

5

The method

  1. Expand and collect

    Expand brackets, then gather the x terms on one side.

  2. Divide carefully

    If you divide by a negative number, reverse the sign.

  3. Surd check

    For a surd coefficient, check its sign before dividing; rationalise to finish.

  4. Overlap

    For a "both" part, write the overlap of the two sets.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Lost sign reversal

    Dividing by a negative needs the sign reversed.

  • ✗
    Union for both

    "Both" is the overlap, never all values in either set.

What a complete response includes

The simple linear part is worth 1 or 2 marks: a method mark for collecting terms correctly (when 2 marks) and an accuracy mark for the correct inequality. The surd version (4 marks) gives method marks for collecting and factorising out x, for dividing and for rationalising, then accuracy for the exact answer with the correct sign. The 'both' part is a single mark for the correct overlap, usually allowed to follow from the student's earlier answers.

Find the set of values of $x$ for which $2(3x-1)<4-3x$.

Worked explanation

  1. $2(3x-1)<4-3x$
  2. $6x-2<4-3x$ (expand the bracket)
  3. $6x+3x<4+2$ (add $3x$ and add $2$ to both sides)
  4. $9x<6$
  5. $x<\frac{6}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$ (divide by $9$, a positive number, so the sign stays the same)
  6. Check with $x=0$: $2(0-1)=-2<4$, true, and $0<\frac{2}{3}$ as expected.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x<\frac{2}{3}$

How the marks are earned

One method mark for correctly collecting the terms to reach $9x<6$ (or equivalent), and one accuracy mark for $x<\frac{2}{3}$. In part (c) of the same question this combines with $-\frac{1}{3}<x<3$ from part (b) to give the overlap $-\frac{1}{3}<x<\frac{2}{3}$.

Calculation check

  • 6 ÷ 9 = 0.6666666666666666
  • 2 × (3 × (2 ÷ 3)-1) = 2
  • 4-3 × (2 ÷ 3) = 2
  • 2 × (3 × 0-1) = -2
