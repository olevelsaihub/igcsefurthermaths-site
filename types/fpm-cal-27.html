Never at rest, and the least speed

Written

To show a particle is never at rest, show $v = 0$ has no real roots: $b^2 - 4ac < 0$. Completing the square also gives the least speed.

2

The calculation

$at^2 + bt + c = 0$ has no real roots when $b^2 - 4ac < 0$

Completing the square: $at^2 + bt + c = a\left(t + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + c - \frac{b^2}{4a}$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Reversing the inequality: $b^2 - 4ac > 0$ gives two roots, not none
  • A bare claim: no roots, with no working, scores nothing

Try itShow that $t^2 + 2t + 5 = 0$ has no real roots, and find the least value of $t^2 + 2t + 5$.

  1. $b^2 - 4ac = 4 - 20 = -16 < 0$, so no real roots
  2. $t^2 + 2t + 5 = (t + 1)^2 + 4 \geqslant 4$, least value $4$ at $t = -1$

5

The method

  1. Write v as a quadratic

    Put it in the form $at^2 + bt + c$.

  2. Choose a method

    Use the discriminant for no roots, or complete the square for a least value.

  3. Show the inequality

    $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, or the completed square is always positive.

  4. Conclude in words

    Say what this means for the particle.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reversed inequality

    $b^2 - 4ac > 0$ gives two roots, not none.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    Finish with a sentence about the particle.

Never at rest, and the least speed · January 2023 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 7 · part (d)(ii) · 3 marks

A particle $P$ is moving along the $x$-axis. At time $t$ seconds ($t \geqslant 0$) the velocity of $P$ is $v$ m/s where $v = t^2 - 10t + 28$. (d)(i) Show that there are no values of $t$ for which $P$ is instantaneously at rest. (ii) Find the least magnitude of the velocity of $P$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. (i) For $t^2 - 10t + 28 = 0$: $b^2 - 4ac = (-10)^2 - 4(1)(28) = 100 - 112 = -12 < 0$.
  2. So $v = 0$ has no real roots: $P$ is never instantaneously at rest.
  3. (ii) $v = t^2 - 10t + 28 = (t - 5)^2 - 25 + 28 = (t - 5)^2 + 3$.
  4. $(t - 5)^2 \geqslant 0$, so $v \geqslant 3$, with $v = 3$ when $t = 5$.
  5. Least magnitude of the velocity $= 3$ m/s.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Least magnitude of velocity $= 3$ m/s (at $t = 5$)

How the marks are earned

For (ii): a method mark for completing the square (or for setting $\frac{dv}{dt} = 2t - 10 = 0$ to get $t = 5$), a method mark for substituting, and accuracy for 3 m/s. Part (i) needs the negative discriminant and the conclusion.

Calculation check

  • (-10) × × 2 - 4 × 1 × 28 = -12
  • 5 × × 2 - 10 × 5 + 28 = 3
