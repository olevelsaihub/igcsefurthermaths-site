Quadratic inequalities

Rearranging an inequality into the form ax^2 + bx + c < 0 (or > 0), finding the critical values and choosing the correct region.

Type 7 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-02

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

An inequality containing $x^2$ (often part (b) of the inequality question), or 'find the set of values of x for which f(x) - k > 0', or a question asking where a curve lies above or below a line, or the range of a quantity such as r where a quadratic condition is given.

Quadratic inequalities

Calculation

Rearrange so one side is 0, find the critical values, then choose the inside or outside region from a sketch of the U-shape.

Check each region

3 steps to find

Which region is below the axis?

  1. Factorise the quadratic
  2. Critical values from the roots
  3. Inside for less than 0

Factorise the quadratic

Why
$(x - 3)(x + 2)$ expands back to $x^2 - x - 6$.

Critical values from the roots

Working
Set each bracket to 0: $x = 3$ and $x = -2$.

Inside for less than 0

Trap
$x < -2$ or $x > 3$ is the outside region.
0 of 3 found

3 steps to find

Which region is above the axis?

  1. Check the factors
  2. Roots from each bracket
  3. Two separate inequalities

Check the factors

Working
$2x^2 - 6x + x - 3$ gives the middle term.

Roots from each bracket

Working
$2x + 1 = 0$, so $x = -\frac{1}{2}$

Two separate inequalities

Trap
Never join the two outside regions into one inequality.
0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ gives $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

For $a > 0$: $ax^2 + bx + c < 0$ between the roots; $> 0$ outside them

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Choosing the inside when the outside is needed
  • Joining two outside regions into one inequality

Try itSolve $x^2 - 2x - 8 \geqslant 0$.

  1. $(x - 4)(x + 2) \geqslant 0$, so the critical values are $x = 4$ and $x = -2$
  2. Outside the roots: $x \leqslant -2$ or $x \geqslant 4$

5

The method

  1. Rearrange

    Move every term so one side is 0.

  2. Find the critical values

    Factorise, or use the formula.

  3. Sketch

    Draw the U-shape through the critical values.

  4. Choose the region

    Inside for less than 0, outside for greater than 0, then write the answer.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign on moving terms

    Change every sign when a term crosses the inequality sign.

  • ✗
    Joined outside regions

    Write two outside regions as two inequalities.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for rearranging to a three-term quadratic compared with zero, a method mark for solving it to find the critical values, a method mark for choosing the correct region (inside or outside), and an accuracy mark for the full correct set. The 3-mark versions combine the rearranging and solving marks.

Quadratic inequalities · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 1 · part (b) · 4 marks

Find the set of values of $x$ for which $3x^2-8x-3<0$.

Worked explanation

  1. Solve $3x^2-8x-3=0$ for the critical values.
  2. $3x^2-8x-3=(3x+1)(x-3)$ (check: $3x^2-9x+x-3=3x^2-8x-3$)
  3. $(3x+1)(x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=-\frac{1}{3}$ or $x=3$
  4. The coefficient of $x^2$ is $3>0$, so the graph is U-shaped and is below the $x$-axis between the roots.
  5. We need $<0$, so take the inside region: $-\frac{1}{3}<x<3$.
  6. Check with $x=0$: $3(0)-0-3=-3<0$, true, and $0$ is between $-\frac{1}{3}$ and $3$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$-\frac{1}{3}<x<3$

How the marks are earned

Marks come for factorising (or using the formula) correctly, for the critical values $-\frac{1}{3}$ and $3$, for choosing the inside region, and an accuracy mark for $-\frac{1}{3}<x<3$ written as a single interval.

Calculation check

  • 3 × 3 × × 2-8 × 3-3 = 0
  • round(3 × (-1 ÷ 3) × × 2-8 × (-1 ÷ 3)-3, 9) = 0
  • 3 × 0 × × 2-8 × 0-3 = -3
