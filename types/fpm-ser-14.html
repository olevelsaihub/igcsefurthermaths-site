Finding n in a geometric series with logarithms

Setting up an equation or inequality for a term or sum of a geometric series, isolating r^n and taking logarithms to find n.

Knowledge card

Finding n in a geometric series with logarithms

Calculation
  • The $n$th term is $u_n = ar^{n-1}$, where $a$ is the first term.
  • The sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ (or $\frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$), for $r \neq 1$.
  • If $|r| < 1$ the series converges and its sum to infinity is $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$.
  • To solve $r^n = k$ take logs: $n \log r = \log k$, so $n = \frac{\log k}{\log r}$.
  • If $0 < r < 1$ then $\log r < 0$, so dividing an inequality by $\log r$ reverses the inequality sign.
  • For the least $n$ round the decimal value up to the next whole number.

Before you begin

  • Laws of logarithms.
  • Rearranging inequalities, including multiplying or dividing by negatives.
  • Geometric sum formulas.
1

The method

  1. Write the term or sum with the formula, using the known $a$ and $r$.

    Why: Brings n into the expression as a power.

  2. Rearrange to get $r^n$ (or a power) on its own on one side.

    Why: Logs can only bring down a power that stands alone.

  3. Take logs of both sides and bring the power down.

    Why: $\log r^n = n \log r$.

  4. Divide by $\log r$, reversing the inequality if $\log r$ is negative.

    Why: Dividing by a negative number flips an inequality.

  5. Round to the correct integer and check with the values either side.

    Why: n counts terms, so it must be whole.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Not reversing the inequality when dividing by $\log r$ with $r < 1$.
  • ✗
    Taking logs before $r^n$ is isolated, e.g. writing $\log(1 - r^n)$ as $\log 1 - \log r^n$.
  • ✗
    Rounding down when the least n is wanted.
  • ✗
    Errors rearranging the sum formula to isolate r^n.

Finding n in a geometric series with logarithms · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 8 · part (d) · 4 marks

A geometric series $G$ has first term $320$ and common ratio $\frac{1}{4}$. The sum to $n$ terms of $G$ is $S_n$. (d) Find, using logarithms, the least value of $n$ such that $S_n > 426.6$

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $S_n = \frac{320\left(1 - \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^n\right)}{1 - \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{1280}{3}\left(1 - \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^n\right)$
  2. $\frac{1280}{3}\left(1 - \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^n\right) > 426.6$
  3. $1 - \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^n > \frac{3 \times 426.6}{1280} = \frac{1279.8}{1280}$
  4. $\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^n < \frac{0.2}{1280} = \frac{1}{6400}$
  5. $n \log 0.25 < \log \frac{1}{6400}$
  6. $\log 0.25 < 0$, so dividing reverses the sign: $n > \frac{\log(1/6400)}{\log 0.25} = 6.32\ldots$
  7. Least $n = 7$
  8. Check: $S_6 = \frac{1280}{3}\left(1 - \frac{1}{4096}\right) \approx 426.56 < 426.6$; $S_7 \approx 426.64 > 426.6$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$n = 7$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the sum formula with a = 320 and r = 1/4 set against 426.6, a method mark for rearranging to (1/4)^n < 1/6400, a method mark for taking logs and dividing correctly (with the sign reversed), and an accuracy mark for n = 7.

Calculation check

  • 0.2 ÷ 1280 = 0.00015625
  • round(1280 ÷ 3 × (1-0.25 × × 6),3) = 426.562
  • round(1280 ÷ 3 × (1-0.25 × × 7),3) = 426.641
  • 4 × × 6 = 4096
  • 4 × × 7 = 16384
