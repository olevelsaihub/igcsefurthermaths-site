Areas and perimeters of shapes made from sectors and triangles

Calculation

A segment is a sector minus a triangle: $\frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$ with $\theta$ in radians. Find each piece, then add or subtract.

Why
Arc length is $r\theta$ with $\theta$ in radians.

Scores

The calculation

Segment $= \frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$, with $\theta$ in radians

Triangle area $= \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, and the chord $c^2 = r^2 + r^2 - 2r^2\cos\theta$.

5

The method

  1. Sketch the pieces

    Split the region into sectors, triangles and segments you can name.

  2. Find each angle

    Use geometry, such as an equilateral triangle, to find each angle at the centre in radians.

  3. Work out each piece

    Use $\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ for sectors and $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ for triangles.

  4. Combine the pieces

    Add or subtract them, counting each piece the right number of times.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Degree mode

    A radian angle needs radian mode on the calculator.

  • ✗
    Subtract, not add

    The triangle inside the sector is taken away to leave the segment.

Areas and perimeters of shapes made from sectors and triangles · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 4 · part (whole) · 7 marks

Two circles $C_1$ and $C_2$ each have radius 6 cm. The centre $O_1$ of $C_1$ lies on $C_2$ and the centre $O_2$ of $C_2$ lies on $C_1$. The circles meet at $A$ and $B$ and enclose a region $R$ of area $P$ cm$^2$. Find the exact value of $P$ in the form $a\pi - b\sqrt{c}$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers.

Worked explanation

  1. $O_1A = O_1O_2 = O_2A = 6$, so triangle $O_1AO_2$ is equilateral and $\angle AO_1O_2 = \frac{\pi}{3}$. By symmetry $\angle AO_1B = \frac{2\pi}{3}$.
  2. Region $R$ is two equal segments, each cut off by chord $AB$.
  3. Sector $O_1AB = \frac{1}{2}(6^2)\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) = 12\pi$
  4. Triangle $O_1AB = \frac{1}{2}(6)(6)\sin\frac{2\pi}{3} = 18 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9\sqrt{3}$
  5. One segment $= 12\pi - 9\sqrt{3}$
  6. $P = 2(12\pi - 9\sqrt{3}) = 24\pi - 18\sqrt{3}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$P = 24\pi - 18\sqrt{3}$ (so $a = 24$, $b = 18$, $c = 3$), about $44.2$ cm$^2$

How the marks are earned

Marks are earned for finding the angle 2pi/3 (via the equilateral triangle), for the sector area, for the triangle area with the exact sine, for segment = sector minus triangle, for doubling, and accuracy for the exact final form.
