Velocity and displacement by integrating

Integrating acceleration to get velocity, and velocity to get displacement, using starting conditions for the constants.

Velocity and displacement by integrating

Calculation

Integrating reverses the chain $a \to v \to s$. Each integral adds a constant, and a starting condition fixes it: never assume it is zero.

The calculation

$v = \int a\,dt$ and $s = \int v\,dt$

Each integral adds a constant; use a condition from the question to find it.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Assuming $c = 0$ without a condition that shows it
  • Differentiating instead of integrating

Try itAcceleration is $a = 6t$ m/s$^2$, and $v = 1$ when $t = 0$. Find $v$ when $t = 2$.

  1. $v = \int 6t\,dt = 3t^2 + c$, and $v = 1$ at $t = 0$ gives $c = 1$
  2. At $t = 2$: $v = 12 + 1 = 13$ m/s

5

The method

  1. Integrate the given expression

    Move one step back along $a \to v \to s$.

  2. Add the constant

    Each integration adds $+c$ or $+d$.

  3. Use the condition

    Find each constant from the starting values.

  4. Substitute the time

    Put in the required time last.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Missing constant

    Forgetting a constant, or assuming it is zero.

  • ✗
    Wrong function

    Substituting into $v$ when $s$ is asked.

Velocity and displacement by integrating · January 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (c) · 4 marks

A particle $P$ is moving along the $x$-axis with velocity $v = 3t^2 - 23t + 30$ m/s at time $t$ seconds ($t \geqslant 0$). When $t = 0$, $P$ is at the point with coordinates $(d, 0)$. Given that, when $t = 8$, $P$ is at the point with coordinates $(26, 0)$, find the value of $d$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $x = \int(3t^2 - 23t + 30)\,dt = t^3 - \frac{23}{2}t^2 + 30t + c$
  2. At $t = 0$, $x = d$, so $c = d$.
  3. At $t = 8$, $x = 26$: $512 - \frac{23}{2}(64) + 240 + d = 26$
  4. $512 - 736 + 240 + d = 26 \Rightarrow 16 + d = 26 \Rightarrow d = 10$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$d = 10$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for integrating $v$, accuracy for the integrated terms, a method mark for using $t = 8$, $x = 26$ with the constant $d$, and accuracy for $d = 10$.

Calculation check

  • 8 × × 3 - 23 ÷ 2 × 8 × × 2 + 30 × 8 = 16.0
  • 26 - 16 = 10
