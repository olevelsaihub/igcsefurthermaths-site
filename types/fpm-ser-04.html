Writing a sigma sum as a formula in n

Recognising that a sum like the sum of (ar + b) from r = 1 to n is arithmetic and writing it as an expression in n.

The calculation

$\sum_{r=1}^{n}(pr+q)$ has $a = p+q$, $d = p$

Last term $pn + q$, so $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$

  • Using $n - 1$ terms instead of $n$

  2. $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(3 + 2n + 1) = n(n+2)$

5

The method

  1. List terms

    Put $r = 1, 2, 3$ into the expression.

  2. Find a, d and last term

    $a$ is the first term, $d$ the coefficient of $r$.

  3. Use the sum formula

    $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$ with $n$ terms.

  4. Simplify

    Expand and finish with = RHS.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong first term

    $q$ is not the first term: use $p + q$.

  • ✗
    Skipped lines

    In a show that, write every line.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for identifying the series as arithmetic with correct first term and common difference (or first and last terms), a method mark for using a correct sum formula with these, and an accuracy mark for reaching the given form with no errors. When constants are to be found, further method marks are for comparing coefficients and accuracy marks for each constant.

(a) Show that $\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$

Worked explanation

  1. $r = 1$: $5(1) - 3 = 2$; $r = 2$: $5(2) - 3 = 7$; $r = 3$: $12$
  2. So the series is arithmetic with $a = 2$ and $d = 5$, and there are $n$ terms.
  3. $\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}[2(2) + (n-1)(5)]$
  4. $= \frac{n}{2}[4 + 5n - 5]$
  5. $= \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$, as required.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a = 2 and d = 5 (or first term 2 and last term 5n - 3), a method mark for putting them into a correct sum formula, and an accuracy mark for simplifying to the printed expression with every step shown.

Calculation check

  • 2+7+12+17 = 38
  • 4 ÷ 2 × (5 × 4-1) = 38
  • 2+7+12+17+22 = 60
  • 5 ÷ 2 × (5 × 5-1) = 60
