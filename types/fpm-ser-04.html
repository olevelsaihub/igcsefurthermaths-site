Writing a sigma sum as a formula in n

Recognising that a sum like the sum of (ar + b) from r = 1 to n is arithmetic and writing it as an expression in n.

Knowledge card

Writing a sigma sum as a formula in n

Written
  • $\sum_{r=1}^{n} f(r)$ means add $f(1) + f(2) + \dots + f(n)$.
  • If $f(r)$ is linear, $pr + q$, the sum is arithmetic with first term $p + q$ and common difference $p$.
  • There are $n$ terms from $r = 1$ to $r = n$, and the last term is $pn + q$.
  • $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$ or $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(\text{first} + \text{last})$.
  • To find unknown constants, expand both sides and compare the coefficients of $n^2$ and of $n$.

Before you begin

  • Sigma notation.
  • Expanding and simplifying brackets.
  • Comparing coefficients of a polynomial identity.
1

The method

  1. Write out the first two or three terms by putting $r = 1, 2, 3$.

    Why: Seeing the terms shows it is arithmetic and gives $a$ and $d$.

  2. State $a$ (the $r = 1$ term) and $d$ (the coefficient of $r$), and note the last term ($r = n$).

    Why: These are what go into the sum formula.

  3. Substitute into $\frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$ or $\frac{n}{2}(a + l)$.

    Why: There are exactly $n$ terms.

  4. Expand and simplify inside the bracket to reach the required form.

    Why: In a show-that the final line must match the given expression exactly.

  5. If constants are unknown, compare coefficients of $n^2$ and $n$ with the given formula and solve.

    Why: Two expressions in $n$ that are equal for every $n$ must have the same coefficients.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Taking the first term as the constant $q$ instead of the value at $r = 1$, $p + q$.
  • ✗
    Forgetting the $\frac{n}{2}$ or using $\frac{n}{2}(a + l)$ with a wrong last term.
  • ✗
    Using $n - 1$ terms instead of $n$.
  • ✗
    In a show-that, jumping to the printed answer without the simplification lines.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for identifying the series as arithmetic with correct first term and common difference (or first and last terms), a method mark for using a correct sum formula with these, and an accuracy mark for reaching the given form with no errors. When constants are to be found, further method marks are for comparing coefficients and accuracy marks for each constant.

Writing a sigma sum as a formula in n · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 3 · part (a) · 3 marks

(a) Show that $\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $r = 1$: $5(1) - 3 = 2$; $r = 2$: $5(2) - 3 = 7$; $r = 3$: $12$
  2. So the series is arithmetic with $a = 2$ and $d = 5$, and there are $n$ terms.
  3. $\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}[2(2) + (n-1)(5)]$
  4. $= \frac{n}{2}[4 + 5n - 5]$
  5. $= \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$, as required.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a = 2 and d = 5 (or first term 2 and last term 5n - 3), a method mark for putting them into a correct sum formula, and an accuracy mark for simplifying to the printed expression with every step shown.

Calculation check

  • 2+7+12+17 = 38
  • 4 ÷ 2 × (5 × 4-1) = 38
  • 2+7+12+17+22 = 60
  • 5 ÷ 2 × (5 × 5-1) = 60
