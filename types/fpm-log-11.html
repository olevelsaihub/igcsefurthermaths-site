Log equations that turn into a quadratic

Solving equations that mix log_a x and log_x a (or other bases) by changing base so they become a quadratic in one log.

Type 5 1 Logarithms, indices and surds FPM-LOG-11

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

An equation containing both $\log_a x$ and $\log_x a$ (the unknown appears as a base), or two logs whose change of base leaves $\log x$ on the bottom of a fraction, e.g. $\log_3 x - 2\log_x 3 = 1$ or $5\log_q 16 + 4\log_2 q = 24$. Usually 5 to 8 marks.

Log equations that turn into a quadratic

Calculation

For an equation that mixes $\log_a x$ and $\log_x a$, use the reciprocal rule to write everything with one log, let $u$ stand for it, and solve the quadratic in $u$.

Which step uses the reciprocal rule?

  1. Reciprocal, not the same log
  2. Multiply every term by u
  3. Index form gives x

Reciprocal, not the same log

Trap
$\log_x 2$ is not $\log_2 x$; it is $\frac{1}{\log_2 x}$.

Multiply every term by u

Why
The 3 becomes 3u, so every term needs the factor u.

Index form gives x

Working
$u = 2$ gives $x = 2^2 = 4$, not $2 \times 2$.

2 steps to find

Which quadratic results?

  1. Multiply by 2u
  2. Index form again

Multiply by 2u

Why
Multiplying by $2u$ gives $2u^2 + 2 = 5u$, then move $5u$ across.

Index form again

Why
$u = \frac{1}{2}$ gives $x = 3^{1/2} = \sqrt{3}$.

The calculation

$\log_x a = \frac{1}{\log_a x}$; with $u = \log_a x$, $x = a^u$

  • Missing the reciprocal: $\log_x 3 \neq \log_3 x$
  • Stopping at u without converting back to x

Try itSolve $\log_5 x + 3\log_x 5 = 4$.

  1. $u = \log_5 x$: $u + \frac{3}{u} = 4$, so $u^2 - 4u + 3 = 0$
  2. $u = 1$ or $3$, so $x = 5$ or $x = 125$

5

The method

  1. Use the reciprocal

    Use $\log_x a = \frac{1}{\log_a x}$ so every log has one base.

  2. Substitute

    Let $u = \log_a x$, so the equation is in $u$ only.

  3. Clear fractions

    Multiply every term by u (or by the denominator).

  4. Solve the quadratic

    Factorise, or use the formula.

  5. Convert back

    Use $x = a^u$ for each value of $u$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Confusing the reciprocal

    $\log_x a$ is not $\log_a x$.

  • ✗
    Dropping a value of u

    A negative u is valid: check each value.

What a complete response includes

Method marks for each correct change of base, for forming a three-term quadratic in the log, for solving it, and for converting back to x by index form; accuracy marks for the correct quadratic and for each value of x. Answers left as values of the log earn only the method marks.

Solve the equation $\log_3 x - 2\log_x 3 = 1$.

Worked explanation

  1. Use $\log_x 3 = \frac{1}{\log_3 x}$ and let $u = \log_3 x$:
  2. $u - \frac{2}{u} = 1$
  3. Multiply by $u$: $u^2 - 2 = u$, so $u^2 - u - 2 = 0$
  4. $(u - 2)(u + 1) = 0$, so $u = 2$ or $u = -1$
  5. $\log_3 x = 2 \Rightarrow x = 3^2 = 9$
  6. $\log_3 x = -1 \Rightarrow x = 3^{-1} = \frac{1}{3}$
  7. Check $x = 9$: $\log_3 9 - 2\log_9 3 = 2 - 2 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1$; check $x = \frac{1}{3}$: $-1 - 2 \times (-1) = 1$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x = 9$ or $x = \frac{1}{3}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the reciprocal rule $\log_x 3 = \frac{1}{\log_3 x}$, a method mark for multiplying through to form a three-term quadratic, an accuracy mark for $u^2 - u - 2 = 0$, a method mark for solving it, a method mark for converting a value of $u$ back to $x$ by index form, and accuracy marks for $x = 9$ and $x = \frac{1}{3}$.

Calculation check

  • 2 × × 2-2-2 = 0
  • (-1) × × 2-(-1)-2 = 0
  • 3 × × 2 = 9
  • 3 × × -1 = 0.3333333333333333
  • 2-2 × (1 ÷ 2) = 1
  • -1-2 × (-1) = 1
