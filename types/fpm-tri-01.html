Reading a sine model: values, biggest and smallest

Reading off values of a sinusoidal model or graph by substituting and using exact values and the range of sine and cosine.

Type 3 10 Trigonometry FPM-TRI-01

★☆☆☆☆ · 1 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

A formula such as $P = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right)$ is given and you are asked for the exact value at a given $t$, or the largest or smallest value of the model; or a sketch of a cosine curve asks where it crosses the axis.

Knowledge card

Reading a sine model: values, biggest and smallest

Calculation

For $P = a + b\sin(kt)$ with $b > 0$, the greatest value is $a + b$ and the least is $a - b$. Put the sine equal to $1$ or $-1$, then check that $t$ is in range.

Why
With $-3$ in front, $\sin = -1$ gives the greatest $P$.

Why

The calculation

$a - b \le P \le a + b$ when $b > 0$, because $-1 \le \sin(kt) \le 1$

The sine is at most $1$ and at least $-1$ for every angle.

5

The method

  1. Read the sign of b

    Check whether the coefficient $b$ is positive or negative before choosing $\pm 1$.

  2. Replace the sine

    Put $\sin(kt) = 1$ and $\sin(kt) = -1$ into the model, one at a time.

  3. Evaluate each value

    Work out $a + b\sin(kt)$ for each case, keeping exact values.

  4. Check the range

    Solve for $t$ and confirm the extreme is reached inside the interval.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Degree mode

    Angles with $\pi$ are radians, so use radian mode.

  • ✗
    Ignoring the sign of b

    With $b = -3$ the greatest value comes from $\sin = -1$.

$P = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right)$, $0 \le t \le 12$. (b) Find (i) the largest value of $P$, (ii) the smallest value of $P$.

Worked explanation

  1. The sine term satisfies $-1 \le \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) \le 1$.
  2. Largest: $\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) = 1$ gives $P = 3 + 2(1) = 5$ (reached at $t = 1.5$, since $\frac{\pi(1.5)}{3} = \frac{\pi}{2}$).
  3. Smallest: $\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) = -1$ gives $P = 3 + 2(-1) = 1$.
  4. Check it is reached: $\frac{\pi t}{3} = \frac{3\pi}{2}$ gives $t = 4.5$, which is in $0 \le t \le 12$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

(i) Largest value $P = 5$; (ii) smallest value $P = 1$

How the marks are earned

One mark is typically given for each correct value, obtained by using sine equal to 1 and to -1.

Calculation check

  • 3+2 × 1 = 5
  • 3+2 × (-1) = 1
  • 3 × 1.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5
