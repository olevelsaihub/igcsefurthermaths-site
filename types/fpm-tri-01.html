Reading a sine model: values, biggest and smallest

Reading off values of a sinusoidal model or graph by substituting and using exact values and the range of sine and cosine.

Type 3 10 Trigonometry FPM-TRI-01

★☆☆☆☆ · 1 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

A formula such as $P = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right)$ is given and you are asked for the exact value at a given $t$, or the largest or smallest value of the model; or a sketch of a cosine curve asks where it crosses the axis.

Knowledge card

Reading a sine model: values, biggest and smallest

Calculation
  • For every angle, $-1 \le \sin\theta \le 1$ and $-1 \le \cos\theta \le 1$.
  • For $P = a + b\sin(kt)$ with $b > 0$: the greatest value is $a + b$ (when $\sin(kt) = 1$) and the least value is $a - b$ (when $\sin(kt) = -1$).
  • If the coefficient $b$ is negative, the greatest value comes from $\sin(kt) = -1$ instead.
  • Exact values: $\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, $\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\pi$ radians $= 180^\circ$.
  • Sine repeats every $2\pi$: $\sin(\theta - 2\pi) = \sin\theta$, and $\sin(\pi + \alpha) = -\sin\alpha$.
  • $\cos x = 0$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$, so the graph of $y = \cos x$ first crosses the positive $x$-axis there.

Before you begin

  • Radian measure and converting between radians and degrees.
  • The shapes of the sine and cosine graphs.
  • Exact trig values of 30, 45 and 60 degrees.
  • Simple surds.
1

The method

  1. Check whether the angle inside the sine is in radians (it contains $\pi$ or the question says so).

    Why: Putting a radian angle into degree thinking gives completely wrong values.

  2. For a value, substitute $t$, simplify the angle, and reduce it to a standard angle using the symmetry of the sine graph.

    Why: Exact values are only known for angles related to $\frac{\pi}{6}$, $\frac{\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{\pi}{3}$.

  3. For greatest or least, replace the sine term by $1$ and by $-1$ and work out the model each time.

    Why: Sine can never go beyond these limits, so these give the extreme values.

  4. Check the extreme is actually reached inside the given range of $t$.

    Why: If the interval is short, the curve may not reach $1$ or $-1$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using degree mode on a calculator for an angle in radians.
  • ✗
    Writing the least value as $a - 1$ instead of $a - b$ (forgetting the coefficient $b$).
  • ✗
    Getting the sign of $\sin\frac{4\pi}{3}$ wrong when reducing a large angle.
  • ✗
    Swapping greatest and least when the coefficient of sine is negative.
  • ✗
    Giving a decimal when an exact answer is asked for.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Reading a sine model: values, biggest and smallest · November 2020 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 1 · part (b)(ii) · 2 marks

$P = 3 + 2\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right)$, $0 \le t \le 12$. (b) Find (i) the largest value of $P$, (ii) the smallest value of $P$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The sine term satisfies $-1 \le \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) \le 1$.
  2. Largest: $\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) = 1$ gives $P = 3 + 2(1) = 5$ (reached at $t = 1.5$, since $\frac{\pi(1.5)}{3} = \frac{\pi}{2}$).
  3. Smallest: $\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{3}\right) = -1$ gives $P = 3 + 2(-1) = 1$.
  4. Check it is reached: $\frac{\pi t}{3} = \frac{3\pi}{2}$ gives $t = 4.5$, which is in $0 \le t \le 12$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

(i) Largest value $P = 5$; (ii) smallest value $P = 1$

How the marks are earned

One mark is typically given for each correct value, obtained by using sine equal to 1 and to -1.

Calculation check

  • 3+2 × 1 = 5
  • 3+2 × (-1) = 1
  • 3 × 1.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5
