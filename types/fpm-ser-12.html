Knowledge card

Does a geometric series have a sum to infinity?

Written
  • A geometric series converges (has a sum to infinity) exactly when $|r| < 1$, that is $-1 < r < 1$.
  • If $|r| > 1$ the terms grow in size and the series has no sum to infinity.
  • $|r|$ means the size of $r$ ignoring its sign: $\left|-\frac{1}{2}\right| = \frac{1}{2}$.
  • Find $r$ by dividing a term by the one before it.

Before you begin

  • Finding a common ratio.
  • Modulus (absolute value).
  • Comparing surds and fractions with 1.
1

The method

  1. Find or quote the common ratio $r$.

    Why: Convergence depends only on r.

  2. Compare $|r|$ with 1.

    Why: This is the test for a geometric series.

  3. Write a clear conclusion in words, e.g. \"$|r| = \frac{1}{2} < 1$, so the series is convergent\".

    Why: The mark needs the statement, not just the number.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing $r < 1$ instead of $|r| < 1$ (a ratio of $-2$ is less than 1 but the series diverges).
  • ✗
    Giving the value of r with no conclusion.
  • ✗
    Using the wrong value of an unknown, giving the wrong r.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Show that1Find $r$ with working, then state $|r| < 1$ and conclude that the series converges.
Explain3One clear sentence quoting $|r|$ and its comparison with 1, e.g. \"$|r| = \sqrt{2} > 1$ so there is no sum to infinity\".

What a complete response includes

Usually one mark for a correct value of r and one for the statement that its modulus is less than (or greater than) 1 with the conclusion; one-mark parts need the statement with a correct reason.

Does a geometric series have a sum to infinity? · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 7 · part (b) · 2 marks

A geometric series has first term $(x - 3)$, second term $(x + 1)$ and third term $(4x - 2)$. The possible values of $x$ are $5$ and $\frac{1}{3}$. Given that $x < 1$, (b) show that the series is convergent.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. From (a): $(x + 1)^2 = (x - 3)(4x - 2) \Rightarrow 3x^2 - 16x + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow (3x - 1)(x - 5) = 0$
  2. $x < 1$, so $x = \frac{1}{3}$
  3. First term $\frac{1}{3} - 3 = -\frac{8}{3}$; second term $\frac{1}{3} + 1 = \frac{4}{3}$
  4. $r = \frac{4/3}{-8/3} = -\frac{1}{2}$
  5. $|r| = \frac{1}{2} < 1$, so the series is convergent.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$r = -\frac{1}{2}$ and $|r| < 1$, so the series converges.

How the marks are earned

One mark for finding r = -1/2 using x = 1/3, and one mark for stating that |r| < 1 so the series is convergent.

Calculation check

  • (1 ÷ 3+1) ÷ (1 ÷ 3-3) = -0.5
  • (4 × (1 ÷ 3)-2) ÷ (1 ÷ 3+1) = -0.5
  • 3 × 1 × × 2-16 × 1 × 3+5 × 9 = 0
