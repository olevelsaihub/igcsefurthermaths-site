Connected rates: linking two changing quantities

Using a known rate of one quantity (for example volume) to find the rate of another (for example surface area) through a shared variable.

Type 16 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-15

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The rate of one quantity is given (e.g. surface area increasing at 0.45 cm$^2$/s) and the rate of a different quantity of the same object is asked (e.g. the volume).

Connected rates: linking two changing quantities

Calculation

Two quantities that share a variable are linked through it: $\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt}$, where $\frac{dx}{dt}$ comes from the given rate.

The calculation

$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt}$, where $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dV/dt}{dV/dx}$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Inverting the wrong derivative: $\frac{dx}{dt}$ is $\frac{dV/dt}{dV/dx}$
  • Using the value of A as the length x

Try itA cube has $V = x^3$. When $x = 3$, $\frac{dV}{dt} = 27$ cm³/s. Find $\frac{dx}{dt}$.

  1. $\frac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2 = 27$ when $x = 3$
  2. $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{27}{27} = 1$ cm/s

5

The method

  1. One variable

    Write $V$ and $A$ in terms of the shared variable $x$.

  2. Differentiate each

    Find $\frac{dV}{dx}$ and $\frac{dA}{dx}$.

  3. Find dx/dt

    Use $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dV/dt}{dV/dx}$ at the instant.

  4. Chain to the answer

    Multiply $\frac{dA}{dx}$ by $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and give the units.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong inversion

    Divide the given rate by the derivative of the quantity you are given.

  • ✗
    Area used as length

    Solve for $x$ first: $6x^2 = 150$ gives $x = 5$, not $150$.

Connected rates: linking two changing quantities · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (whole) · 7 marks

A cube has edges of length $x$ cm. The total surface area, $A$ cm$^2$, of the cube is increasing at a constant rate of 0.45 cm$^2$/s. Find the rate of increase, in cm$^3$/s, of the volume of the cube at the instant when the total surface area of the cube is 384 cm$^2$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $A = 6x^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dA}{dx} = 12x$; $V = x^3 \Rightarrow \frac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2$.
  2. When $A = 384$: $6x^2 = 384 \Rightarrow x^2 = 64 \Rightarrow x = 8$.
  3. $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dA/dt}{dA/dx} = \frac{0.45}{12 \times 8} = \frac{0.45}{96} = 0.0046875$
  4. $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt} = 3(8)^2 \times 0.0046875 = 192 \times 0.0046875 = 0.9$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{dV}{dt} = 0.9$ cm$^3$/s

How the marks are earned

Marks for $\frac{dA}{dx} = 12x$ and $\frac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2$, a method mark for $x = 8$ from $6x^2 = 384$, a method mark for $\frac{dx}{dt}$ from the given rate, a method mark for multiplying by $\frac{dV}{dx}$, and accuracy for 0.9.

Calculation check

  • (384 ÷ 6) × × 0.5 = 8.0
  • 0.45 ÷ (12 × 8) = 0.0046875
  • 3 × 8 × × 2 × 0.45 ÷ (12 × 8) = 0.9
