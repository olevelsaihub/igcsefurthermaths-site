Connected rates: linking two changing quantities

Using a known rate of one quantity (for example volume) to find the rate of another (for example surface area) through a shared variable.

Type 16 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-15

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The rate of one quantity is given (e.g. surface area increasing at 0.45 cm$^2$/s) and the rate of a different quantity of the same object is asked (e.g. the volume).

Knowledge card

Connected rates: linking two changing quantities

Calculation
  • When two quantities depend on the same variable, link them through it: $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt}$.
  • A typical two-step chain: $\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dA}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt}$ where $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dA/dt}{dA/dx}$ comes from the given rate.
  • Cube of edge $x$: $V = x^3$, $A = 6x^2$. Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, $S = 4\pi r^2$.
  • Find the variable's value at the instant from the information given (e.g. $6x^2 = 384$ gives $x = 8$).
  • Units of the final rate follow the quantity asked for (e.g. cm$^3$/s).

Before you begin

  • One-link rates of change (FPM-CAL-14).
  • Surface area and volume formulae.
  • Solving simple equations for the variable at the instant.
1

The method

  1. Write both quantities in terms of the same variable.

    Why: The shared variable is the bridge between the two rates.

  2. Differentiate each with respect to that variable.

    Why: Each derivative is one link of the chain.

  3. Use the given rate to find the variable's rate, e.g. $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dA/dt}{dA/dx}$.

    Why: This is the first link.

  4. Multiply by the derivative of the required quantity and evaluate at the instant.

    Why: This is the second link, giving the answer.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Inverting the wrong derivative in the chain.
  • ✗
    Using the given area (384) as the edge length instead of solving $6x^2 = 384$.
  • ✗
    Mixing up which rate is given and which is required.
  • ✗
    Rounding the intermediate rate too early.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Connected rates: linking two changing quantities · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (whole) · 7 marks

A cube has edges of length $x$ cm. The total surface area, $A$ cm$^2$, of the cube is increasing at a constant rate of 0.45 cm$^2$/s. Find the rate of increase, in cm$^3$/s, of the volume of the cube at the instant when the total surface area of the cube is 384 cm$^2$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $A = 6x^2 \Rightarrow \frac{dA}{dx} = 12x$; $V = x^3 \Rightarrow \frac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2$.
  2. When $A = 384$: $6x^2 = 384 \Rightarrow x^2 = 64 \Rightarrow x = 8$.
  3. $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dA/dt}{dA/dx} = \frac{0.45}{12 \times 8} = \frac{0.45}{96} = 0.0046875$
  4. $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dx} \times \frac{dx}{dt} = 3(8)^2 \times 0.0046875 = 192 \times 0.0046875 = 0.9$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{dV}{dt} = 0.9$ cm$^3$/s

How the marks are earned

Marks for $\frac{dA}{dx} = 12x$ and $\frac{dV}{dx} = 3x^2$, a method mark for $x = 8$ from $6x^2 = 384$, a method mark for $\frac{dx}{dt}$ from the given rate, a method mark for multiplying by $\frac{dV}{dx}$, and accuracy for 0.9.

Calculation check

  • (384 ÷ 6) × × 0.5 = 8.0
  • 0.45 ÷ (12 × 8) = 0.0046875
  • 3 × 8 × × 2 × 0.45 ÷ (12 × 8) = 0.9
