Maximum or minimum? Using the second derivative

Calculation

At a stationary point, the sign of $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ decides the nature: positive gives a minimum and negative gives a maximum.

The calculation

$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} > 0 \Rightarrow$ minimum; $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} < 0 \Rightarrow$ maximum

  • Rule reversed: a positive second derivative is a minimum
  • Testing with $\frac{dy}{dx}$ instead of $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$

Try itShow that $y = x^2 - 6x + 10$ has a minimum at $x = 3$.

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 6 = 0$ at $x = 3$, with $y = 1$
  2. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 2 > 0$, so $(3, 1)$ is a minimum

Exam question

The curve $y = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$ has stationary points at $x = 0$ and $x = 2$. Determine whether each stationary point is a maximum or a minimum. (3 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

5

The method

  1. Differentiate twice

    Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$, then $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$.

  2. Show it is stationary

    Substitute $x$ into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ to show it is $0$.

  3. Read the sign

    Substitute $x$ into $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ and read its sign.

  4. Conclude with the reason

    Write maximum or minimum, quoting the value or sign.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Rule reversed

    Positive means minimum, negative means maximum.

  • ✗
    Wrong derivative

    Classify with $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, not with $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Maximum or minimum? Using the second derivative · June 2021 · Paper 1 · Question 7 · part (c) · 2 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y = \frac{x}{x^2 + 4}$, with stationary points $\left(2, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ and $\left(-2, -\frac{1}{4}\right)$. Given that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{2x(x^2 - 12)}{(x^2 + 4)^3}$, determine the nature of each of these stationary points.

Worked explanation

  1. At $x = 2$: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{2(2)(4 - 12)}{(4 + 4)^3} = \frac{-32}{512} = -\frac{1}{16} < 0$.
  2. So $\left(2, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ is a maximum.
  3. At $x = -2$: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{2(-2)(4 - 12)}{(4 + 4)^3} = \frac{32}{512} = \frac{1}{16} > 0$.
  4. So $\left(-2, -\frac{1}{4}\right)$ is a minimum.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\left(2, \frac{1}{4}\right)$ is a maximum; $\left(-2, -\frac{1}{4}\right)$ is a minimum.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for substituting both $x$-values into the second derivative, and an accuracy mark for both correct conclusions supported by the signs.
