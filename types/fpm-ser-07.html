Knowledge card

Least or greatest number of terms for an arithmetic sum

Calculation
  • An arithmetic series goes up (or down) by the same amount each time: the common difference $d$.
  • The $n$th term is $u_n = a + (n-1)d$, where $a$ is the first term.
  • The sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$, or $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$ where $l$ is the last term.
  • An inequality such as $S_n > 220$ becomes a quadratic inequality in $n$.
  • Solve the matching quadratic equation to find the critical value, usually a decimal.
  • For the least $n$ round the critical value up; for the greatest $n$ round it down.
  • Check by working out $S_n$ for the integer on either side of the critical value.

Before you begin

  • Solving quadratic equations with the formula.
  • Quadratic inequalities.
  • Arithmetic series formulas.
1

The method

  1. Find $a$ and $d$ if needed, then write $S_n$ as a quadratic in $n$.

    Why: The inequality must be in one unknown.

  2. Write the inequality and rearrange so one side is zero.

    Why: This puts it in a form you can solve.

  3. Solve the related equation with the quadratic formula to get the critical value.

    Why: The sum crosses the target value at this point.

  4. Round the positive critical value in the right direction to a whole number.

    Why: n must be an integer; least means just past the critical value, greatest means just before it.

  5. Check $S_n$ for that integer and the one next to it.

    Why: Confirms the choice and secures the final mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Giving the decimal critical value as the answer.
  • ✗
    Rounding the wrong way (down for a least value).
  • ✗
    Sign errors when the common difference is negative or when multiplying out.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to multiply both sides of the inequality by 2 when clearing the fraction.

Least or greatest number of terms for an arithmetic sum · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 2 · part (whole) · 7 marks

An arithmetic series $F$ has third term $8$ and fifth term $20$. Given that the sum to $n$ terms of $F$ is greater than $220$, find the least value of $n$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $a + 2d = 8$ and $a + 4d = 20$
  2. Subtract: $2d = 12 \Rightarrow d = 6$, so $a = 8 - 12 = -4$
  3. $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2(-4) + (n-1)6] = \frac{n}{2}(6n - 14) = n(3n - 7)$
  4. $n(3n - 7) > 220 \Rightarrow 3n^2 - 7n - 220 > 0$
  5. Solve $3n^2 - 7n - 220 = 0$: $n = \frac{7 + \sqrt{49 + 2640}}{6} = \frac{7 + \sqrt{2689}}{6} \approx 9.81$ (the other root is negative)
  6. The least integer above $9.81$ is $10$
  7. Check: $S_9 = 9(27 - 7) = 180$, which is not more than $220$; $S_{10} = 10(30 - 7) = 230 > 220$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$n = 10$

How the marks are earned

Method marks for the two term equations and solving them for a and d, a method mark for the sum formula with their values, a method mark for forming the inequality (or equation) in n, a method mark for solving the quadratic, and accuracy marks for the critical value and the final answer n = 10.

Calculation check

  • round((7+(49+2640) × × 0.5) ÷ 6,3) = 9.809
  • 9 × (3 × 9-7) = 180
  • 10 × (3 × 10-7) = 230
