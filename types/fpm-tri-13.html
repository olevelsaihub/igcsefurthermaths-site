Knowledge card

Lengths in a 3D solid using right-angled triangles

Calculation
  • Pythagoras: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ in any right-angled triangle, including ones drawn inside a solid.
  • Diagonal of a square of side $s$ is $s\sqrt{2}$; of a rectangle $a \times b$ is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$; of a cuboid is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$.
  • In a right pyramid the vertex is directly above the centre of the base, where the diagonals meet.
  • Height, half-diagonal and sloping edge form a right-angled triangle; height, half-side and slant height of a face form another.
  • SOHCAHTOA with exact values, e.g. $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, $\cos 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
  • Simplify surds: $\sqrt{126} = 3\sqrt{14}$.

Before you begin

  • Pythagoras' theorem.
  • Right-angled trigonometry.
  • Surd simplification.
  • Properties of squares, rectangles and isosceles triangles.
1

The method

  1. Find a right-angled triangle that contains the length you want and sketch it flat.

    Why: Pythagoras and SOHCAHTOA only work in right-angled triangles.

  2. Work out any base lengths first, e.g. half the base diagonal or the distance to the midpoint of an edge.

    Why: These are usually one of the sides of the vertical triangle.

  3. Apply Pythagoras or the trig ratio with the given angle.

    Why: This gives the length.

  4. Keep surds exact and simplify if an exact value or a 'show that' is asked.

    Why: Rounded values cannot reach a printed surd.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using the full base diagonal instead of half of it.
  • ✗
    Using a triangle that is not right-angled.
  • ✗
    Treating a sloping edge as the vertical height.
  • ✗
    Rounding a surd and so failing to reach an exact given answer.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for the base length (such as half a diagonal), a method mark for Pythagoras or the trig ratio in the correct triangle, and an accuracy mark for the length; a show-that needs the exact working to the given value.

Lengths in a 3D solid using right-angled triangles · November 2025 · Paper 2 · Question 11 · part (a) · 3 marks

$ABCDE$ is a right pyramid with vertex $E$ and horizontal square base $ABCD$ of side 6 cm. $EA = EB = EC = ED = 12$ cm and the vertical height is $h$ cm. (a) Show that $h = 3\sqrt{14}$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Diagonal $AC = \sqrt{6^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$
  2. Let $O$ be the centre of the base (below $E$). $AO = \frac{1}{2}AC = 3\sqrt{2}$
  3. Triangle $EOA$ is right-angled at $O$: $h^2 = EA^2 - AO^2 = 144 - 18 = 126$
  4. $h = \sqrt{126} = \sqrt{9 \times 14} = 3\sqrt{14}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$h = 3\sqrt{14}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for half the base diagonal, a method mark for Pythagoras in triangle EOA, and the accuracy mark for reaching 3 root 14 with the surd simplification shown.

Calculation check

  • (6 × × 2+6 × × 2) × × 0.5 = 8.48528137423857
  • 12 × × 2-(3 × 2 × × 0.5) × × 2 = 126.0
  • 126 × × 0.5 = 11.224972160321824
  • 3 × 14 × × 0.5 = 11.224972160321824
