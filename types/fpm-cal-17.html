Integrating trig functions using identities

Rewriting trig products or powers as sums of sin(ax) and cos(ax) with identities, then integrating and evaluating exactly.

Type 19 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-17

★★★☆☆ · 9 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

A definite integral of a trig product or power (such as $\int 6\sin 7x\cos x\,dx$ or $\int \cos^4 A\,dA$), usually after a 'show that' identity part, with 'find the exact value' or 'use calculus to evaluate'.

The calculation

$\int \sin ax\,dx = -\frac{1}{a}\cos ax + c$

Also $\int \cos ax\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\sin ax + c$ and $2\sin A\cos B = \sin(A+B) + \sin(A-B)$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Missing the $\frac{1}{a}$: $\int \cos 4x\,dx$ is $\frac{1}{4}\sin 4x$, not $\sin 4x$
  • Integrating a product term by term: $\int \sin 3x\cos x\,dx$ is not $\int \sin 3x\,dx \times \int \cos x\,dx$

Try itEvaluate $\int_0^{\pi/3}\cos^2 x\,dx$ exactly.

  1. $\cos^2 x = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x)$, so the integrand is $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cos 2x$
  2. $= \left[\frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sin 2x}{4}\right]_0^{\pi/3} = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}$

5

The method

  1. Spot the form

    A product of sine and cosine, or a square, cannot be integrated directly.

  2. Rewrite with an identity

    Use product-to-sum, or $\sin^2 x = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2x)$.

  3. Integrate each term

    Divide each term by the multiple of x.

  4. Use exact limits

    Upper minus lower, with exact values such as $\sin\pi = 0$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Missing the 1/a

    $\int \cos 4x\,dx = \frac{1}{4}\sin 4x$, not $\sin 4x$.

  • ✗
    Integrating the square

    $\int \sin^2 x\,dx \neq \frac{1}{3}\sin^3 x$.

Given that $2\sin 7x\cos x = \sin 8x + \sin 6x$, use calculus to evaluate $\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{4}} (6\sin 7x\cos x)\,dx$.

Final-value check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $6\sin 7x\cos x = 3(2\sin 7x\cos x) = 3(\sin 8x + \sin 6x)$
  2. $\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{4}} 3(\sin 8x + \sin 6x)\,dx = 3\left[-\frac{\cos 8x}{8} - \frac{\cos 6x}{6}\right]_0^{\frac{\pi}{4}}$
  3. At $\frac{\pi}{4}$: $-\frac{\cos 2\pi}{8} - \frac{\cos\frac{3\pi}{2}}{6} = -\frac{1}{8} - 0 = -\frac{1}{8}$
  4. At $0$: $-\frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{6} = -\frac{7}{24}$
  5. $3\left(-\frac{1}{8} + \frac{7}{24}\right) = 3 \times \frac{4}{24} = \frac{1}{2}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{1}{2}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for using $\sin 8x + \sin 6x$, method and accuracy marks for integrating to $-\frac{\cos 8x}{8} - \frac{\cos 6x}{6}$ (times 3), a method mark for substituting both limits, and accuracy for $\frac{1}{2}$.

Calculation check

  • 3 × ((-1 ÷ 8 - 0) - (-1 ÷ 8 - 1 ÷ 6)) = 0.5
