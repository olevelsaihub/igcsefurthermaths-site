Finding a point from a line and a distance

Locating an unknown point (m, n) that lies on a known line or has a known gradient to another point, and is a given distance from a point, by forming and solving a quadratic.

Type 9 8 Coordinate geometry FPM-COO-08

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Very Difficult.

An unknown point $(m, n)$ or $(p, q)$ that 'lies on the line' or has a given gradient to another point, together with a length such as $8\sqrt{5}$ or an area, plus a sign condition like '$m > 0$'.

The calculation

$(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = d^2$ for a point at distance $d$ from $(x_0, y_0)$

On a line with gradient $m$, write a point as $(x_0 + t,\ y_0 + mt)$

Try it$P$ lies on $y = 2x$ and $OP = 5$, with $x > 0$. Find $P$.

  1. $t^2 + (2t)^2 = 25$, so $5t^2 = 25$ and $t = \pm\sqrt{5}$
  2. $x > 0$ gives $P = (\sqrt{5}, 2\sqrt{5})$

The method

  1. Write the point

    Use the line to write $P$ as $(t, 2t + 1)$.

  2. Form the equation

    Set the squared distance equal to $d^2$.

  3. Solve the quadratic

    Factorise or use the formula.

  4. Choose with the condition

    Use the stated sign to pick the point.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using the length, not its square

    Set the distance squared equal to $d^2$.

  • ✗
    Both points given

    Reject the root that breaks the stated condition.

What a complete response includes

Method marks for linking the coordinates with the line or gradient condition, for setting up the distance equation, and for solving it, followed by accuracy marks for each correct coordinate after the sign condition is applied. Area-based versions earn an extra method mark for using the area to find the needed length.

Finding a point from a line and a distance · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 6 · part (d) · 7 marks

The line $l$ passes through $A(-2, 2)$ and $B(3, 12)$. The point $C(1, 8)$ lies on $l$ (part (a)). The line $k$ is perpendicular to $l$, passes through $C$, and crosses the $x$-axis at $D$, where $CD = 8\sqrt{5}$ (part (c)). The point $X$ with coordinates $(m, n)$ lies on $l$ such that the area of triangle $DXC$ is $80$ units$^2$. Given that $m > 0$, (d) find the value of $m$ and the value of $n$.

Worked explanation

  1. Gradient of $l = \frac{12 - 2}{3 - (-2)} = 2$, so $l$: $y = 2x + 6$. Line $k$ has gradient $-\frac{1}{2}$ through $C(1, 8)$: $x + 2y = 17$, meeting the $x$-axis at $D(17, 0)$.
  2. $CD = \sqrt{(17 - 1)^2 + (0 - 8)^2} = \sqrt{320} = 8\sqrt{5}$
  3. $X$ is on $l$ and $CD$ is perpendicular to $l$, so the angle at $C$ is $90^\circ$ and area $= \frac{1}{2} \times CD \times CX$.
  4. $\frac{1}{2} \times 8\sqrt{5} \times CX = 80 \Rightarrow CX = \frac{20}{\sqrt{5}} = 4\sqrt{5}$, so $CX^2 = 80$.
  5. $X$ on $l$: $n = 2m + 6$. Then $(m - 1)^2 + (2m + 6 - 8)^2 = 80$
  6. $(m - 1)^2 + 4(m - 1)^2 = 80 \Rightarrow 5(m - 1)^2 = 80 \Rightarrow (m - 1)^2 = 16$
  7. $m - 1 = \pm 4 \Rightarrow m = 5$ or $m = -3$
  8. $m > 0$, so $m = 5$ and $n = 2(5) + 6 = 16$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$m = 5$, $n = 16$

How the marks are earned

Method marks for using the right angle at $C$ to write the area as half $CD$ times $CX$ and finding $CX$, for using $n = 2m + 6$ in a distance equation for $CX$, and for solving the quadratic; accuracy marks for $CX = 4\sqrt{5}$ and for $m = 5$, $n = 16$ after rejecting $m = -3$.

Calculation check

  • ((17-1) × × 2+(0-8) × × 2) = 320
  • round(0.5 × 320 × × 0.5 × 80 × × 0.5,6) = 80
  • (5-1) × × 2+(16-8) × × 2 = 80
  • 2 × 5+6 = 16
  • 17+2 × 0 = 17
