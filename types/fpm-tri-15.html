Angle between a line and a plane

Calculation

The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane. Drop a perpendicular to the plane to form a right-angled triangle.

The calculation

$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{height}}{\text{projection}}$, $\quad \sin\theta = \frac{\text{height}}{\text{line}}$

5

The method

  1. Drop the perpendicular

    From the top end of the line, drop a perpendicular to the plane.

  2. Join the foot

    Join the foot to the other end of the line: this is the projection.

  3. Find the lengths

    Use Pythagoras on the base to find the projection.

  4. Use trig

    Use $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{height}}{\text{projection}}$ and find $\theta$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong angle

    The angle is with the projection, not with an edge of the base.

  • ✗
    Height and projection swapped

    The height is opposite the angle, so it goes on top in tan.

Angle between a line and a plane · November 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 4 · part (whole) · 6 marks

$ABCDEF$ is a right prism with rectangular base $DCBA$. Triangle $CDE$ is a cross-section, with $\angle DCE = \angle ABF = 90^\circ$ and $\angle EDC = \angle FAB = 40^\circ$. $DE = AF = 15$ cm and $EF = DA = CB = 20$ cm. Find, in degrees to one decimal place, the angle between the line $FD$ and the plane $DCBA$.

Worked explanation

  1. $FB \perp BA$ and $FB \perp BC$ (the prism edge), so $FB$ is perpendicular to the plane $DCBA$ and $B$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $F$.
  2. The projection of $FD$ is $BD$, so the angle required is $\angle FDB$.
  3. In triangle $ABF$: $FB = 15\sin 40^\circ = 9.642$ cm and $AB = 15\cos 40^\circ = 11.491$ cm (so $DC = 11.491$ cm).
  4. $BD = \sqrt{DC^2 + CB^2} = \sqrt{11.491^2 + 20^2} = 23.066$ cm
  5. $\tan\angle FDB = \frac{FB}{BD} = \frac{9.642}{23.066} = 0.4180$
  6. $\angle FDB = 22.7^\circ$ (1 d.p.)
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$22.7^\circ$

How the marks are earned

Method marks for FB and AB from the right-angled cross-section, for BD by Pythagoras, for identifying angle FDB, and for the tan ratio; accuracy marks for the lengths and the final 22.7 degrees.
