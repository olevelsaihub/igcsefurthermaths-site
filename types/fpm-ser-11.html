The method

  1. Pick the formula

    Use $\frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ when $r > 1$.

  2. Simplify powers

    $(\sqrt3)^6 = 27$ by counting pairs.

  3. Rationalise

    Multiply by the conjugate.

  4. Write the form asked

    Give $p + q\sqrt3$ exactly.

Do not lose marks here

Adding up a finite geometric series · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Question 7 · part (c) · 4 marks

The $n$th term of a geometric series $G$ is $u_n$. The first term is $a$ and the common ratio is $r$, where $r > 0$. Given that $u_3 = 4$ and $u_7 = 16$, it has been shown that $r = \sqrt{2}$ and $a = 2$. The sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n$. (c) Find $S_{20}$, giving your answer in the form $p(1 + \sqrt{2})$ where $p$ is an integer.

Worked explanation

  1. From $ar^2 = 4$, $ar^6 = 16$: $r^4 = 4$, $r = \sqrt{2}$ (as $r > 0$), $a = \frac{4}{2} = 2$
  2. $S_{20} = \frac{2\left((\sqrt{2})^{20} - 1\right)}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$
  3. $(\sqrt{2})^{20} = 2^{10} = 1024$, so $S_{20} = \frac{2 \times 1023}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = \frac{2046}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$
  4. Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2} + 1$: $\frac{2046(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{(\sqrt{2})^2 - 1} = \frac{2046(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{1}$
  5. $S_{20} = 2046(1 + \sqrt{2})$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$S_{20} = 2046(1 + \sqrt{2})$, so $p = 2046$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the sum formula with a = 2, r = sqrt(2) and n = 20, a method mark for simplifying the power to 1024, a method mark for rationalising the denominator, and an accuracy mark for 2046(1 + sqrt(2)).

Calculation check

  • 2 × × 10 = 1024
  • round(2 × ((2 × × 0.5) × × 20-1) ÷ (2 × × 0.5-1),4) = 4939.4809
  • 2 × (1024-1) = 2046
