Knowledge card

Adding up a finite geometric series

Written
  • A geometric series is multiplied by the same number each time: the common ratio $r$.
  • The $n$th term is $u_n = ar^{n-1}$, where $a$ is the first term.
  • The sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ (or $\frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$), for $r \neq 1$.
  • For $\sum_{r=p}^{q}$ there are $q - p + 1$ terms; the first is found by putting in the lower limit.
  • When $r > 1$ the form $\frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ avoids negative signs.
  • To rationalise $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$ multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2} + 1$.

Before you begin

  • Index laws, including powers of surds.
  • Rationalising denominators.
  • Counting terms from sigma limits.
1

The method

  1. Identify $a$, $r$ and the number of terms $n$.

    Why: These three numbers are all the sum formula needs.

  2. Substitute into the sum formula.

    Why: Direct use of the formula avoids adding many terms.

  3. Simplify powers (e.g. $(\sqrt{2})^{20} = 2^{10}$).

    Why: Keeps the answer exact.

  4. Rationalise any surd denominator and write in the form asked.

    Why: Exact-form answers must match the requested shape.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Miscounting the number of terms from the sigma limits.
  • ✗
    Taking the first term as $a$ when the sum starts somewhere else.
  • ✗
    Sign errors when rationalising the denominator.
  • ✗
    Giving a decimal when an exact form is asked for.

Adding up a finite geometric series · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Question 7 · part (c) · 4 marks

The $n$th term of a geometric series $G$ is $u_n$. The first term is $a$ and the common ratio is $r$, where $r > 0$. Given that $u_3 = 4$ and $u_7 = 16$, it has been shown that $r = \sqrt{2}$ and $a = 2$. The sum of the first $n$ terms is $S_n$. (c) Find $S_{20}$, giving your answer in the form $p(1 + \sqrt{2})$ where $p$ is an integer.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. From $ar^2 = 4$, $ar^6 = 16$: $r^4 = 4$, $r = \sqrt{2}$ (as $r > 0$), $a = \frac{4}{2} = 2$
  2. $S_{20} = \frac{2\left((\sqrt{2})^{20} - 1\right)}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$
  3. $(\sqrt{2})^{20} = 2^{10} = 1024$, so $S_{20} = \frac{2 \times 1023}{\sqrt{2} - 1} = \frac{2046}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$
  4. Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2} + 1$: $\frac{2046(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{(\sqrt{2})^2 - 1} = \frac{2046(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{1}$
  5. $S_{20} = 2046(1 + \sqrt{2})$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$S_{20} = 2046(1 + \sqrt{2})$, so $p = 2046$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the sum formula with a = 2, r = sqrt(2) and n = 20, a method mark for simplifying the power to 1024, a method mark for rationalising the denominator, and an accuracy mark for 2046(1 + sqrt(2)).

Calculation check

  • 2 × × 10 = 1024
  • round(2 × ((2 × × 0.5) × × 20-1) ÷ (2 × × 0.5-1),4) = 4939.4809
  • 2 × (1024-1) = 2046
