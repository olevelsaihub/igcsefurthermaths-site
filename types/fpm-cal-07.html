Finding unknown constants in a curve from its gradient

Using a known gradient (zero at a stationary point, or a tangent or normal gradient) and a known point to find letters such as p and q in the curve's equation.

Type 6 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-07

★☆☆☆☆ · 5 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The curve's equation contains letters such as $p$, $q$, $a$, $b$ and the question gives a stationary point, a gradient or a normal, and asks you to 'show that $p = \ldots$' or 'find the value of $q$'.

Knowledge card

Finding unknown constants in a curve from its gradient

Calculation

Each fact gives one equation: a stationary point gives $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$, and a point on the curve gives $y = f(x)$. Solve the equations together for the unknown constants.

Check each step of the constants

3 steps to find

Each fact gives one equation; two letters need two equations.

  1. Stationary: gradient 0
  2. Point gives a second equation
  3. Solve together

Stationary: gradient 0

Why
At a stationary point $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$.

Point gives a second equation

Working
Put $x = 1$ and $y = -1$ into $y = px^2 + qx$.

Solve together

Working
$q = -2p$, so $p - 2p = -1$, giving $p = 1$.
0 of 3 found

2 steps to find

Use the gradient equation first, then the point.

  1. Derivative with a letter
  2. Point gives b

Derivative with a letter

Working
$\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + a$, so at $x = 1$: $3 + a = 5$.

Point gives b

Trap
Using the gradient 5 as the y-value is a common slip.
0 of 2 found
2

The calculation

$\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x=a} = m$ and $f(a) = b$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Substituting the point into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ instead of the curve
  • Using $y = 0$ instead of $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ at a stationary point

Try itThe curve $y = px^2 + qx$ has gradient $3$ at $x = 1$ and passes through $(-1, 0)$. Find $p$ and $q$.

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2px + q$, so $2p + q = 3$
  2. $(-1, 0)$ gives $p - q = 0$, so $p = q = 1$

5

The method

  1. Differentiate

    Keep the letters: find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ in terms of $a$ and $b$.

  2. Use the stationary point

    Put $x = 1$ into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and set it to $0$.

  3. Use the point

    Put $x = 1$ and $y = -2$ into the curve.

  4. Solve and state

    Solve the two equations together and state both values.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    y = 0 for a stationary point

    A stationary point needs $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$, not $y = 0$.

  • ✗
    Point in the derivative

    Put the point's $y$ into the curve, never into $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Finding unknown constants in a curve from its gradient · November 2021 · Paper 2 · Question 4 · part (a) · 4 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y = 2x^2 + px + q$ where $p$ and $q$ are integers. The curve $C$ has a stationary point at $(3, -5)$. Show that $p = -12$ and find the value of $q$.

Your answer

Figures check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 4x + p$
  2. Stationary at $x = 3$: $4(3) + p = 0 \Rightarrow p = -12$, as required.
  3. The point $(3, -5)$ lies on $C$: $-5 = 2(3)^2 - 12(3) + q$
  4. $-5 = 18 - 36 + q = -18 + q \Rightarrow q = 13$.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$p = -12$, $q = 13$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for differentiating, a method mark for setting $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ at $x = 3$, accuracy for $p = -12$ shown, a method mark for substituting $(3, -5)$ into the curve, and accuracy for $q = 13$.

Calculation check

  • 4 × 3 - 12 = 0
  • 2 × 3 × × 2 - 12 × 3 + 13 = -5
