Quadratic trig equations using an identity

Using sin^2 + cos^2 = 1, a double-angle formula or a proven identity to turn an equation into a quadratic (or polynomial) in one trig ratio, then solving.

Type 21 10 Trigonometry FPM-TRI-27

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

An equation with a mixture such as $\sin^2$ and $\cos$, $\cos 2x$ and $\sin x$, or $\sin^2$ and $\sin\cos$, that can be turned into a quadratic in one ratio; often 'hence' after showing the quadratic.

5

The method

  1. Use the identity

    Replace $\cos^2 x$ by $1 - \sin^2 x$ so only $\sin x$ is left.

  2. Rearrange to a quadratic

    Collect terms so the quadratic equals zero.

  3. Factorise and solve

    Solve each factor for $\sin x$, rejecting any value outside $-1$ to $1$.

  4. Find all angles

    For each value, find every angle in $0 \le x < 360$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong form of cos 2x

    Use the form of $\cos 2x$ that leaves one ratio, and check the signs.

  • ✗
    Impossible value

    A value such as $\sin x = 2$ or $\sin x = -3$ has no angle: reject it.

Quadratic trig equations using an identity · June 2021 · Paper 2 · Question 10 · part (c) · 4 marks

Given that $7\sin^2\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta = 6$, (b) show that $\tan^2\theta + \tan\theta - 6 = 0$. (c) Hence solve the equation $7\sin^2 y^\circ + \sin y^\circ\cos y^\circ = 6$ for $0 \le y < 360$, giving your solutions to the nearest whole number.

Worked explanation

  1. Divide by $\cos^2 y$: $7\tan^2 y + \tan y = \frac{6}{\cos^2 y} = 6(1 + \tan^2 y)$, so $\tan^2 y + \tan y - 6 = 0$.
  2. Factorise: $(\tan y + 3)(\tan y - 2) = 0$, so $\tan y = 2$ or $\tan y = -3$.
  3. $\tan y = 2$: $y = 63.43^\circ$ or $63.43^\circ + 180^\circ = 243.43^\circ$
  4. $\tan y = -3$: $\tan^{-1}(-3) = -71.57^\circ$, so $y = 108.43^\circ$ or $288.43^\circ$
  5. To the nearest whole number: $y = 63, 108, 243, 288$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$y = 63, 108, 243, 288$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for factorising the quadratic in tan, a method mark for solving tan y = 2 and tan y = -3, a method mark for adding 180 degrees, and accuracy marks for all four answers.

  • (-1-(1+24) × × 0.5) ÷ 2 = -3.0

  • round(180-71.56505117707799) = 108
