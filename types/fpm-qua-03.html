Completing the square

Rewriting ax^2 + bx + c in the form a(x + p)^2 + q, or as q - a(x + p)^2 when the x squared term is negative, and stating the constants.

Type 2 2 The quadratic function FPM-QUA-03

★★★★☆ · 11 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question gives a quadratic $f(x)$ and says it 'can be written in the form' $a(x+b)^2+c$, $A-B(x+C)^2$ or $(x+a)^2+b$, then asks for the value of each constant. It is almost always part (a) of a longer quadratic question.

Completing the square

Calculation

To complete the square, halve the $x$ coefficient inside a bracket, then take back the square you added so the value stays the same. Take the $x^2$ coefficient out first.

2

The calculation

$ax^2+bx+c = a(x+p)^2+q$, with $p=\frac{b}{2a}$ and $q=c-ap^2$

Negative $a$: write $q-a(x+p)^2$ instead.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Subtracting $p^2$ without multiplying by $a$
  • Halving $b$ instead of $\frac{b}{2a}$ when $a\ne 1$

Try itWrite $3x^2-12x+5$ as $a(x+p)^2+q$.

  1. $3\left[x^2-4x\right]+5=3\left[(x-2)^2-4\right]+5$
  2. $=3(x-2)^2-7$, so $a=3$, $p=-2$, $q=-7$

Does it score?

Exam question

Given $f(x)=2x^2+8x+11$, write $f(x)$ in the form $a(x+p)^2+q$ and state $a$, $p$ and $q$. (3 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

5

The method

  1. Take out a

    Factor the $x^2$ coefficient out of the $x^2$ and $x$ terms only.

  2. Halve the x term

    Inside the bracket, the number is $\frac{b}{2a}$.

  3. Subtract the square

    Subtract $p^2$ inside the bracket, then multiply that by $a$.

  4. Collect the constant

    Add the outside constant to $-ap^2$ to get $q$.

  5. Check

    Expand, or test one value of $x$ in both forms.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Forgotten factor a

    The subtracted square is multiplied by $a$ too.

  • ✗
    Wrong sign of p

    $(x+p)^2$ has $+2px$, so $p$ takes the sign of $b$.

What a complete response includes

Usually one mark per constant. With a leading coefficient other than 1, the first mark is often a method mark for a correct bracket such as $8(x+\frac{5}{8})^2$, followed by accuracy marks for the remaining constants. The leading coefficient itself is often an easy B mark.

Completing the square · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (a) · 3 marks

$f(x)=8x^2+10x-3$. Given that $f(x)$ can be written in the form $A(x+B)^2+C$ where $A$, $B$ and $C$ are constants, find the value of $A$, the value of $B$ and the value of $C$. (3 marks)

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $f(x)=8\left[x^2+\frac{5}{4}x\right]-3$
  2. Half of $\frac{5}{4}$ is $\frac{5}{8}$, so $x^2+\frac{5}{4}x=\left(x+\frac{5}{8}\right)^2-\frac{25}{64}$
  3. $f(x)=8\left[\left(x+\frac{5}{8}\right)^2-\frac{25}{64}\right]-3=8\left(x+\frac{5}{8}\right)^2-\frac{25}{8}-3$
  4. $-\frac{25}{8}-\frac{24}{8}=-\frac{49}{8}$, so $f(x)=8\left(x+\frac{5}{8}\right)^2-\frac{49}{8}$
  5. Check at $x=2$: $8(2)^2+10(2)-3=49$ and $8\left(\frac{21}{8}\right)^2-\frac{49}{8}=\frac{441}{8}-\frac{49}{8}=49$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$A=8$, $B=\frac{5}{8}$, $C=-\frac{49}{8}$

How the marks are earned

One mark for $A=8$, one for $B=\frac{5}{8}$ (from a correct bracket) and one for $C=-\frac{49}{8}$ (or $-6.125$); the last mark needs the $\frac{25}{64}$ multiplied by 8.

Calculation check

  • 8 × (5 ÷ 8) × × 2+3 = 6.125
  • 8 × 2 × × 2+10 × 2-3 = 49
  • 8 × (2+5 ÷ 8) × × 2-49 ÷ 8 = 49
