Knowledge card

Shading or describing a region

Written
  • Each line splits the grid into two sides; an inequality is true on one side only.
  • Use a test point not on the line, often the origin $(0,0)$: if it makes the inequality true, the wanted side is the side containing that point.
  • For $y\leqslant mx+c$ the wanted region is on or below the line; for $y\geqslant mx+c$ it is on or above.
  • $\leqslant$ and $\geqslant$ include the boundary line; $<$ and $>$ do not.
  • Region R is where every inequality is true at the same time; label it R clearly.
  • To describe a region, find each boundary line's equation, then choose the sign by testing a point inside the region.

Before you begin

  • Drawing straight lines from their equations (FPM-IDN-05)
  • Meaning of inequality signs
  • Substituting coordinates into an expression
1

The method

  1. Take each inequality in turn and pick a test point not on its line (the origin if possible).

    Why: Substituting a point is more reliable than guessing 'above' or 'below', especially for lines like ax + by = c.

  2. Decide whether the test point satisfies the inequality, and mark the wanted side of that line.

    Why: This tells you which half of the grid is allowed by that inequality.

  3. Find the region that is on the wanted side of every line, usually a triangle or quadrilateral whose corners are line crossings.

    Why: R must satisfy all the inequalities together.

  4. Shade R (or shade out the unwanted parts if the question says so) and label it R.

    Why: The examiner must be able to see exactly which region you mean.

  5. When describing a given region instead, write each boundary line's equation and choose the sign using a point inside the region.

    Why: A point inside R must make every inequality true.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Shading the wrong side of one of the lines, so R is the wrong shape.
  • ✗
    Not labelling R, or leaving it unclear which region is R.
  • ✗
    Assuming 'less than' always means 'below' even for a line written as 4y >= x - 8 with a negative coefficient after rearranging.
  • ✗
    Using a strict sign where the boundary is included (or the reverse) when writing down inequalities.
  • ✗
    Choosing a test point that lies on the line, which gives no information.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Show10Shade the single region that satisfies every inequality and label it R; the boundaries must be the lines you drew.
Write down1List the inequalities, one for each boundary, with the correct direction and with $\leqslant$ or $\geqslant$ if the boundary is included; no working is needed.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Usually one or two B marks that need the whole region correct: every boundary in the right place and on the right side, with R identified. When writing down inequalities, there is typically one mark per correct inequality.

Shading or describing a region · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (b) · 1 mark

Show, by shading, the region $R$ defined by the inequalities $y\leqslant 2x+5$, $4y\geqslant x-8$, $5y+3x\leqslant 30$ (the three lines from part (a)).

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Test the origin $(0,0)$ in each inequality.
  2. $y\leqslant 2x+5$: $0\leqslant 5$, true, so $R$ is on the origin side of line (i), which is below/right of it.
  3. $4y\geqslant x-8$: $0\geqslant -8$, true, so $R$ is on the origin side of line (ii), which is above it.
  4. $5y+3x\leqslant 30$: $0\leqslant 30$, true, so $R$ is on the origin side of line (iii), which is below it.
  5. The region on the origin side of all three lines is the triangle with corners where the lines meet: $(-4,-3)$, about $(0.38,5.77)$ and about $(9.41,0.35)$.
  6. Shade this triangle and label it R.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The triangle containing the origin, bounded by the three lines, with vertices $(-4,-3)$, $\left(\frac{5}{13},\frac{75}{13}\right)$ and $\left(\frac{160}{17},\frac{6}{17}\right)$, shaded and labelled $R$.

How the marks are earned

A single mark for shading the correct triangle; every boundary must be the correct line and the correct side.

Calculation check

  • 2 × 0+5 = 5
  • 0-8 = -8
  • 5 × 0+3 × 0 = 0
  • 4 × (-3)-(-4-8) = 0
  • round(5 × (75 ÷ 13)+3 × (5 ÷ 13), 9) = 30
