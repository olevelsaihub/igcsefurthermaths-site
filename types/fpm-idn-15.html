Where a line meets a fraction curve again

Finding the other point where a tangent or normal line meets a curve whose equation is a fraction, using the point already known.

Type 8 3 Identities and inequalities FPM-IDN-15

★☆☆☆☆ · 4 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Final part of a rational curve question: 'The line l meets C again at the point D. Find the x coordinate of D' (or the coordinates of D), where l is a tangent or normal found earlier.

Where a line meets a fraction curve again

Calculation

Set the line equal to the fraction curve and multiply every term by the denominator. The known point is one root, so the other root gives the new point.

The method

  1. Equate

    Write the line equal to the curve.

  2. Clear the fraction

    Multiply every term by the denominator.

  3. Use the known root

    Factorise, using the known x to reject it.

  4. Give the new point

    Find its y and state the point in the form asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Partly cleared

    Multiply every term, including the left side.

  • ✗
    Known point again

    Reject the point already given.

Where a line meets a fraction curve again · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (c) · 4 marks

A curve $C$ has equation $y=\frac{qx-2}{x-p}$, $x\neq p$. The line $l$ with equation $y=x+2$ is the normal to $C$ at the point $A$ where $C$ crosses the $y$-axis. Part (a) gives $p=1$ and $q=3$, so $C$ is $y=\frac{3x-2}{x-1}$. The line $l$ meets $C$ again at the point $D$. Find the $x$ coordinate of $D$.

Worked explanation

  1. Recap of (a): at $x=0$, $y=\frac{-2}{-p}=\frac{2}{p}$; $A$ is on $y=x+2$ so $y=2$, giving $p=1$.
  2. $\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{q(x-1)-(qx-2)}{(x-1)^2}=\frac{2-q}{(x-1)^2}$; at $x=0$ this is $2-q$. The normal has gradient $1$, so the tangent gradient is $-1$: $2-q=-1 \Rightarrow q=3$.
  3. Set the line equal to the curve: $x+2=\frac{3x-2}{x-1}$
  4. Multiply by $(x-1)$: $(x+2)(x-1)=3x-2$
  5. $x^2+x-2=3x-2$
  6. $x^2-2x=0 \Rightarrow x(x-2)=0$
  7. $x=0$ is the point $A$, so $D$ has $x=2$ (and $y=4$).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x=2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for equating $x+2$ with $\frac{3x-2}{x-1}$, a method mark for multiplying through by $(x-1)$ to form a polynomial equal to zero, a method mark for solving it, and an accuracy mark for $x=2$ (rejecting $x=0$).

Calculation check

  • (3 × 2-2) ÷ (2-1) = 4
  • 2+2 = 4
  • (3 × 0-2) ÷ (0-1) = 2
  • (2-3) ÷ (0-1) × × 2 = -1
  • 2 × × 2-2 × 2 = 0
