Exponential equations, including hidden quadratics

Equations with the unknown in a power, solved by matching bases or taking logs, including those that become a quadratic or cubic in a substitution such as u = e^(3x) or u = 2^y.

Type 4 1 Logarithms, indices and surds FPM-LOG-09

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The unknown is in a power: $e^{2x} = 4$, $2^{x} = 7$, or curves such as $y = 4 - e^{2x}$ meeting the $x$-axis or each other. If you see both $e^{6x}$ and $e^{3x}$, or $2^{2y}$ and $2^y$, a substitution is needed.

Knowledge card

Exponential equations, including hidden quadratics

Written
  • $\ln$ means $\log_e$, so $\ln(e^k) = k$ and $e^{\ln k} = k$.
  • To solve $a^x = b$, take logs of both sides: $x = \frac{\log b}{\log a}$ (or $x = \frac{\ln b}{\ln a}$).
  • If both sides are powers of the same base, equate the powers: $3^{6y - 5} = 3^{-5}$ gives $6y - 5 = -5$.
  • $e^{6x} = (e^{3x})^2$ and $2^{2y} = (2^y)^2$, so a substitution $u = e^{3x}$ or $u = 2^y$ can turn the equation into a quadratic or cubic.
  • An exponential $a^x$ (with $a > 0$) is always positive, so any negative or zero value of $u$ must be rejected.
  • Log laws simplify answers: $\frac{1}{2}\ln 4 = \ln 4^{\frac{1}{2}} = \ln 2$.

Before you begin

  • Index laws, especially $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ and $a^{-m} = \frac{1}{a^m}$.
  • The definition of ln as the inverse of $e^x$, and the laws of logs.
  • Solving quadratics by factorising, and simple cubics using the factor theorem.
1

The method

  1. Isolate the exponential term (or, for two curves, set the equations equal and multiply through to clear any negative power).

    Why: You can only take logs cleanly of a single exponential.

  2. If several powers appear, write them in terms of one, e.g. $u = e^{3x}$ so $e^{6x} = u^2$, and form a polynomial in $u$.

    Why: This turns an unfamiliar equation into a quadratic or cubic you can solve.

  3. Solve for $u$ (or the exponential) and reject any value that is zero or negative.

    Why: An exponential can never be zero or negative.

  4. Take logs (ln for base $e$) or match bases to find $x$, then simplify with log laws into the form asked.

    Why: Logs undo the power.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Keeping a negative value of $u$ and writing $\ln$ of a negative number.
  • ✗
    Treating $e^{6x}$ as $2e^{3x}$ or $e^{3x} + e^{3x}$ instead of $(e^{3x})^2$.
  • ✗
    Taking logs before isolating, e.g. writing $\ln(4 - e^{2x}) = \ln 4 - 2x$.
  • ✗
    Dividing by 2 in the wrong place: from $e^{2x} = 4$ writing $x = \ln 2$ without the log-law step, or $x = \ln 4 \div 2$ left unsimplified in a 'show that'.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A method mark for isolating the exponential or making the correct substitution, a method mark for solving the resulting equation, a method mark for taking logs correctly, and accuracy marks for each correct value. In 'show that' parts the final accuracy mark needs the given answer reached exactly, with the simplifying step shown.

Exponential equations, including hidden quadratics · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 11 · part (a)(ii) · 3 marks

The curve $C$ has equation $y = 4 - e^{2x}$. $C$ crosses the $x$-axis at the point $B$. Show that the $x$ coordinate of $B$ is $x = \ln 2$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. At $B$, $y = 0$: $\;4 - e^{2x} = 0$
  2. $e^{2x} = 4$
  3. $2x = \ln 4$
  4. $x = \frac{1}{2}\ln 4 = \ln 4^{\frac{1}{2}} = \ln 2$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x = \ln 2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting $y = 0$ and isolating $e^{2x} = 4$, a method mark for taking natural logs to get $2x = \ln 4$, and an accuracy mark for reaching $x = \ln 2$ with the power-law step shown.

  • round(4-2.718281828459045 × × (2 × 0.6931471805599453),10) = 0
