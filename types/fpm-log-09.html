Exponential equations, including hidden quadratics

Equations with the unknown in a power, solved by matching bases or taking logs, including those that become a quadratic or cubic in a substitution such as u = e^(3x) or u = 2^y.

Type 4 1 Logarithms, indices and surds FPM-LOG-09

★★☆☆☆ · 6 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The unknown is in a power: $e^{2x} = 4$, $2^{x} = 7$, or curves such as $y = 4 - e^{2x}$ meeting the $x$-axis or each other. If you see both $e^{6x}$ and $e^{3x}$, or $2^{2y}$ and $2^y$, a substitution is needed.

Knowledge card

Exponential equations, including hidden quadratics

Calculation

To solve an exponential equation, take logs or match bases. If the power is $e^{2x}$, the substitution $u = e^x$ gives a quadratic, and only positive values of $u$ are kept.

3 steps to find

Which line gives the quadratic?

  1. Square of the power
  2. Both roots stay
  3. Take ln to undo e

Square of the power

Trap
$e^{2x}$ is $(e^x)^2$, not $2e^x$.

Both roots stay

Why
$e^x$ is always positive, so $u = 2$ and $u = 3$ are both valid.

Take ln to undo e

Why
$\ln(e^x) = x$, so $e^x = 2$ gives $x = \ln 2$.

What is the same base on each side?

Write 16 as a power of 2

Why
$2^4 = 16$, so the bases match and the powers can be equated.

Equate the powers only

Trap
Once the bases match, equate only the powers, not the numbers.

5

The method

  1. Spot the pattern

    Look for $e^{2x} = (e^x)^2$ or $2^{2y} = (2^y)^2$.

  2. Substitute

    Let u stand for the repeated power.

  3. Solve for u

    Factorise, or use the formula.

  4. Reject invalid u

    A power of $e$ is always positive: drop zero or negative values.

  5. Back-substitute

    Take $\ln$ to find $x$, then simplify with the log laws.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing e^(2x) as 2e^x

    Its square is $(e^x)^2$, not $2e^x$.

  • ✗
    Taking logs too early

    Isolate the power first; logs of sums do not split.

What a complete response includes

The curve $C$ has equation $y = 4 - e^{2x}$. $C$ crosses the $x$-axis at the point $B$. Show that the $x$ coordinate of $B$ is $x = \ln 2$.

Worked explanation

  1. At $B$, $y = 0$: $\;4 - e^{2x} = 0$
  2. $e^{2x} = 4$
  3. $2x = \ln 4$
  4. $x = \frac{1}{2}\ln 4 = \ln 4^{\frac{1}{2}} = \ln 2$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x = \ln 2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting $y = 0$ and isolating $e^{2x} = 4$, a method mark for taking natural logs to get $2x = \ln 4$, and an accuracy mark for reaching $x = \ln 2$ with the power-law step shown.
