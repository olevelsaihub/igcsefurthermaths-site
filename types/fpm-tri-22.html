Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums

Adding or subtracting two addition formulae to turn a product of trig functions into a sum, or a sum into a product.

Knowledge card

Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums

Written
  • $\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$.
  • $\cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B) = 2\sin A\sin B$.
  • $\sin(A + B) + \sin(A - B) = 2\sin A\cos B$.
  • Sum to product: put $P = A + B$ and $Q = A - B$, so $A = \frac{P + Q}{2}$ and $B = \frac{P - Q}{2}$.
  • $\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\frac{P + Q}{2}\cos\frac{P - Q}{2}$.
  • $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$ and $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$.

Before you begin

  • Addition formulae.
  • Solving simultaneous linear equations.
  • Even and odd properties of cos and sin.
1

The method

  1. Write the expansions of the A + B and A - B formulae.

    Why: These come from the formula sheet.

  2. Add or subtract so the unwanted terms cancel.

    Why: This produces the product or sum identity.

  3. To apply it, choose $A$ and $B$ (or set $P = A + B$, $Q = A - B$ and solve for $A$ and $B$).

    Why: This matches the given angles to the identity.

  4. Substitute and simplify.

    Why: This gives the required form.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign error in $\cos(A - B)$ (it is $\cos A\cos B + \sin A\sin B$).
  • ✗
    Choosing A and B the wrong way round, giving a negative angle without simplifying.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to halve when finding $A$ and $B$ from $P$ and $Q$.
  • ✗
    Not stating the substitution in a 'hence' part.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Show that5Write both expansions in full, show the cancelling, and finish with the exact identity.
Hence3Use the identity just proved: state the substitution (e.g. $A = \frac{P + Q}{2}$, $B = \frac{P - Q}{2}$, or $A = 7x$, $B = x$) and give the result.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

A mark for both correct expansions, a mark for the cancelling step to the identity, and for 'hence' parts a mark for the correct substitution and a mark for the final form.

Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 10 · part (b) · 3 marks

(a) Show that $\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$. (b) Hence show that $\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. From (a): $\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$
  2. Let $P = A + B$ and $Q = A - B$.
  3. Adding: $P + Q = 2A$, so $A = \frac{P + Q}{2}$. Subtracting: $P - Q = 2B$, so $B = \frac{P - Q}{2}$.
  4. Substitute: $\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting P = A + B and Q = A - B, a method mark for solving to get A and B in terms of P and Q, and an accuracy mark for substituting to reach the result.
