Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums

Adding or subtracting two addition formulae to turn a product of trig functions into a sum, or a sum into a product.

Knowledge card

Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums

Written

Add or subtract two addition formulae so that the unwanted terms cancel, leaving a product. The result can then be used in a hence part.

Which terms cancel, and why?

  1. Expand the first bracket
  2. Watch the sign
  3. sin A cos B cancels

Expand the first bracket

Formula
$\sin(A \pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$

Watch the sign

Trap
$\sin(A - B)$ has a minus in the middle.

sin A cos B cancels

Then
The $\cos A\sin B$ terms double, giving $2\cos A\sin B$.

3 lines to find

Which values of A and B are used?

  1. Choose A and B
  2. Read off the product
  3. Order as asked

Choose A and B

Why
$A + B = 3x$ and $A - B = x$ give $A = 2x$ and $B = x$.

Read off the product

Working
Put $A = 2x$ and $B = x$ into $2\cos A\sin B$.

The calculation

$\sin(A + B) + \sin(A - B) = 2\sin A\cos B$

$\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$ and $\cos(A - B) - \cos(A + B) = 2\sin A\sin B$

  • Sign error in $\cos(A - B)$
  • Forgetting to halve when finding $A = \frac{P+Q}{2}$ from $P$ and $Q$

Try itWrite $\cos 5x + \cos x$ as a product.

  1. $A = \frac{5x + x}{2} = 3x$ and $B = \frac{5x - x}{2} = 2x$
  2. $\cos 5x + \cos x = 2\cos 3x\cos 2x$

5

The method

  1. Write the expansions

    Write $\sin(A + B)$ and $\sin(A - B)$ from the formula sheet.

  2. Take the difference

    Subtract one expansion from the other, term by term.

  3. Cancel the matching terms

    The $\sin A\cos B$ terms cancel, leaving the product.

  4. Match the printed form

    Write the answer as $2\cos A\sin B$, the form asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign error

    Check the minus in the middle of $\sin(A - B)$.

  • ✗
    Checking, not proving

    Testing one pair of values does not prove an identity.

Turning sums of trig terms into products, and products into sums · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 10 · part (b) · 3 marks

(a) Show that $\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$. (b) Hence show that $\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$.

Worked explanation

  1. From (a): $\cos(A + B) + \cos(A - B) = 2\cos A\cos B$
  2. Let $P = A + B$ and $Q = A - B$.
  3. Adding: $P + Q = 2A$, so $A = \frac{P + Q}{2}$. Subtracting: $P - Q = 2B$, so $B = \frac{P - Q}{2}$.
  4. Substitute: $\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\cos P + \cos Q = 2\cos\left(\frac{P + Q}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{P - Q}{2}\right)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting P = A + B and Q = A - B, a method mark for solving to get A and B in terms of P and Q, and an accuracy mark for substituting to reach the result.
