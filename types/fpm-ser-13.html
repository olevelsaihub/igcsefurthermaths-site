Knowledge card

Sum to infinity of a geometric series

Calculation

The sum to infinity is $\frac{a}{1-r}$, used only when $|r| < 1$. Choose the root of $r$ that meets this and any sign condition.

1

Sum to infinity

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

2 steps to find

Which root is kept?

  1. Two roots
  2. Use the sign condition

Two roots

Why
Both $\pm\frac{1}{2}$ square to $\frac{1}{4}$.

Use the sign condition

Working
$ar = 8r > 0$, so $r > 0$.
0 of 2 found

2 steps to find

Which formula gives the sum?

  1. Denominator is 1 − r
  2. Divide by a fraction

Denominator is 1 − r

Trap
Using $a \div (1+r)$ is wrong.

Divide by a fraction

Working
$8 \div \frac{1}{2} = 8 \times 2 = 16$
0 of 2 found

The calculation

$S_\infty = \frac{a}{1-r}$ when $|r| < 1$

If two values of $r$ fit, choose the one with $|r| < 1$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Using $\frac{a}{1+r}$ as the formula
  • Using a value of $r$ with $|r| > 1$

Try itFind $S_\infty$ for $a = 20$ and $r = -\frac{2}{5}$.

  1. $S_\infty = \frac{20}{1 + \frac{2}{5}} = \frac{20}{\frac{7}{5}}$
  2. $= \frac{100}{7}$

Exam question

A geometric series has first term 8 and $r^2 = \frac{1}{4}$. Given that the second term is positive, find the sum to infinity of the series. (3 marks)

5

The method

  1. Find r

    Solve $r^2 = \frac{1}{4}$ for both roots.

  2. Choose the root

    Use the sign and size conditions.

  3. Substitute

    Use $\frac{a}{1-r}$.

  4. Simplify

    Divide by the fraction and tidy.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong denominator

    It is $1 - r$, not $1 + r$.

  • ✗
    Ignoring |r|

    Check $|r| < 1$ before using the formula.

Sum to infinity of a geometric series · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 8 · part (c) · 2 marks

A geometric series $G$ has common ratio $\frac{1}{4}$ and first term $320$. (c) Find the sum to infinity of $G$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams

Figures check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $|r| = \frac{1}{4} < 1$, so the sum to infinity exists.
  2. $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} = \frac{320}{1 - \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{320}{\frac{3}{4}}$
  3. $= 320 \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{1280}{3}$ $\left(= 426\frac{2}{3}\right)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$S_\infty = \frac{1280}{3}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a/(1 - r) with a = 320 and r = 1/4, and an accuracy mark for 1280/3 (or an equivalent exact value).

Calculation check

  • 320 ÷ (1-1 ÷ 4) = 426.6666666666667
  • 320 × 4 = 1280
