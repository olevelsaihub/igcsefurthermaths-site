Finding an unknown constant from a condition on the roots

Using a given fact about the roots (a relation between them, or the value of a symmetric expression or difference) to form and solve an equation for an unknown coefficient.

Type 5 2 The quadratic function FPM-QUA-09

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

The equation contains an unknown constant ($k$, $p$, $q$) and the question gives a fact about the roots, such as $\alpha-\beta=3$, $\alpha^4+\beta^4=466$ or $7\alpha\beta=3(\alpha+\beta)$, then asks you to find or show the value of the constant.

Finding an unknown constant from a condition on the roots

Calculation

Write the sum and product with the unknown in them, rewrite the condition on the roots using these, then solve and reject any value that breaks a stated condition.

Turn a condition on the roots into an equation

3 steps to find

Which step puts the condition into the unknown?

  1. Sum is −p
  2. Square the given value
  3. Both signs are valid

Sum is −p

Working
$\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}=-p$, with the sign kept.

Square the given value

Why
The identity works with $(\alpha-\beta)^2$, so square the $1$.

Both signs are valid

Check
$p=7$ gives roots $-3,-4$; $p=-7$ gives $3,4$; both differ by $1$.
0 of 3 found

2 steps to find

Which value does the condition $k<0$ remove?

  1. Two values from k^2
  2. Keep only the negative value

Two values from k^2

Working
$k=\pm 5$ both satisfy $k^2=25$.

Keep only the negative value

Why
$k=5$ breaks the stated condition $k<0$, so only $k=-5$ stays.

The calculation

$\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}$, $\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}$, $(\alpha-\beta)^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-4\alpha\beta$

The unknown may sit in b or c: substitute it, then solve.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Sign slip in $-\frac{b}{a}$ when $b$ contains the unknown
  • Squaring a condition wrongly, such as $\alpha-\beta=3$ becoming $(\alpha-\beta)^2=3$

Try it$x^2+px+12=0$ has roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with $\alpha-\beta=1$. Find $p$.

  1. $(\alpha-\beta)^2=p^2-4(12)=1$, so $p^2=49$
  2. $p=7$ or $p=-7$; both give roots differing by $1$

5

The method

  1. Sum and product

    Write $\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}$ and $\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}$ with the unknown.

  2. Rewrite the condition

    Turn the condition into sum and product, using $(\alpha-\beta)^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-4\alpha\beta$.

  3. Form the equation

    Substitute and simplify to one equation in the unknown.

  4. Solve

    Factorise, use the formula, or treat it as a quadratic in $k^2$.

  5. Reject and state

    Remove any value that breaks a condition, then give the final values.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Dropping a root

    A quadratic in $k^2$ gives $\pm$ values: give both unless one breaks a condition.

  • ✗
    Wrong sign of the sum

    With $b=k$, $-\frac{b}{a}=-k$, not $k$.

Finding an unknown constant from a condition on the roots · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (c) · 3 marks

The roots of the quadratic equation $2x^2+(6+2p)x+2p=0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$. In part (b) it was shown that $(\alpha-\beta)^2=9+2p+p^2$. Given that $\alpha-\beta=3$, find the possible values of $p$. (3 marks)

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\alpha+\beta=-\frac{6+2p}{2}=-(3+p)$ and $\alpha\beta=\frac{2p}{2}=p$
  2. $(\alpha-\beta)^2=(3+p)^2-4p=9+2p+p^2$ (part (b))
  3. $\alpha-\beta=3 \Rightarrow 9+2p+p^2=9$
  4. $p^2+2p=0 \Rightarrow p(p+2)=0$
  5. $p=0$ or $p=-2$
  6. Check $p=-2$: $2x^2+2x-4=0$ has roots $1$ and $-2$, difference $3$. Check $p=0$: $2x^2+6x=0$ has roots $0$ and $-3$, difference $3$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$p=0$ or $p=-2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for setting $9+2p+p^2=3^2$, a method mark for solving the quadratic in $p$, and an accuracy mark for both values $0$ and $-2$.

Calculation check

  • (3+(-2)) × × 2-4 × (-2) = 9
  • (3+0) × × 2-4 × 0 = 9
  • 1-(-2) = 3
