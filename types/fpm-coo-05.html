Knowledge card

Parallel and perpendicular lines: proving it or finding an unknown

Calculation
  • Parallel lines have equal gradients: $m_1 = m_2$.
  • Perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to $-1$: $m_1 m_2 = -1$, so $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$.
  • The gradient of $ax + by + c = 0$ is $-\frac{a}{b}$ (rearrange to $y = \ldots$ to see this).
  • A vertical line (gradient undefined) is perpendicular to a horizontal line (gradient $0$).
  • Setting a product of gradients containing a letter equal to $-1$ often gives a quadratic; a given condition (such as $p < 0$) picks one root.
  • A proof or show-that must end with a written conclusion.

Before you begin

  • Gradient from two points.
  • Rearranging to $y = mx + c$.
  • Solving linear and quadratic equations.
  • Reciprocals of fractions.
1

The method

  1. Find each gradient, from two points with $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ or by rearranging an equation to $y = mx + c$.

    Why: The condition is about gradients.

  2. For a proof, multiply the gradients (perpendicular) or compare them (parallel).

    Why: These are the two tests.

  3. For an unknown, write the condition as an equation, e.g. $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$, and solve.

    Why: The condition gives the equation for the unknown.

  4. Reject any root that breaks a stated condition and write a conclusion.

    Why: Only one value meets the question, and the conclusion earns the final mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Taking the reciprocal without changing the sign (perpendicular to $2$ written as $\frac{1}{2}$).
  • ✗
    Reading the gradient of $ax + by + c = 0$ as $a$ or $\frac{a}{b}$.
  • ✗
    Not writing a conclusion in a show-that or prove part.
  • ✗
    Keeping both roots of a quadratic when a sign condition rules one out.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Show that3Show both gradients and their product (or equality), then write a conclusion such as '$m_1 m_2 = -1$ so the lines are perpendicular'.
Prove1Set up the condition with the letter, solve fully, reject the root that fails the given condition and state the result.
Find3Show the gradients and the condition equation, then give the value.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Method marks for finding each gradient and for applying the parallel or perpendicular condition, then an accuracy mark for the value or for a complete conclusion. In longer prove parts, extra method marks are earned for forming and solving the quadratic and an accuracy mark for selecting the correct root.

Parallel and perpendicular lines: proving it or finding an unknown · November 2020 · Paper 1 · Question 5 · part (a) · 3 marks

The points $P$, $Q$, $R$ and $S$ have coordinates $(4, 7)$, $(3, 0)$, $(10, 1)$ and $(11, 8)$ respectively. (a) Show, by calculation, that the lines $PR$ and $QS$ are perpendicular.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Gradient of $PR = \frac{1 - 7}{10 - 4} = \frac{-6}{6} = -1$
  2. Gradient of $QS = \frac{8 - 0}{11 - 3} = \frac{8}{8} = 1$
  3. Product of gradients $= -1 \times 1 = -1$
  4. Since the product of the gradients is $-1$, $PR$ and $QS$ are perpendicular.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$m_{PR} \times m_{QS} = (-1)(1) = -1$, so $PR$ is perpendicular to $QS$.

How the marks are earned

A method mark for finding one gradient from the coordinates, a method mark for the second gradient together with multiplying them (or comparing with the negative reciprocal), and a final accuracy mark for $-1$ with a clear conclusion.

Calculation check

  • (1-7) ÷ (10-4) = -1
  • (8-0) ÷ (11-3) = 1
  • ((1-7) ÷ (10-4)) × ((8-0) ÷ (11-3)) = -1
