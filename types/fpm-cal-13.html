Knowledge card

Small changes and percentage changes

Calculation
  • For a small change $\delta x$: $\delta y \approx \frac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$.
  • An increase of $p\%$ in $x$ means $\delta x = \frac{p}{100}x$ (e.g. 3% gives $\delta x = 0.03x$).
  • Percentage change in $y$ $\approx \frac{\delta y}{y} \times 100$.
  • When $\delta x$ is given as a number, substitute it directly with the value of $x$.
  • If the change in $y$ is known, rearrange: $\delta x \approx \frac{\delta y}{dy/dx}$.

Before you begin

  • Product, quotient and chain rules.
  • Percentages as decimals.
  • Simplifying algebraic fractions.
1

The method

  1. Differentiate the quantity with respect to the variable that changes.

    Why: The derivative is the rate that links the two small changes.

  2. Write $\delta x$ from the information (a number, or a percentage of $x$).

    Why: Using '3' instead of $0.03x$ is the most common slip.

  3. Use $\delta y \approx \frac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x$.

    Why: This is the approximation the question tests.

  4. For a percentage change, divide by $y$ and multiply by 100, then simplify.

    Why: A percentage is relative to the original value.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Taking $\delta x = 3$ for a 3% increase instead of $0.03x$.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to divide by $y$ for a percentage change.
  • ✗
    Differentiating with respect to the wrong variable.
  • ✗
    Giving the answer as a fraction of 1 rather than a percentage, or not in the requested form.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

Small changes and percentage changes · November 2021 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (whole) · 6 marks

$y$ and $x$ vary so that $y = xe^{-2x}$. Given that the value of $x$ increases by 3%, use calculus to find, in terms of $x$, an estimate for the percentage change in $y$. Give your answer in the form $a(b - cx)$ where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{-2x} + x(-2e^{-2x}) = e^{-2x}(1 - 2x)$
  2. A 3% increase: $\delta x = 0.03x$.
  3. $\delta y \approx \frac{dy}{dx}\,\delta x = e^{-2x}(1 - 2x)(0.03x)$
  4. Percentage change $\approx \frac{\delta y}{y} \times 100 = \frac{0.03x\,e^{-2x}(1 - 2x)}{x e^{-2x}} \times 100$
  5. $= 3(1 - 2x)$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Percentage change in $y \approx 3(1 - 2x)\%$ (so $a = 3$, $b = 1$, $c = 2$)

How the marks are earned

A method mark for the product rule, accuracy for $\frac{dy}{dx}$, a method mark for $\delta x = 0.03x$ in $\delta y \approx \frac{dy}{dx}\delta x$, a method mark for dividing by $y$ and multiplying by 100, and accuracy for $3(1 - 2x)$. Check at $x = 0.3$: $3(1 - 0.6) = 1.2$.

Calculation check

  • 0.03 × (1-2 × 0.3) × 100 = 1.2
  • 3 × (1-2 × 0.3) = 1.2
