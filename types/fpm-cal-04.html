Finding the equation of a tangent

Calculation

The tangent at a point has the gradient $\frac{dy}{dx}$ found at that point. Write it as $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ using a point on the curve.

3 steps to find

Find y from the curve, then the gradient from $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

  1. Get y from the curve
  2. Gradient is one number
  3. Line through the point

Get y from the curve

Working
$2^3 - 4(2) + 2 = 2$
Trap
Putting $x = 2$ into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ gives $8$, not $y$.

Gradient is one number

Why
The tangent's gradient is the value at $x = 2$, not an expression.

Line through the point

Working
$y - 2 = 8(x - 2)$, so $y = 8x - 14$.

3 steps to find

Find the gradient at the point, then the line through it.

  1. Chain rule on e^(−x)
  2. Gradient value at x = 0
  3. Line through (0, 6)

Chain rule on e^(−x)

Working
$\frac{d}{dx}\left(6e^{-x}\right) = -6e^{-x}$
Trap
Dropping the minus sign in the exponent.

Gradient value at x = 0

Why
Substitute $x = 0$ into $\frac{dy}{dx}$ to get $m$.

Line through (0, 6)

Working
$y - 6 = -6(x - 0)$.

2

The calculation

$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, where $m = \frac{dy}{dx}$ at $(x_1, y_1)$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Using $\frac{dy}{dx}$ as an expression in $x$ instead of its value
  • Taking $y_1$ from $\frac{dy}{dx}$ instead of from the curve

Try itFind the tangent to $y = x^2 + 3x$ at $x = 1$.

  1. At $x = 1$: $y = 4$ and $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2(1) + 3 = 5$
  2. $y - 4 = 5(x - 1)$, so $y = 5x - 1$

5

The method

  1. Find the point

    Put $x$ into the curve to get $y$.

  2. Differentiate

    Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and substitute the $x$-value to get $m$.

  3. Write the line

    Use $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

  4. Tidy the form

    Rearrange into the form asked, with integer coefficients if needed.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Gradient not evaluated

    $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is an expression until you put in the $x$-value.

  • ✗
    y from the derivative

    Take $y_1$ from the curve, never from $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Finding the equation of a tangent · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 8 · part (b) · 5 marks

The curve $C_1$ has equation $y = 5e^{-2x} + 4$ and the curve $C_2$ has equation $y = e^{2x}$. The curves intersect at the point $A$ (in part (a), $A$ is found to be $\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln 5, 5\right)$). The tangent at $A$ to $C_1$ meets the $x$-axis at the point $B$. Show that the $x$ coordinate of $B$ is $\frac{1}{2}(5 + \ln 5)$.

Worked explanation

  1. Find $A$: $e^{2x} = 5e^{-2x} + 4$. Let $u = e^{2x}$: $u = \frac{5}{u} + 4 \Rightarrow u^2 - 4u - 5 = 0 \Rightarrow (u-5)(u+1) = 0$, so $u = 5$ ($u > 0$).
  2. So $e^{2x} = 5$, $x = \frac{1}{2}\ln 5$ and $y = 5$: $A\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln 5, 5\right)$.
  3. For $C_1$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = -10e^{-2x}$. At $A$, $e^{-2x} = \frac{1}{5}$, so $m = -10 \times \frac{1}{5} = -2$.
  4. Tangent: $y - 5 = -2\left(x - \frac{1}{2}\ln 5\right)$.
  5. At $B$, $y = 0$: $-5 = -2\left(x - \frac{1}{2}\ln 5\right) \Rightarrow x - \frac{1}{2}\ln 5 = \frac{5}{2}$.
  6. $x = \frac{5}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\ln 5 = \frac{1}{2}(5 + \ln 5)$, as required.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x_B = \frac{1}{2}(5 + \ln 5)$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for differentiating $5e^{-2x} + 4$, accuracy for the gradient $-2$ at $A$, a method mark for a straight line through $A$ with that gradient, a method mark for setting $y = 0$, and the final accuracy mark for reaching the printed form exactly.

Calculation check

  • -10 × (1 ÷ 5) = -2.0
  • 5 × (1 ÷ 5) + 4 = 5.0
  • 5 × × 2 - 4 × 5 - 5 = 0
  • 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5
