5

The method

  1. Find intercepts

    Put $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ to get two points.

  2. Check a third point

    Substitute an extra point to confirm the line.

  3. Plot

    Use the scale on the axes and plot carefully.

  4. Draw and label

    Join with a ruler across the grid and label it.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign of an intercept

    Keep the sign: $y = -2$ is below the $x$-axis.

  • ✗
    Short line

    Extend the line across the whole grid.

What a complete response includes

Usually one B mark for each correctly drawn line, judged on position within a small tolerance; there is no method mark, so a wrong intercept loses the mark for that line.

Drawing the lines for an inequality graph · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 5 · part (a) · 3 marks

On the grid, draw the lines with equations (i) $y=2x+5$, (ii) $4y=x-8$, (iii) $5y+3x=30$.

Figures check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. (i) $y=2x+5$: $x=0 \Rightarrow y=5$, giving $(0,5)$; $y=0 \Rightarrow 2x=-5 \Rightarrow x=-2.5$, giving $(-2.5,0)$.
  2. (ii) $4y=x-8$: $x=0 \Rightarrow 4y=-8 \Rightarrow y=-2$, giving $(0,-2)$; $y=0 \Rightarrow x=8$, giving $(8,0)$.
  3. (iii) $5y+3x=30$: $x=0 \Rightarrow y=6$, giving $(0,6)$; $y=0 \Rightarrow x=10$, giving $(10,0)$.
  4. Check an extra point on (i): $x=-4 \Rightarrow y=-3$, so $(-4,-3)$ is on the line; it also lies on (ii) since $4(-3)=-12=-4-8$.
  5. Plot each pair of points and draw a ruled line through them across the whole grid, labelling each line.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Three ruled lines: (i) through $(-2.5,0)$ and $(0,5)$; (ii) through $(0,-2)$ and $(8,0)$; (iii) through $(0,6)$ and $(10,0)$.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each line drawn in the correct position across the grid.

Calculation check

  • 2 × (-2.5)+5 = 0
  • (0-8) ÷ 4 = -2
  • 30 ÷ 5 = 6
  • 30 ÷ 3 = 10
  • 4 × (-3)-(-4-8) = 0
