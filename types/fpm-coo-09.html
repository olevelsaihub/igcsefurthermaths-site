Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics · 4PM1

Area of a triangle or quadrilateral from coordinates

Finding the area of a triangle, trapezium, parallelogram, kite or other quadrilateral whose vertices are known, using base and perpendicular height, half the product of perpendicular sides or diagonals, splitting into triangles, or the coordinate (shoelace) method.

Type 8 8 Coordinate geometry FPM-COO-09

★★★★★ · 16 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

'Find the area of triangle $ABM$', 'find the exact area of the quadrilateral $PQRS$', 'area of the trapezium or parallelogram', usually the last part of a coordinate question using points found earlier.

Area of a triangle or quadrilateral from coordinates

Calculation

Use a base and perpendicular height, or the shoelace method on the vertices in order. Give an exact area when it is asked for.

3 steps to find

Which side is the base, and what is the height?

  1. Horizontal base is easy
  2. Perpendicular height only
  3. State the units

Horizontal base is easy

Why
Its length is a difference of $x$-coordinates.

Perpendicular height only

Trap
A slanted side is not the height.

State the units

Scores
Give square units.
0 of 3 found

3 steps to find

What does each pair of vertices give?

  1. Multiply across each pair
  2. Keep the vertices in order
  3. Halve the total

Multiply across each pair

Working
$x_1 y_2 - x_2 y_1$ for each pair

Keep the vertices in order

Trap
Out of order, the shape crosses itself.

Halve the total

Scores
Area is $\frac{1}{2}$ of the absolute sum.
0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

Triangle: $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$; shoelace: $\frac{1}{2}\left|\sum (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\right|$

Perpendicular diagonals: area $= \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2$

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Using a slanted side as the height
  • Losing the $\frac{1}{2}$ in the formula

Try itFind the area of the triangle $A(0, 0)$, $B(6, 0)$ and $C(2, 5)$.

  1. Base $AB = 6$ (horizontal), height $5$: $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 5 = 15$
  2. Area $= 15$ square units

5

The method

  1. List the vertices

    Write them in order around the shape.

  2. Pick a method

    Use a right angle, a horizontal base, splitting into triangles, or shoelace.

  3. Find the lengths

    Use the distance formula or coordinate differences for base and height.

  4. Calculate

    Apply the 1/2 and give an exact area if asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Slanted height

    The height is perpendicular to the base, not a side.

  • ✗
    Out of order

    Vertices must go around the shape, or the shoelace sum is wrong.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for a valid area method with correct lengths or coordinates (for example a correct shoelace set-up or correct perpendicular base and height), sometimes a further method mark for finding the lengths, then an accuracy mark for the area.

Area of a triangle or quadrilateral from coordinates · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Question 6 · part (c) · 4 marks

The point $A$ has coordinates $(3, 0)$ and the point $B$ has coordinates $(2, 2)$. The line $L_1$ passes through $B$ and is perpendicular to $AB$. The line $L_2$ with equation $x - 7y - 3 = 0$ intersects $L_1$ at the point $C$. The midpoint of $AC$ is $M$. (c) Find the area of the triangle $ABM$.

Worked explanation

  1. Gradient of $AB = \frac{2 - 0}{2 - 3} = -2$, so $L_1$ has gradient $\frac{1}{2}$: $y - 2 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 2) \Rightarrow x - 2y + 2 = 0$.
  2. Solve with $x - 7y - 3 = 0$: subtracting, $5y + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -1$, $x = -4$, so $C(-4, -1)$.
  3. $M = \left(\frac{3 + (-4)}{2}, \frac{0 + (-1)}{2}\right) = \left(-\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}\right)$
  4. Shoelace with $A(3, 0)$, $B(2, 2)$, $M\left(-\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}\right)$: area $= \frac{1}{2}\left|3(2 - (-\frac{1}{2})) + 2(-\frac{1}{2} - 0) + (-\frac{1}{2})(0 - 2)\right|$
  5. $= \frac{1}{2}\left|7.5 - 1 + 1\right| = \frac{1}{2}(7.5) = 3.75$
  6. Check: triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $B$ with $AB = \sqrt{5}$ and $BC = \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$, so its area is $\frac{1}{2} \times \sqrt{5} \times 3\sqrt{5} = 7.5$. $BM$ is a median, so area $ABM = \frac{7.5}{2} = 3.75$ ✓
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Area of triangle $ABM = \frac{15}{4}$ ($= 3.75$) units$^2$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for a valid area method with the correct coordinates of $A$, $B$ and $M$ (such as the shoelace set-up, or base and perpendicular height), a further method mark for evaluating it correctly, and an accuracy mark for $\frac{15}{4}$. The marks for $C$ and $M$ themselves belong to part (b).

Calculation check

  • 0.5 × (3 × (2-(-0.5))+2 × (-0.5-0)+(-0.5) × (0-2)) = 3.75
  • round(0.5 × 5 × × 0.5 × 45 × × 0.5 ÷ 2,6) = 3.75
  • -4-2 × (-1)+2 = 0
  • -4-7 × (-1)-3 = 0
