Finding the unknown letters in a rational curve

Working backwards from given asymptotes and intercepts to find the unknown letters in y = (ax + b)/(cx + d).

Knowledge card

Finding the unknown letters in a rational curve

Calculation
  • If the vertical asymptote is $x = k$, the denominator is zero at $x = k$: for $x + r$ this means $r = -k$; for $cx - d$ it means $\frac{d}{c} = k$
  • If the horizontal asymptote is $y = m$, then (coefficient of $x$ on top) $\div$ (coefficient of $x$ below) $= m$
  • A given $y$-intercept $(0, n)$ means the curve equation holds with $x = 0$, $y = n$
  • A given $x$-intercept $(s, 0)$ means the numerator is zero at $x = s$
  • Conditions such as 'prime' or 'integer' are there to pick one answer when an equation like $4a = 5b$ has many solutions

Before you begin

  • Asymptotes and intercepts of a rational curve (FPM-GRA-04, FPM-GRA-05)
  • Solving linear equations
  • Prime numbers and integers
1

The method

  1. List what is given: each asymptote and each intercept.

    Why: Each feature gives one equation linking the constants.

  2. Use the vertical asymptote to fix the denominator.

    Why: The denominator must be zero at that $x$-value.

  3. Use the horizontal asymptote to link the $x$-coefficients.

    Why: For large $x$, $y$ tends to their ratio.

  4. Substitute an intercept: $x = 0$ for the $y$-intercept, or set the numerator to zero at the $x$-intercept.

    Why: This gives the remaining constants.

  5. Apply any integer or prime condition, then write out the final equation and check one feature.

    Why: The condition picks the unique answer; the check catches sign errors.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sign error from the vertical asymptote: $x = -4$ means the denominator is $x + 4$
  • ✗
    Using the $y$-intercept condition where the $x$-intercept is meant, or the other way round
  • ✗
    Ignoring the prime or integer condition and giving a non-unique answer
  • ✗
    Setting the whole fraction (not the numerator) equal to the intercept value incorrectly
  • ✗
    Not substituting the found constants back to give the final equation when asked

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from typical examiner credit patterns for this method; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for forming a correct equation from each given feature (asymptote or intercept) and accuracy marks for each constant. Write-down parts are single marks for each value.

Finding the unknown letters in a rational curve · November 2021 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (b) · 2 marks

A curve has equation $y = \frac{a - bx}{cx - d}$, $x \neq \frac{d}{c}$, where $a$, $c$ and $d$ are prime numbers and $b$ is an integer. The curve crosses the $x$-axis at the point $\left(\frac{5}{4}, 0\right)$. Find the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. On the $x$-axis $y = 0$, so the numerator is zero at $x = \frac{5}{4}$: $a - b\left(\frac{5}{4}\right) = 0$
  2. Multiply by 4: $4a - 5b = 0$, so $4a = 5b$
  3. $5$ divides $4a$ and has no factor in common with $4$, so $5$ divides $a$; since $a$ is prime, $a = 5$
  4. Then $4(5) = 5b$, so $b = 4$ (an integer, as required)
  5. Check: $5 - 4\left(\frac{5}{4}\right) = 5 - 5 = 0$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$a = 5$, $b = 4$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for using the $x$-intercept to set the numerator to zero, giving $4a = 5b$ (or equivalent), and an accuracy mark for both $a = 5$ and $b = 4$.

Calculation check

  • 5-4 × (5 ÷ 4) = 0.0
  • 4 × 5 = 20
  • 5 × 4 = 20
