Knowledge card

Angle between two planes using a right-angled triangle

Calculation

The angle between two planes is found from two perpendiculars to their common edge, drawn from the same point. For a face of a right pyramid, use the midpoint of the base edge.

Why

Trap

Why

The calculation

$\tan\theta = \frac{VO}{OM}$ and $\cos\theta = \frac{OM}{VM}$

5

The method

  1. Find the common edge

    Identify the line where the two planes meet.

  2. Take the midpoint

    Choose the midpoint M of that edge and draw a perpendicular to it in each plane.

  3. Find the lengths

    Use half the side for OM and Pythagoras for the slant height.

  4. Use trig

    Use $\tan\theta = \frac{VO}{OM}$ or $\cos\theta = \frac{OM}{VM}$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sloping edge

    A sloping edge is not perpendicular to the common edge.

  • ✗
    Corner instead of midpoint

    Join to the midpoint of the edge, not a corner, or the angle is wrong.

Angle between two planes using a right-angled triangle · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 9 · part (whole) · 6 marks

A right pyramid has vertex $V$ and base $ABCDE$, a regular pentagon with sides $2x$ cm. $VA = VB = VC = VD = VE = 3x$ cm. Find, in degrees to one decimal place, the angle between the plane $VBC$ and the base $ABCDE$.

Worked explanation

  1. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $BC$ and $O$ the centre of the pentagon. $VM \perp BC$ and $OM \perp BC$, so the angle required is $\angle VMO$.
  2. Slant height: $VM = \sqrt{(3x)^2 - x^2} = \sqrt{8}x = 2\sqrt{2}x$
  3. Angle $BOC = \frac{360^\circ}{5} = 72^\circ$, so $\angle BOM = 36^\circ$ and $OM = \frac{BM}{\tan 36^\circ} = \frac{x}{\tan 36^\circ} = 1.3764x$
  4. Triangle $VOM$ is right-angled at $O$: $\cos\angle VMO = \frac{OM}{VM} = \frac{1.3764x}{2.8284x} = 0.4866$
  5. $\angle VMO = 60.9^\circ$ (1 d.p.)
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$60.9^\circ$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for identifying angle VMO, method marks for the slant height VM and for OM from the pentagon, a method mark for the trig ratio, and accuracy marks for the lengths and the angle.
