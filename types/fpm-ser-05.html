Knowledge card

Adding up an arithmetic series between two limits

Calculation
  • $\sum_{r=p}^{q} f(r) = \sum_{r=1}^{q} f(r) - \sum_{r=1}^{p-1} f(r)$: take away the sum up to one less than the lower limit.
  • The number of terms from $r = p$ to $r = q$ is $q - p + 1$.
  • Alternatively use $\frac{N}{2}(\text{first} + \text{last})$ with first term $f(p)$, last term $f(q)$ and $N = q - p + 1$ terms.
  • A formula for $\sum_{r=1}^{n}$ found in an earlier part can be reused with different values of $n$.

Before you begin

  • Sum formula for an arithmetic series.
  • Sigma notation.
  • Careful counting of terms.
1

The method

  1. Find the sum up to the upper limit, $S_q$, using the formula (often from the previous part).

    Why: That counts every term from the start up to the last one you want.

  2. Find the sum up to one less than the lower limit, $S_{p-1}$.

    Why: These are the unwanted terms before $r = p$; the term $r = p$ itself must be kept.

  3. Subtract: $S_q - S_{p-1}$.

    Why: What is left is exactly the terms from p to q.

  4. Check with the first/last method: $N = q - p + 1$ terms, $\frac{N}{2}(f(p) + f(q))$.

    Why: Two methods agreeing is strong evidence the answer is right.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Subtracting $S_p$ instead of $S_{p-1}$, which removes a term that should be included.
  • ✗
    Counting the terms as $q - p$ instead of $q - p + 1$.
  • ✗
    Using the formula for $u_n$ instead of $S_n$ at the limits.
  • ✗
    Arithmetic slips when evaluating large sums.

What a complete response includes

A method mark for a correct subtraction of two sums (with the right lower value) or for correct first term, last term and number of terms; an accuracy mark for the value. Longer parts add marks for first finding the needed terms or the sum formula.

Adding up an arithmetic series between two limits · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 3 · part (b) · 2 marks

Given that $\sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r - 3) = \frac{n}{2}(5n - 1)$, (b) hence, or otherwise, evaluate $\sum_{r=31}^{60}(5r - 3)$

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\sum_{r=1}^{60}(5r - 3) = \frac{60}{2}(5 \times 60 - 1) = 30 \times 299 = 8970$
  2. $\sum_{r=1}^{30}(5r - 3) = \frac{30}{2}(5 \times 30 - 1) = 15 \times 149 = 2235$
  3. $\sum_{r=31}^{60}(5r - 3) = 8970 - 2235 = 6735$
  4. Check: first term $5(31) - 3 = 152$, last term $5(60) - 3 = 297$, $60 - 31 + 1 = 30$ terms, $\frac{30}{2}(152 + 297) = 15 \times 449 = 6735$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$6735$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for subtracting the sum to 30 from the sum to 60 (or for using 30 terms with first term 152 and last term 297) and an accuracy mark for 6735.

Calculation check

  • 30 × (5 × 60-1) = 8970
  • 15 × (5 × 30-1) = 2235
  • 30 × (5 × 60-1)-15 × (5 × 30-1) = 6735
  • 15 × (152+297) = 6735
