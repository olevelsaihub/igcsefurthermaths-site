Distance travelled by integrating velocity

Calculation

Distance counts every metre moved. Where $v = 0$ the direction can change, so split there, take the size of each piece, and add.

1

Check each step of the working

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 steps to find

Find where the direction can change, then add the sizes.

  1. Find the turning time
  2. Keep the sign, then take size
  3. Add sizes, not signed values

Find the turning time

Why
Direction can change only where $v = 0$, so split at $t = 3$.

Keep the sign, then take size

Trap
Keeping $-9$ gives a negative distance. Use $9$.

Add sizes, not signed values

Trap
Adding the signed values gives the displacement, $-\frac{20}{3}$, not the distance.
0 of 3 found

2 steps to find

Check whether $v$ ever reaches zero.

  1. Negative: no real roots
  2. One integral is enough

Negative: no real roots

Why
$b^2 - 4ac < 0$, so $v$ never reaches $0$.

One integral is enough

Working
With no turn, the distance is the integral, $\frac{14}{3}$ m.
0 of 2 found
2

The calculation

$\text{Distance} = \int |v|\,dt$ between the two times

Split at each time $v = 0$, take the size of each piece, then add.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Integrating straight across a turn: the parts cancel
  • Reporting a negative distance

Try itA particle has $v = 2t - 4$ for $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 3$. Find the distance travelled.

  1. $v = 0$ at $t = 2$, so split: $\int_0^2 (2t - 4)\,dt = -4$ and $\int_2^3 (2t - 4)\,dt = 1$
  2. Distance $= |-4| + 1 = 5$ m

5

The method

  1. Find where v = 0

    These are the only times the direction can change.

  2. Split at each turning time

    Integrate each part on its own.

  3. Take sizes

    Distance is never negative: take the size of each part.

  4. Add the parts

    The total is the distance travelled.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Integrating across a turn

    Parts in opposite directions cancel, so the total is too small.

  • ✗
    Using displacement

    A signed integral gives displacement, not distance.

Distance travelled by integrating velocity · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (b) · 4 marks

A particle $P$ moves in a straight line. At time $t$ seconds, the velocity, $v$ m/s, of $P$ is given by $v = t^2 - 4t + 7$. Find the distance, in m, that $P$ travels in the interval $0 \leqslant t \leqslant 6$.

Worked explanation

  1. Check for rest: $b^2 - 4ac = 16 - 28 = -12 < 0$, so $v = 0$ has no real roots and (as $v > 0$ at $t = 0$) $v > 0$ throughout: $P$ never changes direction.
  2. Distance $= \int_0^6 (t^2 - 4t + 7)\,dt = \left[\frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 7t\right]_0^6$
  3. $= (72 - 72 + 42) - 0 = 42$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Distance $= 42$ m

How the marks are earned

A method mark for showing (or noting) that $P$ does not change direction, a method mark for integrating $v$, a method mark for substituting the limits, and accuracy for 42 m.

Calculation check

  • (-4) × × 2 - 4 × 1 × 7 = -12
  • 6 × × 3 ÷ 3 - 2 × 6 × × 2 + 7 × 6 = 42.0
