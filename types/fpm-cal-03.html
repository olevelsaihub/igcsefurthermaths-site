Differentiating twice and proving a relation between y and its derivatives

Differentiating twice (usually with the product rule) and substituting y, dy/dx and d2y/dx2 into a given relation to show it holds or to find constants.

Type 17 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-03

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

You are given $y$ (usually an exponential times a sine or cosine, or an exponential times a polynomial) and asked to 'show that' an equation containing $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $y$ is true, or to find integers $A$ and $B$ that make it true.

Knowledge card

Differentiating twice and proving a relation between y and its derivatives

Calculation
  • $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ means differentiate $\frac{dy}{dx}$ again.
  • For $y = e^{ax}\sin bx$: $\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{ax}(a\sin bx + b\cos bx)$ by the product and chain rules.
  • Keep $e^{ax}$ as a common factor at every stage so the $\sin$ and $\cos$ terms can be collected.
  • To prove a relation such as $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = 0$, substitute all three expressions and collect the coefficients of $e^{ax}\sin bx$ and $e^{ax}\cos bx$.
  • If the relation has unknown constants, compare coefficients of $\sin$ and $\cos$ (or of each power of $x$) to find them.

Before you begin

  • Product rule and chain rule (FPM-CAL-01).
  • Derivatives of $e^{kx}$, $\sin kx$ and $\cos kx$.
  • Collecting like terms and solving simple simultaneous equations.
1

The method

  1. Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ with the product rule and chain rule, and factorise out the exponential.

    Why: A tidy first derivative makes the second derivative much easier.

  2. Differentiate again (product rule again) to get $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, then collect like terms.

    Why: The second derivative is needed for the relation.

  3. Substitute $y$, $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ into the left-hand side of the relation.

    Why: This is the step that earns the method mark for using the relation.

  4. Collect the coefficients of each type of term (for example $e^{3x}\sin 2x$ and $e^{3x}\cos 2x$) and show they give the required right-hand side, or solve for the constants.

    Why: Showing each coefficient is zero (or matches) is the proof.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    A slip in the first derivative, which then spoils every later line.
  • ✗
    Losing the chain-rule factor on the second differentiation, e.g. $\frac{d}{dx}\cos 2x = -\sin 2x$.
  • ✗
    Multiplying out and losing track of the common factor $e^{ax}$, so terms do not collect.
  • ✗
    In a 'show that', jumping to '= 0' without showing the coefficients cancelling.

What a complete response includes

Marks for a correct first derivative (method then accuracy), the same for the second derivative, a method mark for substituting into the relation, and a final accuracy mark for a fully shown simplification to the result or for the correct constants.

Differentiating twice and proving a relation between y and its derivatives · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Question 8 · part (whole) · 8 marks

Given that $y = e^{3x}\sin 2x$, show that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = 0$.

Question restated by olevels.com from the original paper; the original is below.

View full original question and diagrams
After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3e^{3x}\sin 2x + 2e^{3x}\cos 2x = e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x)$
  2. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 3e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x) + e^{3x}(6\cos 2x - 4\sin 2x)$
  3. $= e^{3x}(9\sin 2x + 6\cos 2x + 6\cos 2x - 4\sin 2x) = e^{3x}(5\sin 2x + 12\cos 2x)$
  4. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = e^{3x}(5\sin 2x + 12\cos 2x) - 6e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x) + 13e^{3x}\sin 2x$
  5. $= e^{3x}\left[(5 - 18 + 13)\sin 2x + (12 - 12)\cos 2x\right]$
  6. $= e^{3x}(0\sin 2x + 0\cos 2x) = 0$, as required.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = 0$ (shown)

How the marks are earned

Credit for a correct $\frac{dy}{dx}$ by the product rule, then for a correct $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, a method mark for substituting all three into the expression, and the final accuracy mark for collecting the $\sin 2x$ and $\cos 2x$ coefficients to show the result is exactly 0.

Calculation check

  • 5 - 18 + 13 = 0
  • 12 - 12 = 0
  • 3 × 3 - 4 = 5
  • 3 × 2 + 6 = 12
