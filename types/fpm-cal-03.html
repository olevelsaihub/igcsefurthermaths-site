Differentiating twice and proving a relation between y and its derivatives

Differentiating twice (usually with the product rule) and substituting y, dy/dx and d2y/dx2 into a given relation to show it holds or to find constants.

Type 17 9 Calculus FPM-CAL-03

★★★☆☆ · 8 of 44 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

You are given $y$ (usually an exponential times a sine or cosine, or an exponential times a polynomial) and asked to 'show that' an equation containing $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $y$ is true, or to find integers $A$ and $B$ that make it true.

The calculation

Try itGiven $y = e^{2x}\cos x$, show that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 4\frac{dy}{dx} + 5y = 0$.

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{2x}(2\cos x - \sin x)$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = e^{2x}(3\cos x - 4\sin x)$
  2. Substituting: $e^{2x}[(3 - 8 + 5)\cos x + (-4 + 4)\sin x] = 0$

Given $y = e^{-x}\sin 3x$, show that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 2\frac{dy}{dx} + 10y = 0$. (5 marks)

5

The method

  1. Differentiate once

    Use the product and chain rules; factor out $e^{-x}$.

  2. Differentiate again

    Apply the product rule to the bracket, keeping the $e^{-x}$ factor.

  3. Substitute

    Put $y$, $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ into the left-hand side.

  4. Collect coefficients

    Show the $\cos 3x$ and $\sin 3x$ coefficients each give $0$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Lost chain factor

    $\frac{d}{dx}\sin 3x = 3\cos 3x$, not $\cos 3x$.

  • ✗
    Jumping to zero

    Show the coefficients cancelling before you write $= 0$.

What a complete response includes

Marks for a correct first derivative (method then accuracy), the same for the second derivative, a method mark for substituting into the relation, and a final accuracy mark for a fully shown simplification to the result or for the correct constants.

Given that $y = e^{3x}\sin 2x$, show that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = 0$.

Worked explanation

  1. $\frac{dy}{dx} = 3e^{3x}\sin 2x + 2e^{3x}\cos 2x = e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x)$
  2. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 3e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x) + e^{3x}(6\cos 2x - 4\sin 2x)$
  3. $= e^{3x}(9\sin 2x + 6\cos 2x + 6\cos 2x - 4\sin 2x) = e^{3x}(5\sin 2x + 12\cos 2x)$
  4. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = e^{3x}(5\sin 2x + 12\cos 2x) - 6e^{3x}(3\sin 2x + 2\cos 2x) + 13e^{3x}\sin 2x$
  5. $= e^{3x}\left[(5 - 18 + 13)\sin 2x + (12 - 12)\cos 2x\right]$
  6. $= e^{3x}(0\sin 2x + 0\cos 2x) = 0$, as required.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} - 6\frac{dy}{dx} + 13y = 0$ (shown)

How the marks are earned

Credit for a correct $\frac{dy}{dx}$ by the product rule, then for a correct $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, a method mark for substituting all three into the expression, and the final accuracy mark for collecting the $\sin 2x$ and $\cos 2x$ coefficients to show the result is exactly 0.

Calculation check

  • 5 - 18 + 13 = 0
  • 12 - 12 = 0
  • 3 × 3 - 4 = 5
  • 3 × 2 + 6 = 12
