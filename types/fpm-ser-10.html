Finding r from sums of a geometric series

The calculation

$a + ar = a(1+r)$ and $ar + ar^2 = ar(1+r)$

Divide the second equation by the first to cancel $(1+r)$ and $a$.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Cancelling single terms, not whole factors
  • Keeping a root that breaks a condition

Try it$a(1+r) = 10$ and $ar(1+r) = 2$. Find $r$.

  1. $\frac{ar(1+r)}{a(1+r)} = \frac{2}{10}$
  2. $r = \frac{1}{5}$

The method

  1. Write two equations

    Each sum of two terms gives one equation.

  2. Factorise

    Take out $a$ or $ar$ with the $(1 + r)$.

  3. Divide

    The second equation by the first gives $r$.

  4. Find a

    Substitute $r$ into one equation.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cancelling terms

    Divide whole factors, not single terms.

  • ✗
    Unchecked answer

    Check both given sums.

What a complete response includes

Method marks for correct equations from the sums, for eliminating a and for solving the resulting equation in r; accuracy for r (with any invalid root rejected) and then for a. In a show-that the last accuracy mark needs the printed equation or value reached with no errors.

The sum of the first and second terms of a geometric series $G$ is $400$. The sum of the second and third terms of $G$ is $100$. (a) Show that the common ratio of $G$ is $\frac{1}{4}$.

Worked explanation

  1. $a + ar = 400 \Rightarrow a(1 + r) = 400$
  2. $ar + ar^2 = 100 \Rightarrow ar(1 + r) = 100$
  3. Divide the second by the first: $\frac{ar(1 + r)}{a(1 + r)} = \frac{100}{400}$
  4. $r = \frac{1}{4}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$r = \frac{1}{4}$, as required.

How the marks are earned

Method marks for forming each equation in a and r, a method mark for dividing (or substituting) to remove a, and an accuracy mark for r = 1/4 shown clearly.

Calculation check

  • 100 ÷ 400 = 0.25
  • 320 × (1+0.25) = 400
  • 320 × 0.25 × (1+0.25) = 100
