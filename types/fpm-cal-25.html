Differentiating displacement or velocity with respect to time and substituting a time.

Knowledge card

Velocity and acceleration by differentiating

Calculation

Each derivative with respect to $t$ moves one step along $s \to v \to a$, where $v = \frac{ds}{dt}$ and $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$. Substitute the time after differentiating.

1

Check each step of the working

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 steps to find

Differentiate once for $v$, twice for $a$.

  1. One derivative gives v
  2. Second derivative gives a
  3. Substitute last

One derivative gives v

Why
$v = \frac{ds}{dt}$ is the velocity.

Second derivative gives a

Trap
$\frac{dv}{dt}$ of $-18t$ is $-18$, not $-18t$.

Substitute last

Working
Substituting $t = 2$ into $v$ gives $0$: the particle is at rest, but $a = 6$.
0 of 3 found

2 steps to find

Check the units match each step.

  1. One derivative: per second
  2. Two derivatives: per s²

One derivative: per second

Working
Differentiating with respect to $t$ divides the unit by seconds.

Two derivatives: per s²

Scores
The unit of $a$ is $\text{m/s}^2$.
0 of 2 found
2

The calculation

$v = \frac{ds}{dt}, \quad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$

Substitute the time after differentiating, and give the unit with the answer.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Substituting before differentiating
  • Dropping a term: $\frac{d}{dt}(9t) = 9$, not $9t$

Try itA particle has $s = t^3 - 3t$ metres at time $t$ seconds. Find its acceleration when $t = 1$.

  1. $v = 3t^2 - 3$, so $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6t$
  2. When $t = 1$, $a = 6$ m/s$^2$

5

The method

  1. Know what you have

    Decide whether you have $s$, $v$ or $a$, and which you need.

  2. Differentiate with respect to t

    Each step moves one place: $s \to v \to a$.

  3. Substitute the time

    Put in the time after differentiating.

  4. Give units

    Metres, m/s or m/s$^2$ with the answer.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Substituting too early

    Substituting before differentiating gives a number, not $a$.

  • ✗
    Dropping a term

    $\frac{d}{dt}(9t) = 9$, and a constant term disappears.

Velocity and acceleration by differentiating · June 2019 · Paper 2 · Question 3 · part (a) · 2 marks

A particle $P$ moves in a straight line. At time $t$ seconds, the velocity, $v$ m/s, of $P$ is given by $v = t^2 - 4t + 7$. Find the acceleration of $P$, in m/s$^2$, when $t = 3$.

Worked explanation

  1. $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 2t - 4$
