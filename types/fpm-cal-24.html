Knowledge card

Volume of revolution about the y-axis

Calculation
  • Rotating about the $y$-axis between $y = c$ and $y = d$: $V = \pi\int_c^d x^2\,dy$.
  • Rearrange the curve to give $x^2$ in terms of $y$ (e.g. $y = \frac{x^2}{4} + 2 \Rightarrow x^2 = 4(y - 2)$).
  • The limits are $y$-values, not $x$-values.
  • Between two boundaries: $V = \pi\int_c^d (x_{\text{outer}}^2 - x_{\text{inner}}^2)\,dy$, where outer is further from the $y$-axis.
  • Split the integral if the boundary changes at some $y$-value.

Before you begin

  • Rearranging equations to make $x$ the subject.
  • Finding intersection points.
  • Integration of polynomials in $y$.
1

The method

  1. Rearrange each boundary to give $x^2$ (or $x$) in terms of $y$.

    Why: The integral is with respect to $y$.

  2. Find the $y$-limits of the region.

    Why: Using $x$-limits is the classic error.

  3. Integrate $x^2$ with respect to $y$, subtracting the inner from the outer if needed.

    Why: This gives the volume divided by $\pi$.

  4. Multiply by $\pi$ and simplify.

    Why: The answer is usually in terms of $\pi$.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using $x$-limits instead of $y$-limits.
  • ✗
    Integrating $y^2\,dx$ (the $x$-axis formula) by mistake.
  • ✗
    Squaring wrongly when rearranging, e.g. $x = 2y - 4 \Rightarrow x^2 = 4y^2 - 16$.
  • ✗
    Mixing up outer and inner boundaries.

Volume of revolution about the y-axis · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Question 4 · part (b) · 4 marks

The curve $S$ with equation $y = \frac{x^2}{4} + 2$, $x \geqslant 0$, and the line $l$ with equation $2y - x - 4 = 0$, $x \geqslant 0$, intersect at $A(0, 2)$ and $B$. The finite region bounded by $S$ and $l$ is rotated through $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis. Use algebraic integration to find the volume of the solid generated, giving your answer in terms of $\pi$.

Worked explanation

  1. $B$: $\frac{x^2}{4} + 2 = \frac{x + 4}{2} \Rightarrow x^2 + 8 = 2x + 8 \Rightarrow x(x - 2) = 0$, so $B(2, 3)$. The $y$-limits are 2 and 3.
  2. Curve: $x^2 = 4(y - 2)$. Line: $x = 2y - 4$, so $x^2 = (2y - 4)^2 = 4(y - 2)^2$.
  3. For $2 < y < 3$, $y - 2 < 1$ so $4(y - 2) > 4(y-2)^2$: the curve is the outer boundary.
  4. $V = \pi\int_2^3\left[4(y - 2) - 4(y - 2)^2\right]dy = \pi\left[2(y - 2)^2 - \frac{4}{3}(y - 2)^3\right]_2^3$
  5. $= \pi\left(2 - \frac{4}{3}\right) - 0 = \frac{2\pi}{3}$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$V = \frac{2\pi}{3}$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for writing $x^2$ in terms of $y$ for both boundaries, a method mark for the $y$-limits 2 and 3, a method mark for integrating outer minus inner with respect to $y$, and accuracy for $\frac{2\pi}{3}$.

  • (2+4) ÷ 2 = 3.0
