Solving log and exponential equations with your graph

Using log laws, or switching between log and exponential form, to turn an equation into 'plotted curve = straight line', then solving it from the graph.

Use e to undo ln

Why
$e^{\ln k} = k$, so applying $e$ to both sides removes the $\ln$.

Which step is not allowed?

  1. No split of ln(x + 1)
  2. Apply e to both sides
  3. Line is the other side

No split of ln(x + 1)

Trap
$\ln(x + 1)$ is not $\ln x + \ln 1$: a log of a sum cannot be split.

Apply e to both sides

Why
$e$ on both sides turns $\ln(x + 1)$ into $x + 1$.

Line is the other side

Why
The straight side is $x + 1$, so the line is $y = x + 1$.

The calculation

$\ln k = m \Leftrightarrow k = e^m$, and $\log_b k = m \Leftrightarrow k = b^m$

5

The method

  1. Match the curve

    Compare the equation with the plotted curve.

  2. Remove the log or power

    Apply $e$ to both sides for $\ln$, or use a log law.

  3. Rearrange

    Reach $f(x) =$ a straight line, with $f(x)$ exactly the plotted curve.

  4. Draw the line

    Two or three points across the interval, drawn with a ruler.

  5. Read the root

    Give the x-coordinate of the intersection to the accuracy asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Splitting a log of a sum

    $\ln(3 + x)$ cannot be split into $\ln 3 + \ln x$.

  • ✗
    Partial exponentiation

    Apply $e$ to the whole equation, not only to one part.

What a complete response includes

The early method marks are for the correct log or exponential manipulation and the rearrangement to the plotted function; then a mark for the correct line drawn and an accuracy mark for the root within an allowed range.

Solving log and exponential equations with your graph · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Question 5 · part (c) · 3 marks

The graph of $y = e^{3x - 2}$ has been drawn for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$. By drawing a suitable straight line on the grid, obtain an estimate, to one decimal place, of the root of the equation $3x = 2 + \ln(3 - x)$.

Worked explanation

  1. Subtract 2: $3x - 2 = \ln(3 - x)$
  2. Exponentiate both sides: $e^{3x - 2} = 3 - x$
  3. So the line to draw is $y = 3 - x$: points $(0, 3)$, $(0.5, 2.5)$, $(1, 2)$
  4. The line meets the curve near the right-hand end of the interval: at $x = 0.9$ the curve is $e^{0.7} = 2.01$ and the line is $2.1$ (curve below); at $x = 0.95$ the curve is $e^{0.85} = 2.34$ and the line is $2.05$ (curve above)
  5. The intersection is at $x \approx 0.91$, so the root is $x \approx 0.9$
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

$x \approx 0.9$

How the marks are earned

A method mark for turning the equation into $e^{3x - 2} = 3 - x$, a mark for drawing $y = 3 - x$ on the grid, and an accuracy mark for the root $0.9$ read from the intersection.
