Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics · 4PM1

Sketch and draw: what must be labelled

A 'sketch' is a quick graph showing the shape and key features, not exact plotting. A 'draw' needs accurate plotting on the grid provided.

Command words: Sketch, DrawUsually 2 to 4 marksAbout 1 minute per mark

What Pearson asks

A 'sketch' is a quick graph showing the shape and key features, not exact plotting. A 'draw' needs accurate plotting on the grid provided.

How the marks are earned

Marks are for the correct shape, for the key features placed correctly, and for the labels. For a draw question, accuracy marks need correctly plotted points and a smooth curve.

Method

  1. Find the key features first: asymptotes and intercepts.

    Why: These are what you must label.

  2. Mark axes, asymptotes (dashed) and intercepts with coordinates.

    Why: Unlabelled features lose marks.

  3. Decide which region each branch lies in.

    Why: A test point shows which side of an asymptote a branch is on.

  4. Draw smooth curves that approach asymptotes without crossing them.

    Why: Shape marks need a clean curve.

Answer frame

Labelled axes, dashed asymptotes with equations, intercepts with coordinates, smooth correct-shaped curve.

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from how 4PM1 command words are usually credited; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Not labelling the asymptotes or the intercepts.
  • Drawing straight line segments instead of smooth curves.
  • Letting a curve cross or curl away from its asymptote.
  • Putting a branch in the wrong region.

Worked illustration

January 2020 · Paper 2 · Q9(b)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (b).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Vertical asymptote: $x - 1 = 0$, so $x = 1$

Horizontal asymptote: $y = \frac{3}{1} = 3$

$y$-intercept: $x = 0$ gives $y = \frac{-2}{-1} = 2$, so $(0, 2)$

$x$-intercept: $3x - 2 = 0$, so $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$

Corners: the intercepts are left of $x = 1$ and below $y = 3$, so one branch is bottom-left; test $x = 2$: $y = \frac{4}{1} = 4 > 3$, so the other branch is top-right

Draw the bottom-left branch coming in just under $y = 3$ from the left, passing through $(0, 2)$ and $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$ and plunging down beside $x = 1$; draw the top-right branch coming down beside $x = 1$ and levelling out just above $y = 3$

Final sketch: two branches with dashed asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = 3$; the lower-left branch passes through $(0, 2)$ and $(\frac{2}{3}, 0)$.

Why it earns the marks

Marks are for the correct asymptotes, the correct intercepts with coordinates, and a correct shape in the right regions.

Question types that use this card

  • Drawing the lines for an inequality graph
  • Drawing the graph from your table
  • Sketching a rational curve with its asymptotes
  • Sketching curves and where they cross the axes
