Rectangular Cartesian coordinates · Type 1

Using a line's equation to find a missing coordinate or intercept

Working with a line whose equation (or gradient) is already known to find a missing coordinate, an axis crossing point, or to draw the line on a grid.

Finding the length between two points

Using the distance formula to find a length between two points, often leaving the answer as a simplified surd, and using those lengths to compare sides or form a ratio.

Midpoints and points that split a line in a ratio

Writing the equation of a line through two points

Parallel and perpendicular lines: proving it or finding an unknown

Using the rule that parallel lines have equal gradients and perpendicular gradients multiply to -1, either to prove a relationship or to find an unknown coordinate or gradient.

Area of a triangle or quadrilateral from coordinates

Finding the area of a triangle, trapezium, parallelogram, kite or other quadrilateral whose vertices are known, using base and perpendicular height, half the product of perpendicular sides or diagonals, splitting into triangles, or the coordinate (shoelace) method.
